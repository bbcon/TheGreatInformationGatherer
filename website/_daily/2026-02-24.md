**Trump's 10% global tariffs took effect overnight, down from the threatened 15% rate, as markets stabilized following Monday's AI-driven software selloff.** **Asian chipmakers rallied on "picks and shovels" positioning, while Fed speakers emphasized inflation vigilance ahead of tomorrow's Nvidia earnings.** **Japan's PM cast doubt on BOJ rate hikes, weakening the yen to 155.90 against the dollar.**

**Tariffs & Trade Policy**

The 10% global tariff levy is now in force with a 150-day timeline, requiring Congressional renewal for extension. Section 232 and 301 investigations are launching to rebuild the tariff framework following the Supreme Court's AIPA ruling, though the process could take 2-10 months.

- Effective US tariff rate on China fell to 24% from 31% post-ruling
- FedEx became the first company to sue over refund processes
- $170 billion in potential refunds could complicate Treasury issuance
- Sectoral investigations targeting batteries, plastics, and grid equipment ongoing

**Equities & Earnings**

The S&P 500 recovered from Monday's 1% decline as tech rebounded, though software remains under pressure from AI displacement concerns.

- Nasdaq gained 1.2% as Anthropic issued collaborative messaging to calm disruption fears
- AMD surged 9-10% on Meta's multi-billion dollar chip deal plus equity warrants for 160 million shares
- IBM fell 13% Monday (worst day since 2000) on Anthropic's Claude code threatening COBOL displacement
- Workday dropped 7% after hours on disappointing guidance, reinforcing AI margin pressure concerns
- Asian chipmakers (TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix) rallied as less vulnerable "infrastructure" plays

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

Fed speakers emphasized the dual mandate is rebalancing toward inflation control, pushing back on aggressive rate cut expectations.

- Waller called March cut a "coin flip" dependent on February payrolls data
- Goolsbee stressed "multiple cuts in 2026" require inflation progress, warned of "warning signs"
- 2-year Treasury yields rose ~2 basis points to 3.45% on hawkish messaging  
- 10-year yields held near 4.04% as bonds maintained safe haven bid
- Consensus shifting to two cuts in 2026, both potentially under incoming chair Waller

**Asia & Central Banks**

China reopened after Lunar New Year with a 1.5% CSI 300 rally despite global AI concerns, while Japan faced political pressure on monetary policy.

- China left LPR rates unchanged in first post-holiday policy signal
- PM Takayichi's dovish BOJ comments pushed USD/JPY to 155.90
- Market pricing of 50+ basis points BOJ hikes in 2026 appears excessive given political interference
- China imposed export bans on 20 Japanese defense companies in retaliation for Taiwan comments

**Commodities & FX**

Energy markets reflected geopolitical premium despite supply surplus, while currency moves tracked central bank divergence.

- Brent crude near $71 with $6/barrel Iran risk premium despite Goldman's 2.3 million bpd surplus forecast
- Gold held near record highs above $2,100 on tariff and AI uncertainty
- Bitcoin fell toward $60,000, on track for steepest monthly drop since 2022
- Dollar posted best week of 2026 versus euro, which weakened to 1.0780 on ECB succession concerns

**Risks & Catalysts**

Tomorrow's Nvidia earnings represent a critical test for AI infrastructure demand, with the street expecting 68% revenue growth to $65.6 billion.

- Software sector remains in "prove-it mode" requiring 2-3 quarters of AI-resilient results
- Private credit stress emerging with Blue Owl down 50% year-to-date on 20% software exposure
- Iran-US tensions escalating ahead of Thursday Geneva talks; Rubio briefing Congress today
- State of the Union tonight may address Supreme Court tariff ruling and affordability agenda

**Positioning & Flows**

Institutional positioning shows caution despite positive flows, with record-low mutual fund cash balances limiting dry powder for further risk-taking.

- Hedge funds maintaining positive flows but not adding leverage
- European banks outperforming on attractive 10x P/E valuations versus 20x S&P multiples
- "HALO trade" (heavy assets, low obsolescence) gaining traction: ASML +40% YTD, Corning +70%
- Equal-weight discretionary/staples ratio breaking down, challenging US consumer strength narrative
