DAILY BRIEF:
Oil collapsed from $120 to $90 as Trump signaled Iran war resolution "very soon," triggering broad risk asset recovery despite persistent supply disruptions. TSMC's 30% sales surge validated AI infrastructure demand amid geopolitical turmoil. European auto sector distress deepened with VW posting its worst margins since 2015.

**Oil & Geopolitics**

Oil prices experienced historic intraday volatility as Trump's conflicting signals on Iran war resolution whipsawed markets.

- Brent crude fell to $90 from $120 intraday high following Trump's "very soon" war-end comments
- WTI dropped below $90, down from similar peaks despite brief recovery above $100
- Shanghai crude declined 13% as Asian markets priced relief rally
- Physical supply disruption persists with Strait of Hormuz near-standstill - only one Greek tanker passing alongside Iranian vessels
- Gulf producers cutting ~6% of global oil supply (~1.8M barrels per day) due to storage constraints
- Middle East supply loss estimated at 8-9M barrels per day, roughly half of Persian Gulf exports

**Equities & Earnings**

Asian markets posted broad gains on de-escalation hopes, led by technology and semiconductor names.

- Korea surged 6%, Taiwan gained 3.5%, Nikkei rose 3-4% as risk appetite returned
- SOX index climbed 4%, Nasdaq advanced 1.5% on tech buying into close
- Samsung and SK Hynix both jumped over 10% on memory demand optimism
- TSMC reported 30% year-over-year sales growth in January-February, validating continued AI infrastructure investment
- CATL posted strong results with FY25 profit up 42%, driven by overseas sales (18% of revenue) and energy storage (9% of revenue)
- Airlines gained 3-5% on oil price relief, with Cathay Pacific up 5%

European auto sector distress intensified with dismal earnings results.

- VW operating margins fell to 2.8%, lowest since 2015 and matching Dieselgate-era weakness
- Porsche margins collapsed to 0.3% from 14.5% in the prior year, effectively unprofitable
- Memory chip costs surged 300%, pressuring EV margins across the sector

**Rates & Central Banks**

Bond markets reflected shifting inflation and policy expectations as oil volatility dominated headlines.

- US 10-year yields declined to 4.11% from 4.20% peak as immediate inflation fears eased
- Fed rate cut expectations diminished with markets pricing less than 50% probability per meeting
- ECB surprisingly pricing potential hikes despite inflation running below target pre-conflict
- Wage growth data showed 3.5% annualized pace over three months, suggesting core inflation closer to 3% than Fed's 2% target

Central banks face mounting stagflation risks as supply shocks compound existing inflation pressures.

- G7 nations discussing Strategic Petroleum Reserve release of 300-400M barrels, covering 45-50 days of current supply loss
- RBA Governor signaled potential rate hike next meeting citing inflation dangers
- PBoC set stronger yuan fix below 7.30, most supportive since January ahead of potential Xi-Trump meeting

**China & Emerging Markets**

China positioned as relative safe haven despite broader regional volatility.

- CSI 300 gained modest 0.8% while Hang Seng rose 1.6% and H-Tech advanced 2%
- Record southbound flows followed record selling Thursday, suggesting stabilization
- China CPI expected to turn positive, ending deflationary exports and adding global inflation pressure
- Estimates show 0.7% CPI if oil stays $90-100, rising to 1.0% if conflict prolongs oil above $100
- Trade data pending with exports estimated up 7.2%, imports up 7%, surplus $176B

**Commodities & Positioning**

Gold failed its traditional haven test amid crowded positioning and margin call pressures.

- Gold ETF outflows exceeded 30 tons last week with estimated 60 tons underwater at January highs
- Potential for 40 additional tons of liquidation if outflows approach 100 tons total
- Korea margin debt remained unchanged despite 8% market selloff, providing underlying support
- KORU 3x leverage product gained 800-1,000% over 12 months on extreme compounding effects

**Outlook & Risks**

Markets face persistent uncertainty despite tactical relief rally on Trump's comments.

- Physical oil flows remain disrupted regardless of rhetoric until Strait of Hormuz normalizes
- Iran shows no de-escalation signs under hardline new Supreme Leader
- Sanctions waiver speculation includes potential Russian oil additions of ~1M barrels per day
- European stagflation risk elevated given gas dependence and energy vulnerability
- Hedge fund deleveraging incomplete after major losses including Millennium's $1.5B hit
