US-Israeli strikes on Iran entered their third day with no clear end in sight, as markets grappled with the prospect of sustained Middle East conflict rather than the brief military action initially anticipated. Oil markets surged on supply disruption fears before pulling back from peaks, while equity weakness remained measured despite the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Central banks face mounting pressure as energy-driven inflation concerns clash with growth risks from the expanding regional conflict.

**Energy & Supply Disruption**

Oil prices jumped sharply before moderating from session highs, with markets weighing immediate supply risks against demand destruction fears.

- Brent crude rose 6-9% to around $78-79/barrel, having briefly touched $82 in early trading
- WTI gained 7-8% to approximately $71-72/barrel with circuit breakers triggered at the open
- Trading volumes spiked 1,100% above average as risk premiums expanded
- Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Citi estimate $18-20/barrel geopolitical premium with consensus targets of $80-90 near-term
- Strait of Hormuz traffic fell 80% despite no formal Iranian blockade, with 40+ VLCCs carrying 2 million barrels each now idling in the Persian Gulf
- European natural gas futures surged 20-50% following Qatar's LNG production halt after drone attacks
- Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Rastanura (550,000 bbl/day capacity) was struck and halted operations

**Market Response**

Equity markets declined broadly but avoided panic selling, with defensive rotations into energy and defense while travel and technology lagged.

- S&P 500 futures dropped 1.0-1.8% with technology underperforming
- Hang Seng fell 1.3% and broke below 5,000, now 10% off October highs in correction territory
- Defense contractors outperformed with BAE Systems up 6%, Rheinmetall +5%, and Lockheed Martin +5%
- European travel stocks plunged 6-12% as Gulf airspace closures stranded 300,000+ passengers
- Airlines faced dual pressure from fuel costs and operational disruptions, with United and Delta down 5-6%
- Gold gained 1.6-2% approaching $2,365 as haven demand remained steady but not extended
- VIX rose to 23, elevated but suggesting measured rather than panicked positioning

**Fixed Income & Currency**

Treasury yields initially fell before reversing higher as inflation concerns outweighed safe-haven demand, while the dollar strengthened across major pairs.

- 10-year Treasury yields rose to 394-396 basis points after gapping down to 392bp at the open
- 2-year yields climbed 8bp to 345bp as Fed rate cut expectations diminished
- Dollar strengthened 0.5-0.6% against major currencies, reclaiming safe-haven status
- Japanese yen weakened despite risk-off sentiment due to Japan's vulnerability to higher energy import costs
- Swiss franc was the only currency holding ground against the dollar

**Central Bank Implications**

Energy price spikes complicated central bank easing plans as inflation concerns reemerged alongside growth risks.

- Fed rate cut expectations moderated as crude oil near $80 revived inflation fears
- Bank of England March rate cut odds fell to 50% from higher levels
- Bank of Japan's Himino indicated no urgency to support the yen despite currency weakness
- OPEC+ responded by raising output 200,000 bpd above expected levels to calm markets

**Geopolitical Developments**

The conflict's scope and duration remained highly uncertain, with regime change objectives suggesting prolonged engagement beyond initial estimates.

- Trump indicated operations could last 4-5 weeks while claiming three Iranian succession candidates were killed in strikes
- Iran's national security chief Ali Larijani stated Iran "will not negotiate with the US," signaling no clear off-ramp
- Eleven countries have now been struck by Iranian missiles as Hezbollah opened a Lebanon front
- Gulf states including Qatar, UAE, Kuwait, and Bahrain suspended flight operations
- Major shipping lines including MSC suspended Middle East bookings as insurance coverage was withdrawn

**Key Risks**

Duration uncertainty poses the primary market risk, with energy infrastructure damage potentially irreversible unlike shipping delays. Private credit stress from recent weeks could amplify risk-off moves if volatility persists. The Trump-Xi summit scheduled for April faces complications from high oil prices and Iran tensions, though analysts expect it to proceed. Gulf state retaliation threats, particularly from Saudi Arabia, raise escalation risks beyond current Iran-focused operations.

Markets are pricing geopolitical risk premiums but remain skeptical of sustained disruption, evidenced by 2027 oil contracts still trading in the $60s despite front-end spikes.
