**TSMC's 37% January sales surge validated continued AI infrastructure demand amid escalating Middle East conflict. Oil prices surged toward $87/barrel as the Strait of Hormuz closure entered its seventh day, with Qatar warning crude could reach $150 if the Iran war continues. China's National People's Congress delivered stability over stimulus, signaling acceptance of slower growth despite market pressure.**

**Oil & Energy Crisis**

The Iran conflict drove Brent crude toward $87/barrel, marking the largest weekly gain since 2022 as the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed.

- Brent crude rose 3.5% to $87/barrel, WTI near $84 - up approximately 20% for the week
- Strait of Hormuz transits collapsed from 40 ships/day in January to ~1 tanker by March 3
- Qatar's energy minister warned oil could hit $150/barrel within weeks if war continues
- US gasoline prices reached 18-month highs, adding 30-40 basis points to March CPI estimates
- Qatar LNG shutdown affects 20% of global supply, with restart requiring 2 weeks post-conflict resolution
- European natural gas futures spiked 50% weekly as inventories draw down rapidly

**Markets & Risk Assets**

Asian equities suffered their worst week since March 2020 as the energy shock compounded growth concerns.

- MSCI Asia Pacific fell 7% weekly, with record outflows from Asian markets totaling $11 billion
- MSCI China dropped for the fifth consecutive week, worst streak since March 2023
- Taiwan saw record outflows amid semiconductor export control concerns
- VIX approached 25, reflecting sustained but not panic-level risk aversion
- Flight cancellations to Middle East hubs exceeded 27,000 as regional aviation collapsed

**Central Banks & Monetary Policy**

The energy-driven inflation shock complicated central bank policy paths, with rate cut expectations diminishing across multiple jurisdictions.

- Fed officials acknowledged pump price impacts on sentiment, though policy response remains uncertain pending conflict duration
- ECB markets now price 60-70% probability of rate hikes as next move, reversing from cuts
- Malaysia held rates unchanged, citing downside risks from Middle East conflict
- February payrolls expected at 30,000 (down from initial forecasts due to healthcare strike impact)
- Unemployment rate forecast at 4.4%, matching December levels
- 10-year Treasury yields rose 30 basis points weekly to 4.17% on inflation concerns

**China Policy Response**

China's National People's Congress delivered measured policy adjustments rather than crisis-level stimulus despite market pressure.

- GDP target set at 4.5-5%, down from approximately 5% previously
- Fiscal deficit maintained at 4% of GDP, preserving elevated post-COVID levels
- PBoC adviser emphasized structural adjustment over demand-side stimulus, ruling out "big bazooka" response
- Further monetary easing expected through rate cuts and reserve requirement reductions
- China ordered refiners to halt fuel exports amid energy security concerns

**Currency & Safe Haven Flows**

The dollar posted its strongest week since the November 2024 election as traditional safe havens failed to attract flows.

- Dollar rallied most sharply against energy-importing currencies (EUR, JPY, Asian FX)
- USD/CNY reached 6.91, with dollar strength capping gold gains
- Gold remained volatile and off session highs despite geopolitical tensions
- Yen and Swiss franc failed as haven assets given Japan and Europe's energy import dependence

**Technology & Export Controls**

Proposed US AI chip export restrictions threatened to disrupt global semiconductor supply chains.

- Draft rules would require government approval for all global AI chip sales
- New restrictions could affect 70% of Intel and Broadcom revenue streams
- Korean semiconductor stocks (SK Hynix, Samsung) fell 2-3% on regulatory uncertainty
- TSMC's 37% January sales surge demonstrated continued AI infrastructure demand despite geopolitical headwinds

**Risk Outlook**

Multiple scenarios could extend market volatility, with binary outcomes creating significant whipsaw potential.

- Strait of Hormuz closure duration remains the critical unknown, with mid-March the earliest reopening estimate
- Gulf State involvement could dramatically escalate conflict if UAE, Bahrain, or Saudi Arabia retaliate against Iranian attacks
- Iran regime succession remains unclear, with IRGC infighting and no designated successor
- US strategic petroleum reserve releases could provide temporary price relief but would signal policy panic

The combination of geopolitical energy shock and persistent inflation pressures suggests markets may face an extended period of stagflationary dynamics, particularly challenging for energy-importing economies in Asia and Europe.
