DAILY BRIEF:
Major Asian markets suffered sharp declines led by a 5.5% plunge in Korea's KOSPI as Broadcom's disappointing AI chip outlook triggered violent unwinding of leveraged semiconductor positions. Meanwhile, emerging market currencies continued deteriorating with Indonesia's rupiah hitting record lows at 18,000 per dollar. US payrolls data due today could provide critical direction for Fed policy expectations.

**Asian Equities & Tech Selloff**

Korean markets led global losses as Broadcom's weak AI guidance sparked broad semiconductor selling. 

- KOSPI fell 5.5% with Samsung down 4% and SK Hynix declining 6%
- Leveraged single-name ETFs amplified volatility, falling 15% and creating technical selling pressure
- Taiwan and Japan chip suppliers declined 3-7%, with TSMC suppliers and Tokyo Electron under pressure
- Nasdaq futures dropped 1% extending Thursday's selloff in US tech names

Hong Kong financials faced separate pressure from regulatory scrutiny over mainland client accounts.

- HSBC and Standard Chartered fell 1.5-4% on reports of crossborder account suspensions
- Insurers AIA and Prudential declined 4% on mainland client exposure concerns
- China A-shares outperformed the region, trading flat to slightly positive

**Emerging Market Currency Stress**

Asian currencies extended losses with intervention risks rising across the region.

- Indonesian rupiah touched 18,000 per dollar, marking fresh record lows
- Options markets pricing 40% probability of 19,000 by December as selling intensifies
- Korean won approached weakest levels since 2009, compounding equity losses
- Japanese yen remains weak at 160 despite intervention; BOJ rate hike expectations rising
- Chinese yuan held relatively stable at 6.77, outperforming regional peers

**Economic Data & Labor Markets**

Mixed signals emerged from regional economic indicators ahead of US payrolls.

- Philippines CPI came in at 6.8% for May, below 7.8% estimate but still elevated
- Japan wage data rose for the fourth consecutive month, supporting BOJ tightening expectations
- China property showed signs of recovery with 12 cities posting two consecutive months of price gains
- US May payrolls expected to show solid gains with unemployment steady at 4.3%

**Central Bank Policy Outlook**

Fed rate cut expectations continued diminishing as labor market resilience persists.

- Markets now pricing potential rate hike in early 2027, shifting from cut expectations weeks ago
- Fed's Daly called for patience and warned against premature forward guidance
- RBI decision expected today with hold at 5.25% likely despite inflation below 4% target
- Asian central banks facing pressure for earlier rate hikes due to currency weakness

**Corporate Developments**

Broadcom's guidance disappointment highlighted potential cracks in AI investment momentum.

- CEO Hock Tan noted organic growth trajectory ($50B revenue doubling 2024-26) makes M&A unattractive
- Company's disappointing AI chip sales forecast triggered broader semiconductor selling
- Tech layoffs approaching two-year highs despite continued AI spending surge
- Lululemon cut revenue guidance on weak North American demand, shares falling 12%

**Private Credit & Liquidity Concerns**

Signs of stress emerged in private credit markets as major funds restricted redemptions.

- Blackstone limited withdrawals from BCR, its largest private credit fund
- Partners Group implemented caps on redemptions amid liquidity pressures
- Regulators including Fed, SEC increasing scrutiny as credit cycle turns
- Investors demanding greater transparency on asset markings

**Geopolitical & Energy Markets**

Middle East tensions persisted despite earlier ceasefire optimism.

- Hezbollah rejected Lebanon truce proposal, contradicting Trump administration's "final stages" narrative
- Iran FM indicated no tangible progress in broader negotiations
- Brent crude held near $95 despite earlier selloff on ceasefire hopes
- Strategic petroleum reserves continue declining as negotiations extend

**Key Risk Factors**

- US payrolls at 1:30pm London could trigger significant bond and equity volatility given bearish positioning
- Korea's leveraged ETF dynamics creating technical selling pressure with limited natural buyers
- Private credit liquidity crisis potentially spreading as funds gate redemptions
- Asian FX intervention risks rising as currencies approach critical technical levels
- Iran negotiations timeline uncertainty keeping energy markets elevated

The combination of tech momentum stalling, emerging market stress intensifying, and US labor data pending creates a challenging environment for risk assets. Bond markets appear positioned for volatility in either direction depending on payrolls strength, while Asian central banks face increasing pressure to defend currencies through rate hikes.
