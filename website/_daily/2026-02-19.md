DAILY BRIEF:
TSMC's 37% January sales surge validated continued AI infrastructure demand. US military buildups in the Middle East drove oil prices to a two-month high above $71. Fed minutes revealed a hawkish tilt with several officials open to rate hikes if inflation persists.

**Economic Data & Labor Markets**

Initial jobless claims dropped sharply to 206K versus estimates of 225K, suggesting labor market stabilization despite broader recession concerns. However, deeper analysis reveals cooling dynamics as job switchers now earn just a 1.9% premium compared to 9% in 2022—the lowest on record according to ADP data.

- Trade deficit widened to $70.3B in December versus $55.5B expected
- Import surge of $12.3B driven by AI infrastructure and gold flows  
- Exports to Canada fell to lowest level since January 2022
- Philadelphia Fed business index improved to 16.3 from 12.6 prior
- Prices paid component declined to 38.6 from 46.9, signaling input cost relief

**Consumer & Retail**

Walmart's conservative FY26 earnings guidance of $2.75-2.85 versus consensus $2.97 highlighted consumer uncertainty despite strong Q4 performance. The retailer's CFO cited a "fluid macro backdrop" including tariff and labor market uncertainty.

- Q4 comparable sales rose 4.6%
- Stock declined 3% in pre-market trading (recovering from -4%)
- Trades at 44x PE versus Amazon's 21x multiple
- Consumer bifurcation deepening with middle-income wage growth at 1.6% versus 3.7% for upper-income cohorts

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

FOMC minutes revealed a notable hawkish shift, with "several participants" indicating support for two-sided rate language and potential upward adjustments if inflation remains elevated. This contrasts with market pricing that still implies 50bp of cuts in 2026.

- Two-year yields rose 2bp to 3.47%
- OIS markets appear disconnected from Fed rhetoric
- Policy transition uncertainty complicates near-term outlook

**Equities & Sector Rotation**

Markets consolidated recent gains with the S&P 500 down 0.3% and Nasdaq falling 0.5%. A significant rotation accelerated away from mega-cap technology toward value and cyclical sectors.

- Equal-weight S&P 500 gained 7% year-to-date versus 0.7% for cap-weighted index
- Russell 2000 advanced 7% with emerging markets up 11%
- Materials, energy, and industrials led sector performance
- Mag 7 stocks now trade at 26x PE versus Russell 2000's 25x (despite half having negative earnings)
- Defensive sectors reached record highs with utilities and staples extended

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Brent crude surged 5% over two days to $71.50, marking the largest gain since October on escalating Middle East tensions. Reports suggest US military preparations for potential Iran strikes as early as this weekend.

- Risk of Strait of Hormuz closure driving energy infrastructure concerns  
- Oil price strength contrasts with broader commodity weakness
- Energy sector constituents now 90%+ above 200-day moving averages

**Credit Markets**

Investment-grade technology issuance forecasts increased to $360B from $300B as hyperscalers tap debt markets for AI infrastructure funding. However, stress emerged in private credit markets.

- Blue Owl halted redemptions amid liquidity concerns
- UBS analysts flagged AI disruption risk to software/business services loans
- These sectors comprise 50%+ of BDC portfolios in the $2T private credit market
- Redemption risk concentrated in 20-25% of the market

**Key Risks Ahead**

Supreme Court tariff ruling expected Friday on IEEPA authority, with prospective-only relief likely but potential for political volatility ahead of Tuesday's State of the Union address. Iran military action represents the most immediate catalyst for energy markets and broader risk sentiment.

The consumer spending outlook remains uncertain given Walmart's cautious guidance and widening income inequality trends, while Fed policy direction hinges on incoming leadership transition approach relative to current hawkish rhetoric.
