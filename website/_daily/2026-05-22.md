DAILY BRIEF:
Markets extended their eighth consecutive weekly gain despite elevated oil prices and shifting central bank dynamics, as Fed Chair Worsh prepares for his swearing-in ceremony amid the highest 10-year yields for an incoming chair since 1987. Asian equities led gains on AI infrastructure momentum, with Korea up 90% year-to-date and Lenovo surging 14% to 26-year highs. Emerging market central banks across Asia shifted toward tightening as inflation broadens beyond energy sectors.

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

Fed Chair Kevin Worsh's swearing-in ceremony today marks a critical transition as the central bank faces the most challenging environment for a new chair in decades.

- 10-year Treasury yields holding at 4.55%, representing highest levels since Greenspan era
- Markets now pricing one Fed hike by year-end as easing bias evaporates
- 45% of US CPI components running at 5%+ annualized rates, signaling broad inflation pressures beyond energy
- FOMC divided with 9 of 12 voting members maintaining easing bias at May meeting, deferring decision to Worsh
- Expected communication changes include potential elimination of dot plots and reduced forward guidance

**Asian Markets & AI Infrastructure**

Asian equities rallied on dual catalysts of Middle East peace optimism and accelerating AI infrastructure buildout.

- Korea's KOSPI up 2%, extending 90% year-to-date gain with 17 of 21 weeks positive
- Lenovo surged 14% to 26-year highs as AI infrastructure revenue jumped 84% year-over-year to 38% of total
- Taiwan semiconductor names rallying on agentic AI theme, though TSMC's 40%+ index weight limiting fund additions
- Quantum computing stocks up 20% following Trump administration investment announcements
- Hyperscaler capex expectations doubled to $800 billion for 2026 from $375 billion previously

**Emerging Market Central Banks**

Central banks across emerging Asia pivoted toward tightening as inflation pressures intensify beyond commodity shocks.

- Philippines considering emergency rate hike with central bank warning of "behind the curve" risks
- Bank Indonesia delivered 50bp hike this week, signaling broader regional shift
- Philippine peso strengthening on emergency hike speculation
- Japan core inflation fell to 1.4%, a four-year low, complicating BOJ rate hike path
- USD/JPY holding near 159 with intervention risk elevated during Monday's holiday trading

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Oil markets remain elevated despite renewed peace negotiations as structural supply issues persist.

- Brent crude at $104.66, up 1.6% after reversing three-day decline
- Strait of Hormuz remains closed with 88 commercial vessels turned back
- Iran proposing permanent tolling system, rejected by US as "unacceptable"
- European Commission downside scenario assumes oil peaks at $180/barrel, halving EU growth
- US gasoline prices approaching $5/gallon nationally with potential $6+ spike if closure extends

**Global Equities**

Equity strength remained concentrated in AI and technology themes while broader participation lagged.

- S&P 500 within 1% of all-time highs, driven by just 10 stocks year-to-date
- Small caps quietly rallying with +35% one-year, +65% three-year returns
- European luxury outperformed as Richemont beat expectations with 17% Americas growth
- Ross Stores posted 17% same-store sales growth while Walmart and Kroger absorbed costs
- Turkey's Borsa Istanbul fell 6%, triggering circuit breakers on political instability

**Credit & Fixed Income**

Bond markets reflected divergent central bank policies and fiscal concerns across regions.

- Korean bonds worst performing globally as government scales back June issuance
- High yield credit up 7% one-year, 20% three-year despite rate backup
- UK gilt markets remain cautious despite Manchester Mayor Burnham's fiscal rule pledges
- Credit spreads tight despite elevated rates, signaling continued economic resilience

**Technology Sector Evolution**

AI research focus shifting from language models to physical applications requiring different infrastructure.

- World models for robotics and drug discovery timeline shortened to 1-3 years for B2B applications
- Biotech AI adoption accelerating with protein design models now industry standard
- Paris emerging as deep tech hub challenging Silicon Valley's dominance in applied AI research
- Current LLM infrastructure buildout may have limited relevance to next AI wave

**Risks & Outlook**

Multiple crosscurrents create complex risk environment as markets navigate policy transitions and geopolitical uncertainty. Fed credibility at stake with failure to remove easing bias at June meeting, while prolonged energy disruption could trigger demand destruction. Momentum concentration in AI names creates vulnerability to rotation if Middle East tensions resolve, similar to November 2020's vaccine announcement rotation pattern.
