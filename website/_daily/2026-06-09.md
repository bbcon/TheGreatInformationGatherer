**TSMC's January sales surge validated AI infrastructure demand as equities rebounded from Friday's volatility spike. China's export growth accelerated to 19% year-over-year, driven by semiconductor shipments, while oil fell below $90 on Middle East ceasefire optimism.**

**Equities & Earnings**

Asian markets led the global recovery with MSCI Asia Pacific gaining 2.4%, marking the first advance in three sessions. South Korea's KOSPI surged 8% as investors rotated back into AI names following Friday's sharp selloff.

- SK Hynix jumped 14% on semiconductor demand optimism
- SOX index climbed 5.6%, retracing half of Friday's decline  
- Applied Digital gained 10% after securing a 15-year AI hyperscaler lease
- S&P futures advanced 0.3% for a second consecutive session
- VIX compressed from 20 to 18, though remains elevated above normal ranges

**Economic Data**

China's trade figures exceeded expectations, underscoring robust demand for AI-related components and infrastructure.

- Exports grew 19% year-over-year versus 15% consensus, marking the fastest pace in three months
- Imports expanded 27% year-over-year against 26% estimates  
- Semiconductor and computer exports drove roughly half of total export growth
- Chip sales specifically surged 111% year-over-year
- South Korea imports from China jumped 200% on semiconductor component demand
- UK retail sales beat expectations, supporting GBP strength

Notably, China's oil imports fell 29% year-over-year to an 8-year low, suggesting structural shifts toward electrification and reduced commodity intensity.

**Rates & Fed Policy**

Treasury markets showed little movement ahead of Wednesday's CPI release, with the 2-year yield holding roughly 30 basis points above the Fed's policy rate ceiling despite recent pullbacks.

- 30-year yields remained stuck at 5.02-5.03%, reflecting persistent deficit and inflation concerns
- Markets now price at least one 25-basis-point hike in 2025, creating a disconnect with Fed policy narrative
- Wednesday's CPI consensus stands at 4.2% year-over-year, with potential upside risks given recent energy pressures

**Central Banks**

Bank Indonesia delivered a surprise 25-basis-point rate hike to 5.5% to defend the rupiah amid capital outflows and policy uncertainty. The Bank of Japan may halt its quarterly bond purchase reductions according to media reports, with potential rate increases delayed until October after a June pause.

**Commodities & FX**

Oil markets reversed sharply on Middle East ceasefire developments, though the timeline for resolution remains uncertain.

- WTI crude fell below $90 per barrel
- Brent declined 8% to $93 per barrel on Iran-Israel ceasefire pledge
- China's 29% decline in oil imports provided additional downward pressure
- The dollar weakened 0.2% for a second consecutive session as haven demand faded
- Japanese yen remained above 160 despite modest USD softness

**IPO & Capital Markets**

SpaceX's $75 billion IPO attracted over $10 billion in institutional orders, positioning it as potentially the largest public offering in history. The deal includes a 30% retail allocation and early NASDAQ inclusion, creating significant technical demand. OpenAI filed confidentially for its own public offering, while Anthropic remains in the pipeline, intensifying competition for capital in the AI space.

**Risk Assessment**

Bank of America warned that 70% of bear market indicators now flash red, citing extreme PE ratio spreads and lofty growth expectations vulnerable to disappointment. The firm suggests taking profits as AI momentum shows vulnerability.

- High PE/low PE spread at extreme levels
- VIX break above 20 on Friday signaled potential volatility regime shift
- Lebanon operations remain a sticking point in Middle East ceasefire talks, with Hezbollah excluded from Israel-Iran agreements

Wednesday's CPI print emerges as the key near-term catalyst, with analysts warning that a 4.2% reading would be "terrifying" given broadening inflation concerns and "greedflation" narratives. The disconnect between front-end Treasury pricing and Fed rhetoric suggests markets remain skeptical of the central bank's productivity-driven disinflation thesis.

Oil's sharp reversal below $90 may prove premature given ongoing geopolitical uncertainties, while the concentration of AI IPOs tests market liquidity for momentum trades. China's structural demand shift away from oil imports signals potential long-term implications for commodity pricing dynamics.
