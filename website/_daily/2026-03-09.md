Iran's appointment of hardliner Mujtaba Khamenei as Supreme Leader signals regime continuity, driving oil prices above $100 for the first time since 2022. Asian equity markets entered correction territory with regional benchmarks down 5% as the conflict enters its second week with no de-escalation in sight. The dollar surged to January highs on safe-haven flows while traditional hedges including gold and Treasuries failed to perform.

**Energy & Supply Disruption**

Oil markets experienced their largest single-day surge since the conflict began, with WTI climbing 30% to $118 and Brent advancing 20% to $113. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed except for Iran-linked and Chinese vessels.

- Major Gulf producers halted operations due to storage constraints: UAE, Kuwait, and Iraq production offline
- Shanghai crude futures gained 17% in sympathy with international benchmarks
- European gas futures rose 15% on supply concerns
- Oil futures curve displaying record backwardation, with time spreads at all-time highs signaling extreme market tightness
- Oil spending as percentage of global GDP reached 10-10.5%, approaching the 12% historical demand destruction threshold

**Equity Markets & Risk Assets**

Asian markets bore the brunt of selling pressure, with the regional MSCI benchmark breaking below its 100-day moving average. Korean markets triggered circuit breakers as the KOSPI fell 8%.

- Taiwan declined 5.5%, Japan dropped 4-5%
- S&P futures fell 1%, Nasdaq declined 1%, Russell 2000 down 1.7%
- Energy emerged as the only positive sector: CNOOC and PetroChina each gained 8%
- Airlines under pressure with Qantas and Cathay Pacific down 5-6% on jet fuel cost concerns
- Technology stocks declined 3-5% despite ongoing AI narrative, with Nvidia falling 2.6%
- Gold dropped 2%, failing in its traditional safe-haven role amid positioning unwinds

**Central Bank Policy & Rates**

The oil shock triggered a dramatic repricing of central bank expectations across major economies. Markets now price two ECB rate hikes compared to cuts expected weeks ago.

- Bank of England: Over 50% probability of rate increase, reversing from fully priced March cut
- RBA May hike fully priced; Canada shows 70% probability of December hike
- Philippines BSP indicated $100 oil breaches their "tolerance range," potentially requiring policy response with inflation at 4%
- Reports of potential emergency meetings at BOK and RBA
- Treasury yields rose 3-5bps across the curve; UK gilts gained 20bps, German bunds up 8-9bps

**Currency Markets**

The dollar strengthened to its highest level since January, benefiting from safe-haven flows and the US position as an energy exporter.

- Asian currencies fell 0.5% collectively, a rare magnitude decline
- Philippine peso and Indonesian rupiah hit record lows
- Chinese yuan fixed at 6.93, weakest since November 2024
- EUR/USD under pressure as Europe faces energy import dependency

**Economic Data**

Chinese inflation data showed mixed signals with CPI beating estimates at 1.3% year-over-year in February, while PPI deflation narrowed to -0.9% on commodity strength.

- US private payrolls declined 30,000, with the two-month average around 35,000 consistent with 2023 trends

**Geopolitical Developments**

Iran's leadership transition to Mujtaba Khamenei represents continuity rather than change, as the IRGC-backed appointee maintains his predecessor's hardline approach. Gulf states are shifting defensive strategies to lower-cost air defense tactics, suggesting expectations of sustained conflict.

- Reports suggest potential US special forces operation targeting enriched uranium stockpiles
- G7 emergency meeting scheduled to discuss coordinated Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases
- China maintains 1.4 billion barrel strategic reserves, providing 8-10 month buffer

**Outlook & Risk Factors**

The oil spending ratio approaching 12% of global GDP raises concerns about demand destruction, historically a recession trigger around the $133 per barrel equivalent level. Asia faces particular vulnerability as the region imports over 70% of LNG and oil from the Middle East.

Current account deficits are widening approximately 30 basis points per 10% oil price increase across energy-importing economies. The situation presents central banks with a challenging policy environment, as hiking rates into a supply shock risks triggering recession while doing little to address the underlying inflationary pressure from energy costs.

Markets appear to be transitioning from pricing a temporary supply shock to a more persistent stagflationary scenario, with growth concerns mounting alongside inflation fears.
