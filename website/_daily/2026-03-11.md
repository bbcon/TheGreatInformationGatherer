**ORACLE SURGED 11% ON AI INFRASTRUCTURE GUIDANCE WHILE OIL CLIMBED TOWARD $90 DESPITE IEA'S LARGEST-EVER RESERVE RELEASE PROPOSAL.** Markets fully priced out 2025 Fed cuts as the Iran-Hormuz conflict intensified, though February CPI data predates the March 1 war outbreak. **CENTRAL BANKS DIVERGED AS ECB HAWKS FLOATED RATE HIKES DUE TO ENERGY COSTS WHILE THE BOJ DELAYED TIGHTENING AMID GOVERNMENT RESISTANCE.**

**Energy & Geopolitics**

The IEA proposed releasing 300-400 million barrels from strategic reserves, marking the largest emergency release in history, yet crude continued climbing toward $90 as military strikes intensified around the Strait of Hormuz.

- WTI and Brent crude gained approximately $5, approaching $90 per barrel
- Proposed IEA release covers roughly one month of the 13 million barrel per day supply gap from Hormuz closure
- Strait normally handles ~20 million barrels daily, currently blocked by Iranian mines and anti-ship missiles
- Maritime conflict expanded with 16 Iranian vessels destroyed; narrow strait described as "shooting gallery" for anti-ship missiles
- Timeline for mine clearance operations remains unknown

**Federal Reserve & Monetary Policy**

Markets completely priced out Fed rate cuts for 2025 despite February inflation data that predates the energy shock and reflects pre-war conditions.

- February CPI expected flat month-over-month and year-over-year
- Data excludes March energy spike and incorporates government shutdown housing distortions
- Treasury yields rose slightly as traders eliminated 2025 easing expectations
- February payrolls declined 92,000, among the largest drops since the pandemic, though data quality questioned

**Equities & Earnings**

Oracle's blowout guidance drove sharp sector rotation as AI infrastructure demand validated elevated capex spending, while software names declined on Nvidia concerns.

- Oracle jumped 11% in premarket trading on light positioning into earnings
- Company raised FY revenue guidance to $90 billion versus consensus of $85-86 billion
- Infrastructure revenue surged 80% year-over-year
- Software sector fell 4-5% on concerns about Nvidia's potential agentic AI platform rollout at next week's GTC conference
- Oracle maintains highest capex-to-revenue ratio globally, raising financing questions despite management reassurances

**European Markets & ECB**

European equities declined 1% as ECB hawks suggested rate hikes could address energy-driven inflation, contrasting with underlying benign price pressures.

- ECB hawks floated possible rate increases due to energy costs
- German data shows underlying eurozone inflation remains "very benign"
- Euro weakened against the dollar on divergent central bank expectations
- Fiscal price caps may absorb oil shock impact instead of monetary tightening

**Central Bank Divergence**

Global central banks showed conflicting approaches to the energy shock and domestic conditions.

- Bank of Japan delayed expected rate hike despite favorable economic conditions due to government resistance
- Bank of England maintained easing bias as underlying UK inflation weakened despite electricity pricing distortions
- Markets appear to be overreacting to oil spike that hasn't yet appeared in actual inflation data

**Risk Assessment**

The disconnect between market pricing and fundamental conditions suggests potential opportunities as second-round effects remain unclear.

- Profit-led inflation appears more likely than wage-price spiral if oil remains elevated
- Retailers may expand margins using energy costs as justification
- IEA release buys time but doesn't address underlying supply disruption
- Nvidia's GTC conference next week represents critical catalyst for AI infrastructure narrative
- Current Fed pricing at zero cuts for 2025 may create asymmetric opportunities if energy shock proves temporary

**Market Positioning**

Trading patterns reflected defensive positioning around geopolitical uncertainty while selectively embracing AI infrastructure themes.

- Oracle's surge occurred on light institutional positioning ahead of earnings
- Infrastructure and security names (Cloudflare, Digital Ocean, Snowflake) outperformed on clearer ROI visibility
- Application software faced headwinds pending clarity on Microsoft Azure acceleration
- Bond markets showed signs of overreaction to headline energy developments versus underlying inflation dynamics
