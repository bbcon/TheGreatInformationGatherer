Global markets gained modestly as reports of a potential 45-day Iran ceasefire emerged, though oil remained elevated near recent highs. Trump extended the Hormuz deadline for a fourth time to Tuesday 8pm ET, providing temporary relief while maintaining supply disruption concerns. First-quarter inflation data due this week will reveal the initial impact of recent tariff implementations and oil price shocks on the US economy.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Oil prices retreated from session highs but held near recent peaks as ceasefire reports tempered supply concerns.

- WTI crude fell 0.5% to $111/barrel, Brent declined to $103
- US gasoline prices reached $4.12/gallon unleaded, diesel at $5.62/gallon
- Trump's fourth Hormuz deadline extension to Tuesday 8pm ET creates ongoing volatility risk
- Energy equities declined in pre-market trading: Exxon, Chevron, and Occidental each down 2%

**Equities & Risk Assets**

US equity futures posted modest gains on de-escalation hopes, with growth sectors outperforming.

- S&P 500 futures rose 0.3%, Nasdaq gained 0.5%, Russell 2000 up 0.5%
- Memory semiconductor stocks advanced on Mizuho upgrade citing "overblown concerns"
- Biotech sector highlight: Selena Therapeutics surged 30% on reports of Neurocrine acquisition talks valued at $2.5 billion
- SpaceX reportedly meeting with banks today to prepare IPO at $1 trillion valuation

**Fixed Income & Currency**

Treasury yields edged higher as risk appetite improved, while the dollar retreated from recent safe-haven gains.

- 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.36%, 2-year at 3.85%, 30-year at 4.92%
- Dollar index declined 0.2% as investors reduced defensive positioning
- Gold gained 0.5% to $4,698/ounce, maintaining safe-haven premium

**Economic Data & Central Bank Watch**

Critical inflation readings due this week will provide first glimpse of tariff and oil shock impacts.

- March CPI release scheduled for Friday, PCE data Thursday
- ISM Services index due Monday
- Fed policy path increasingly dependent on inflation trajectory given energy price surge
- No major central bank policy decisions scheduled

**Commodities & Agriculture**

Hedge funds established first significant wheat long position in four years amid supply concerns.

- Net long wheat contracts reached 8,500+, highest since 2022
- Positioning driven by fertilizer shortages and adverse weather conditions
- Agricultural markets broadly supported by geopolitical supply disruption risks

**Key Risks & Catalysts**

Multiple high-impact events loom this week that could drive significant market moves.

- Trump's repeated deadline extensions raise questions about negotiating credibility versus tactical flexibility
- Oil prices vulnerable to further spikes toward $150/barrel if Hormuz closure persists
- March inflation data carries potential for significant upside surprise given energy and tariff effects
- Defense spending package worth $200 billion under consideration for anti-missile and drone technology
- Civilian infrastructure targeting threats could escalate conflict beyond current scope

The market's cautious optimism reflects hope for diplomatic progress while acknowledging substantial downside risks remain. Energy price levels continue to pose stagflationary threats to the broader economy, with this week's inflation data providing crucial insight into the scale of the challenge facing policymakers. The repeated deadline extensions suggest ongoing behind-the-scenes negotiations, though credibility concerns grow with each postponement.

Defense contractors may benefit from proposed spending increases, while refiners face margin pressure from elevated crude costs amid potential demand destruction. The combination of geopolitical uncertainty, inflation concerns, and energy price volatility creates a challenging environment for risk asset positioning.
