DAILY BRIEF:
Iran's conflict with US and Israeli forces entered its sixth day with no signs of de-escalation, as a US submarine sank an Iranian warship in the first such incident since WWII. China set its lowest GDP growth target since 1991 at 4.5-5%, signaling pragmatic acceptance of structural economic challenges. Korea's equity market staged a dramatic 10% rebound after Wednesday's historic 19.5% crash, though broader risk-off sentiment persisted.

**Iran Conflict & Energy Markets**

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed despite no formal announcement, creating severe supply disruptions as oil storage tanks fill and refineries face potential production shutdowns.

- Brent crude rose 4% to $85/barrel, testing key resistance after five consecutive sessions of gains
- WTI crude gained 2.6% to $76.30, with Shanghai crude contracts up 40% week-over-week
- European natural gas spiked 8% intraday before paring gains to close up 5.2%
- Qatar declared force majeure on LNG exports, removing a top-3 global supplier from markets
- Over 23,000 flights cancelled across the Middle East (4.4 million seats affected)
- Iraq began curtailing crude output as storage capacity constraints bite

The US Senate cleared a pathway for continued strikes against Iran on largely party-line voting, with Iran launching fresh missiles and drones at US and Israeli targets following the warship incident.

**China Policy Pivot**

Beijing delivered its most dovish growth outlook in three decades while maintaining record fiscal support, reflecting acceptance of slower structural growth amid external pressures.

- GDP growth target lowered to 4.5-5% range from higher previous targets
- CPI inflation target maintained at 2%, implying 6.5-7.5% nominal GDP growth if achieved
- Fiscal deficit held at record 4% of GDP with ¥5.89 trillion central government deficit
- ¥4.4 trillion in local government special bonds allocated for infrastructure and debt resolution
- ¥1.3 trillion in ultra-long bonds for strategic initiatives, including ¥300 billion for state bank recapitalization
- Defense spending growth slowed to 7% despite regional tensions, suggesting fiscal constraints

The PBOC fixed the yuan at 6.88, 20 pips below market estimates, in an aggressive signal against currency weakness despite the growth slowdown.

**Equity Markets & Volatility**

Korea's Kospi delivered its largest single-day gain since 2008 after Wednesday's worst decline since the index's 1980 inception, highlighting extreme positioning and leverage in the market.

- Samsung and SK Hynix surged 12% as semiconductor names recovered from record losses
- Asian equities gained 3.5% on the regional benchmark, led by Taiwan (+4%) and Korea
- China's CSI 300 rose 0.9%, with robotics, AI, and biotech sectors outperforming on policy support
- US equity futures declined after Thursday's 1.5% Nasdaq rally, with Euro Stoxx 50 futures down 1%

Market analysts noted margin lending had reached maximum levels before the Korea crash, with single-stock futures and options products potentially larger than underlying markets, creating mechanical instability.

**Fixed Income & Central Banks**

Bond markets globally repriced higher inflation expectations as oil shock transmission threatened the disinflation narrative.

- US 10-year Treasury yields rose to 4.125%, pressured by strong domestic data and weak foreign bond auctions
- ECB rate hike probability for December moved to 40-50% from near zero before the conflict
- Markets removed near-term Fed rate cut expectations as energy-driven inflation concerns mounted
- European bond yields climbed as ECB's Rehn advocated a "steady hand" approach to the supply versus demand shock debate

Poland cut rates despite the inflationary environment, described as "brave" but potentially requiring convergence with ECB policy if the latter tightens.

**Currency & Commodity Markets**

Energy importers faced renewed pressure as oil supply disruptions intensified, while safe-haven flows favored the dollar over traditional alternatives.

- EUR/USD fell 1.5% over recent days as Europe's energy import dependence weighed
- EM currencies showed elevated volatility, with Korea and Japan hit hardest as major oil importers
- CEE carry trades in Poland and Hungary saw outflows despite high real yields
- Gold extended gains but underperformed as the dollar reasserted safe-haven status

**Outlook & Risks**

The combination of geopolitical escalation and China's growth downgrade creates a challenging backdrop for risk assets, particularly those exposed to energy costs or leverage. Private credit markets showed early stress signs as software company valuations declined and semi-liquid funds faced redemption pressure. Friday's US employment data will test whether labor market strength can justify higher-for-longer rate expectations amid mounting external pressures.
