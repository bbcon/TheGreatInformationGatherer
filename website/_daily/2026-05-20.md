Samsung union confirmed a general strike for tomorrow, threatening chip supply disruptions as markets await Nvidia earnings after the close.**

**US Fixed Income & Fed Repricing**

Bond markets aggressively sold duration as yields reached multi-year highs across the curve.

- 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.17%, triggering $15 billion in block trades at 3x normal volume
- Treasury volatility surged 20% over two days as positioning panic gripped fixed income markets
- Markets now fully price a Fed rate hike by January 2026, reversing previous easing expectations through March 2027
- Philadelphia Fed's unnamed official called policy "mildly restrictive" but Taylor rule analysis suggests Fed funds remain at neutral, not restrictive levels
- City/Barclays target 5.5% on 30-year yields as bond markets reject rate cut narrative
- Equity risk premium turned negative with S&P earnings yield below Treasury yields; fair value estimated at 5,700 vs current levels

**UK & European Data**

UK inflation continued its downward trajectory though central bankers warned of temporary relief.

- UK CPI fell to 2.8% in April from 3.3% in March, the lowest reading in over a year
- Bank of England expects reacceleration through year-end due to Iran war energy shock
- Markets price 57 basis points of BOE rate hikes versus Bloomberg Economics' one-hike base case
- Japan's 20-year JGB yield dropped 10 basis points after strong auction demand but remains near multi-decade highs
- USD/JPY held above 159 despite JGB yield relief as yen weakness persisted

**Asia-Pacific Markets**

Regional equities declined for the fourth consecutive session amid mounting macro headwinds.

- MSCI Asia Pacific fell 1.1% with Kospi dropping 1.7% on Samsung strike concerns
- Chinese stocks showed relative outperformance, with domestic chip stocks rallying for three days bucking regional tech weakness
- Nikkei declined for a fifth session despite earnings season support
- Retail options volume hit 2.8x normal levels, driving intraday volatility particularly in Korean markets
- Indonesia, India, and Philippines currencies faced pressure from yield surge and oil shock dynamics

**Commodities & Geopolitics**

Energy markets remained elevated despite ceasefire speculation as structural supply concerns persisted.

- Brent crude held above $110 despite Trump threats of renewed Iran strikes within 2-3 days if no deal emerges
- WTI July contracts fell 2% to $102.44 while December contracts remained at record levels
- Strait of Hormuz closure probability through June at 30%, the lowest since conflict began
- NATO considering intervention by early July with 800 vessels and 20,000 seafarers stranded
- Trump set Friday-Sunday window for deal or resumed strikes as Senate Republicans showed signs of defection

**Semiconductors & Corporate Developments**

The sector faced multiple crosscurrents ahead of key earnings and labor disruptions.

- Nvidia gained 1.5% pre-market ahead of after-hours earnings with Q1 revenue expected up 80% year-over-year to $79 billion
- Options pricing suggested 5-12% post-earnings move, representing over $100 billion in market cap swing
- Samsung union confirmed general strike tomorrow after management rejected bonus demands, threatening production at the world's largest memory chip supplier
- Intel rose 4% following JPMorgan conference presentations
- Fabrication plants, if taken offline, could require six months to restore full production capacity

**Options & Positioning Dynamics**

Market structure showed signs of frothy sentiment despite elevated yields.

- VIX remained around 18 despite bond volatility surge, with RBC noting "spot up, vol up" phenomenon driven by call buying rather than fear
- Historic call buying volumes created skew inversion signaling extreme optimism
- Positioning shifted from "wait and see" to aggressive upside chasing in what RBC described as "full-on FOMO right tail chase"
- Realized volatility higher on upside moves than downside, suggesting "crashup not meltup" pattern
- BofA survey showed majority semiconductor overweight positioning ahead of key earnings
