DAILY BRIEF:
Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted 10 million barrels per day of oil flows, creating what the IEA terms the worst supply disruption in history. Brent crude briefly topped $100 overnight before settling at $96-98, marking a 30% gain month-to-date despite a record 400 million barrel IEA reserve release. US equities declined modestly with the S&P 500 down 0.5% as markets weigh whether the 13-day conflict resolves quickly or extends into a prolonged stagflation scenario.

**Energy Markets**

Oil prices surged as the Strait of Hormuz operates at just 16% of normal capacity, choking off 7.5% of global supply.

- Brent crude: $96-98 (briefly touched $101 overnight, +30% month-to-date)
- WTI: $91-92, up 5-7% in the session
- December Brent futures repriced from $70s to ~$80 as duration expectations extend
- Goldman Sachs forecasts Q4 Brent at $76 for a 30-day disruption versus $93 for 60 days
- XLE gained 0.75%, significantly underperforming the 30% crude surge, suggesting stretched positioning

The 172 million barrel Strategic Petroleum Reserve release over four months has failed to suppress prices as it addresses stock levels rather than flow disruptions.

**Equities & Credit**

Equity markets showed measured declines despite the geopolitical shock, with small caps bearing the brunt of concerns.

- S&P 500: -0.5%, Nasdaq: -0.4%
- Russell 2000: -1.3% as smaller companies face heightened rate and growth concerns
- VIX remained subdued as markets attempt to "look through" the geopolitics
- Energy equities barely reacted to the oil surge, indicating positioning was already stretched pre-conflict

Credit markets maintained stability in public markets while private credit stress intensified.

- Investment grade and high yield spreads remained tight
- Record issuance this week included Amazon's jumbo deal
- Morgan Stanley and Cliffwater gated redemptions at 5%
- JPMorgan restricted lending to private credit funds and began marking down loans

**Rates & Central Banks**

The Federal Reserve faces policy paralysis as oil shocks create stagflation risks while labor markets show signs of weakening.

- 10-year Treasury: 4.22% (trading range 3.90-4.30% year-to-date)
- 2-year Treasury: 3.65%, approaching September highs
- Fed cuts have been priced out, with only one cut expected in 2025 versus 3+ previously
- ECB and Bank of England face emerging hike risks if oil sustains above $100

The market pricing reflects zero near-term cuts as policymakers grapple with the impossible dual mandate of addressing both inflation and growth concerns.

**Economic Data**

US economic data showed mixed signals amid the geopolitical backdrop.

- Jobless claims: 213,000 (versus 215,000 estimate)
- Continuing claims declined to 1.85 million from 1.87 million
- Housing starts: +7.2% (versus -4.5% estimate), though permits fell 5.4%
- Trade deficit narrowed to $54.5 billion as imports declined 0.8% while exports gained 5.5%
- Average US gas prices reached $3.60 per gallon, up 20% in under two weeks

**Geopolitical Developments**

The conflict has expanded beyond initial expectations with attacks spreading across the Gulf region.

- Iran's Supreme Leader expected to issue first statement since taking power post-assassination
- Tankers attacked off Iraq, Oman terminals hit
- UAE intercepted over 2,000 projectiles
- Dubai financial hub staff at Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Standard Chartered working from home
- Israel reportedly targeting Iranian nuclear facilities

The rhetoric has shifted from "days not weeks" to "weeks not months" as the conflict enters its 13th day.

**Commodity & Sector Impacts**

Secondary effects are emerging across global supply chains and agriculture.

- Fertilizer stocks gained 4-6% (Mosaic, CF Industries) as 40-50% of urea flows through Hormuz
- India requested urea supplies from China amid fertilizer crisis concerns
- Asian airlines canceled flights and mandated carpooling
- Agricultural commodity prices spiked ahead of planting season

**Market Outlook**

The bifurcated market response reflects uncertainty over conflict duration, with equities down modestly while oil surges. The critical variable remains whether this resolves in weeks or extends into months, determining whether stagflation risks materialize. Current positioning appears extremely light in technology stocks after months of underperformance, while energy positioning was already stretched before the conflict began.

Private credit markets face a potential liquidity crisis with $1.7 trillion at risk, representing 18% of insurance company bond holdings and creating systemic spillover concerns.
