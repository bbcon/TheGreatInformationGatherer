**Fed holds rates steady with hawkish pivot as oil surges past $110 on Iran conflict escalation**. **Micron delivered record results that failed to impress investors already pricing AI growth**. **Markets sold off broadly with 2-year yields hitting highest levels since August as rate cut expectations evaporated**.

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

The Fed held rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75% with an 11-1 vote, with Governor Myron dissenting for a cut. Powell's hawkish tone during the press conference drove the market selloff as he emphasized the Fed won't "look through" the energy shock lightly.

- Fed revised 2026 PCE inflation up to 2.7% from 2.4% in December
- Core PCE forecast raised to 2.7% from 2.5%
- 2026 GDP revised higher to 2.4% from 2.3% despite energy shock
- Longer-run neutral rate increased to 3.1% from 3.0%
- Powell acknowledged "nobody knows" the scope or duration of the Hormuz crisis
- February payrolls showed -92,000 (weather/strike-adjusted)
- Several Fed members discussed rate hikes as the possible next move

Powell confirmed he will stay as chair pro tem until successor Kevin Warsh is confirmed and the DOJ investigation concludes, breaking succession norms not seen since Marriner Eccles in 1948.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Crude oil prices surged to their highest levels in years as the Iran conflict threatens global supply chains.

- Brent crude hit $111/barrel, WTI reached $100
- Oil prices up over 80% from recent lows
- Gasoline prices increased 30% since conflict began
- Straits of Hormuz closure risk threatens 20% of global oil production
- Production disruptions could take weeks to months to restore

**Equities & Earnings**

Markets declined broadly despite strong corporate results, with the Fed's hawkish pivot overshadowing positive earnings.

- S&P 500 fell 1.4% in the broadest selloff of 2025
- Dow declined 1.6%, Nasdaq dropped 1.5%
- All 11 S&P sectors finished lower
- Consumer discretionary and staples led declines

Micron delivered blockbuster quarterly results but shares fell 1.6% after hours as investors questioned whether the bar had been set too high.

- Q2 revenue: $23.86B vs $19.74B estimate (tripled year-over-year)
- Q3 guidance: $33.5B vs $23.7B estimate
- Gross margin: 74.9% vs 69.1% estimate
- Stock up 390% over past 12 months created elevated expectations

**Fixed Income**

Treasury markets repriced hawkishly as the Fed's tone shift caught investors positioned for cuts.

- 2-year yield rose 11bp to 375bp, highest since August
- 10-year yield increased 2bp to 421bp
- Fed funds futures now price zero cuts for 2026
- First rate cut pushed to July 2027
- Market had previously priced single December 2025 cut
- Break-evens rising but long-term inflation expectations remain anchored

**Other Central Banks**

European central banks are diverging from Fed policy as they face different inflation dynamics.

- ECB decision expected overnight with markets pricing two quarter-point hikes for 2026
- Former Vice Chair Brainard warns current shock may differ from prior episodes given cumulative supply pressures from tariffs, labor, and now energy

**Commodities & FX**

Gold declined sharply as uncertainty premiums faded amid higher real rates.

- Gold fell 3.4% as real yields increased
- Treasury curve flattened as front-end repriced more aggressively
- Energy complex mixed despite oil surge as refined product margins varied

**Outlook & Risk Assessment**

The Fed faces an unprecedented challenge with oil prices doubling while maintaining price stability credibility after five years above the 2% target. Stagflation risks are building as consumers already stressed by housing and food costs now face sustained gasoline price increases.

Memory chip supply constraints are expected to persist through 2027-28, supporting pricing but creating cycle risks if demand shifts. Consumer discretionary spending pullbacks appear likely as 30% gasoline price increases hit household budgets.

The duration and scope of Middle East supply disruptions remain unknown, creating significant uncertainty for monetary policy. Powell's extended tenure through the DOJ investigation adds political complexity to an already challenging economic environment.
