DAILY BRIEF:
Iranian oil supply disruptions pushed Brent crude above $100 for the first time since 2022, with the Strait of Hormuz closure now entering its third week. Federal Reserve rate cut expectations collapsed as the energy shock reignited inflation concerns, driving Treasury yields higher despite the geopolitical crisis. Asian semiconductor producers face additional pressure from a critical helium supply shortage that threatens advanced chip production.

**Energy Markets**

Oil markets sustained above $100 despite coordinated strategic reserve releases, with supply disruption fears overriding policy intervention.

- Brent crude closed at $101-$102/barrel, up 65% year-to-date
- WTI reached $95/barrel as backwardation steepened, signaling immediate supply scarcity
- RBC Energy expects $128/barrel within 3-4 weeks based on Washington policy discussions
- IEA announced 400 million barrel release, but markets dismissed the intervention as insufficient
- Energy equities continued outperforming: Chevron +30% YTD, Occidental +42%, ConocoPhillips +28%

The Strait of Hormuz remains closed with Iran's hardline leadership showing no signs of compromise. Iranian mining operations have proven effective despite minimal hardware investment, while the US Navy cannot guarantee safe passage without major military escalation.

**Federal Reserve & Monetary Policy**

The Fed faces an increasingly complex policy dilemma as oil-driven inflation concerns override traditional safe-haven Treasury demand.

- 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.27% despite geopolitical uncertainty
- 2-year yields reached 7-8 month highs as rate cut expectations evaporated
- MOVE volatility index hit 9-month highs, reflecting unprecedented rate market uncertainty
- Zero Fed cuts now priced for 2025, with Goldman Sachs pushing first cut expectations to September 2025
- 2-year breakeven inflation expectations reached 1-year highs

Over 10 central banks meet next week facing the worst-case scenario of supply-driven inflation amid growth concerns. The ECB and Bank of England may prove more hawkish than the Fed given stickier underlying inflation dynamics.

**Asian Markets & Supply Chain Risks**

Asian markets declined broadly as multiple risk factors converged, though China showed relative resilience.

- Nikkei fell 1%, Kospi led regional declines on semiconductor concerns
- Hang Seng dropped 0.7%, but Chinese markets outperformed with CSI 300 down only 0.4%
- USD/JPY reached 159.34, approaching intervention territory ahead of next week's BOJ meeting
- Shanghai crude futures surged 5%, reflecting regional supply premium

A critical helium shortage emerged from Qatar's Ras Laffan facility closure, which supplies 33% of global helium production. Asian chipmakers have approximately 3 months of helium stockpiles before production constraints hit advanced semiconductor manufacturing (5nm and below nodes).

- TSMC fell 1.5%, SK Hynix declined 2%, Samsung dropped 2% on helium supply concerns
- No substitute exists for helium in advanced chip production processes
- Memory producers face particular vulnerability given their reliance on cutting-edge manufacturing

**Economic Data**

UK economic momentum stalled as January GDP came in flat versus 0.2% consensus expectations.

- UK GDP registered 0.0% monthly growth, with services stagnating
- Manufacturing and construction sectors barely positive
- Philippine electricity prices expected to rise 16% in April due to energy pass-through
- South Korea implemented fuel price caps for the first time since 1997

**Corporate Developments**

Technology earnings showed mixed results amid broader AI disruption concerns.

- Adobe fell 8% despite beating earnings expectations, with CEO departure adding uncertainty
- Chinese tech names found support from OpenClaw AI adoption, with Tencent and Alibaba showing relative strength
- Software sector faced continued pressure on AI displacement concerns

**Geopolitical Developments**

Ukraine faces diplomatic challenges maintaining European support as Middle East conflicts divert attention and resources.

- Air defense supplies may redirect from Ukraine to Middle East operations
- Sustained higher oil prices could materially improve Russia's fiscal position, reducing settlement pressure
- European sanctions enforcement may weaken due to energy security concerns
- Pakistan launched strikes against Kabul, opening a potential new regional front

**Outlook**

Markets appear increasingly complacent despite mounting risks, with the S&P 500 only 4.4% off record highs despite the oil shock. Principal Asset Management warns of vulnerability in coming weeks as downside scenarios worsen while equity positioning remains elevated. PCE inflation data due today could cement the no-cut Fed scenario if it runs hot, likely extending Treasury market volatility.
