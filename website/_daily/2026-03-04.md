DAILY BRIEF:
Oil shock from Iran conflict drove unprecedented volatility in Asian markets, with Korea's KOSPI suffering its worst single-day decline in history at -12%. TSMC's 37% January sales surge validated AI infrastructure demand despite broader tech sector pressure. Fed officials remain divided on rate cuts as oil prices surge to $83 Brent amid supply disruptions.

**Geopolitical Developments & Oil Markets**

Iran conflict escalation triggered major supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz, driving sharp moves across energy markets.

- Brent crude surged to $85 intraday before settling around $80-83, up 40% from recent lows
- WTI crude reached $76, with forward curves remaining relatively flat suggesting markets view shock as temporary
- Goldman Sachs raised 2026 oil forecast by $10 per barrel
- European natural gas futures jumped 70% week-to-date as LNG tankers blocked from Qatar/UAE exports
- Iraq cut production due to full storage capacity; Saudi Aramco rerouting 7M bbl/day via East-West pipeline
- Trump's naval escort plan for Hormuz transit faces implementation challenges, with RBC calling it a "concept of a plan"
- Iran fired 600+ ballistic missiles at UAE (mostly intercepted) and continues attacks despite US claims of "complete control"

**Asian Market Carnage**

Asian equities entered technical correction territory with broad-based selling across the region.

- Korea's KOSPI plunged 12% in single session, triggering circuit breakers and marking worst day in history
- Samsung and SK Hynix fell 15% each, leading semiconductor selloff
- Thailand dropped 8% before trading halt, Hang Seng declined 2.6%
- MSCI Asia-Pacific fell 4%, worst day since April
- Foreign outflows reached $3B from Korea, $3.6B from Taiwan this week
- Korean won breached 1,500 per dollar, hitting GFC levels despite KOSPI's 45% YTD gains before correction

**Fed Policy & Central Banks**

Federal Reserve officials showed increasing division on rate cut timing as geopolitical risks complicate monetary policy.

- Fed's Kashkari suggested oil shock "could take rate cut off table" for 2025, shifting hawkish
- Minneapolis Fed's Marin maintains dovish stance, still targeting 100bp cuts to neutral rate around 2.75%
- Current Fed pricing shows two cuts baseline if rapid de-escalation occurs
- Indonesia and India central banks intervened overnight to defend currencies against oil-driven inflation pressures
- 10-year Treasury yields rose to 4.07%, up 15-20bp since conflict began

**US Equity Markets & Positioning**

Domestic markets showed resilience despite global turmoil, masking significant internal rotation.

- S&P 500 traded within 2% of all-time highs despite average member drawdown of -12% YTD
- Tech sector down 5-6% YTD while five sectors gained double-digits (industrials, materials, staples leading)
- Investment grade credit spreads widened from generational low of 71bp to 84bp
- Private credit facing withdrawal pressures with Blackstone raising $150M from partners to cover BCRED redemptions
- Moderna gained 5% premarket on $950M litigation settlement removing patent overhang

**Key Economic Data**

Several data points provided mixed signals on US economic momentum.

- ADP payrolls showed 63K additions (vs 50K estimate), strongest since July with services jobs dominating (+47K)
- January ADP revised sharply lower to 22K from initial 172K reading
- China Manufacturing PMI declined to 49.0 from 49.3, second consecutive month of contraction
- Caixin PMI surprised higher at 52.1 vs 50.2 estimate, showing divergence between official and private surveys
- Gasoline prices jumped 20 cents in two days, biggest increase in 21 years

**Currency & Commodity Moves**

Dollar strength accelerated as safe haven flows overwhelmed debasement concerns.

- Dollar posted strongest two-day rally in nearly a year against major currencies
- Swiss franc outperformed yen as preferred safe haven, given Japan's oil import vulnerability
- Yuan weakened to 693 per dollar as PBOC appears comfortable with current depreciation pace
- Tanker rates surged from $200K to $500K for China routes since Friday

**Corporate Developments**

Limited corporate news amid macro focus, though crypto assets surged on policy expectations.

- Bitcoin jumped 7.5% above $73K following reports of Trump-Coinbase CEO meeting
- Ross Stores gained 8% on Q4 comparable sales beating expectations at +9% vs +5% estimate
- Brown-Forman fell 7% despite earnings beat as CEO cited "stubborn weakness" in US spirits market

**Risk Assessment**

Market positioning amplified moves as traders were caught long Asia equities, long EM carry trades, and short USD. Critical timeframe appears to be 3-5 weeks - if conflict extends beyond this window, markets may need to reprice from temporary inflation shock to sustained growth impact. Fed rate cut expectations remain intact if de-escalation occurs, but policy divergence is emerging among officials.
