DAILY BRIEF:
TSMC's 37% January sales surge validated continued AI infrastructure demand despite broader market skepticism. Asian markets hit records while US tech concentration unwound, with semiconductors falling 3.5% on Nvidia's blockbuster earnings. Chinese yuan strengthened to 35-month highs as the PBOC signaled intervention concerns near 6.8.

**AI & Semiconductors**

Nvidia delivered exceptional Q4 results but markets responded with indifference, signaling potential exhaustion in the AI trade.

- Revenue surged 73% year-over-year to $68B, beating estimates by $2B
- Q1 guidance of $78B exceeded consensus of $72B but fell short of whisper numbers
- Gross margins held steady at 75%, up from 50% in prior years
- Stock traded flat to down 5% depending on session, worst performance since April despite the beat
- Supply commitments jumped from $50B to $90B, indicating potential memory shortages ahead

Memory supply constraints emerged as a critical bottleneck across the tech ecosystem.

- DRAM memory costs surged 100% quarter-over-quarter according to HP
- Global smartphone market forecast to decline 13% due to memory shortages
- Samsung and SK Hynix reportedly have memory booked through 2027-28, supporting Korean market outperformance

**Regional Market Divergence**

Asian markets significantly outperformed while US tech concentration continued unwinding.

- South Korea's KOSPI hit records, up 50% year-to-date and 20% month-to-date
- Korean benchmark closed lower only 6 times year-to-date
- MSCI World ex-US outperformed S&P 500 by 11 percentage points over four months
- European equities outperformed for the fifth consecutive month
- Shanghai Composite approached 4,165, nearing 10-year highs

US markets showed clear sector rotation away from mega-cap technology.

- SOX semiconductor index fell 3.5% despite strong Nvidia results
- Russell 2000 gained 0.5% as small caps benefited from domestic cyclical rotation
- Materials, industrials, and energy sectors outperformed
- MAG7 now trades at a 20% premium to the S&P 500, down from a 40% average and the smallest PE premium in seven years

**Currency & China Developments**

The Chinese yuan reached its strongest level since May 2023 despite dovish PBOC signals.

- Yuan strengthened to 6.85 against the dollar
- PBOC set daily fix at 6.92, a record 610 basis points weaker than market estimates
- The wide divergence suggests PBOC intervention threshold around 6.8
- Hong Kong reported its first budget surplus in multiple years, driven by equity market stamp duty revenues

China's economic data showed mixed signals for consumer spending.

- Lunar New Year tourism spending reached 800 billion yuan, up 18% year-over-year
- Per-trip spending remained below 2024 highs
- Box office revenues posted the weakest performance since 2019

**Central Bank Positioning**

Fed officials increasingly coalesced around an extended pause in rate cuts.

- US jobless claims fell to 212K versus 216K expected, the lowest January turnover in nine years
- Continuing claims declined to 1.83M from 1.86M
- Core PCE hit 3% after bottoming in April 2024, well above the Fed's 2% target
- Market pricing shifted to expect only 1-2 cuts in the second half of 2025

Asian central banks maintained steady positions.

- Bank of Korea held rates at 2.5% unanimously, signaling no policy changes for six months
- BOJ Governor Ueda continued examining the impact of December's rate hike
- Japanese yen strengthened to 155.92 against the dollar

**Geopolitical & Risk Factors**

Iran tensions remained elevated ahead of US-Iran talks in Geneva.

- Substantial US military buildup described as the largest since 2003
- New sanctions imposed on 30+ entities
- Embassies evacuated non-essential staff
- Trump administration deadline approaching within 10-15 days

Corporate earnings highlighted sustainability concerns around AI spending.

- Hyperscaler capex guidance reached approximately $700B for 2025
- Investment grade credit issuance expected at $400B, up 20% year-over-year
- Private credit stress emerged with forced selling of liquid assets pressuring public markets
- Cost-per-token declining faster than usage growth creates margin compression risk for AI labs

**Outlook**

The market appears to be pricing in a structural shift away from US tech concentration toward international diversification and cyclical sectors. Memory supply constraints may limit the AI buildout's near-term trajectory, while geopolitical tensions and Fed pause expectations provide additional crosscurrents for positioning decisions ahead.
