US equities hit fresh records despite oil surging to $108 on the prolonged Hormuz closure, as investors remained focused on AI-driven semiconductor gains ahead of major tech earnings. The Fed prepares for its final meeting under Powell's leadership Wednesday, with markets pricing rate cuts in the second half of 2026 despite mounting energy-driven inflation pressures. Iran's proposal to reopen the strait in exchange for lifting the US port blockade made little progress, leaving both chokepoints closed as the standoff continues.

**Oil & Energy Crisis**

Brent crude reached $108 per barrel, marking its sixth consecutive daily gain as the Strait of Hormuz remains shut with no diplomatic breakthrough in sight.

- WTI traded at $96, with Goldman Sachs raising its Q4 Brent forecast to $90 from $60 pre-conflict
- US gasoline prices hit $4.11 per gallon, creating political pressure ahead of the summer driving season
- Iran proposed reopening Hormuz in exchange for the US lifting its port blockade, but Trump dismissed this as a "non-starter," calling the blockade "crucial leverage"
- Goldman warned that extreme inventory drawdowns are unsustainable, with record-low cushions building
- Forward oil curves remain elevated, with December Brent at the highest levels since the conflict began

**Equity Markets & Semiconductors**

US markets extended their rally to record highs, driven by an unprecedented 18-day winning streak in chip stocks that has added 40% to the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index.

- S&P 500 posted its fourth consecutive weekly gain, reaching new all-time highs
- Semiconductor names surged on AI infrastructure optimism, with TSMC posting its best 3-day performance since November 2022
- Asian equities gained 1.6%, led by memory and chip manufacturers including Samsung and SK Hynix
- The Nikkei 225 broke above 60,000 for the first time, while the Kospi advanced 2%
- VIX climbed above 19 despite equity resilience, signaling underlying tension

**Central Bank Week**

Four major central banks meet this week amid conflicting signals from energy-driven inflation and slowing growth momentum.

- Fed holds Wednesday in Powell's final meeting as chair, with Kevin Warsh's Senate confirmation vote scheduled for the same day
- Markets price Fed rate cuts in the second half of 2026 despite gasoline inflation, expecting a shift to a more symmetrical policy stance
- Bank of Japan meets Tuesday with markets skeptical of near-term action despite the yen trading near 160 per dollar
- ECB and Bank of England both meet Thursday, with the ECB facing pressure from persistent energy price shocks
- Ten-year Treasury yields rose 2 basis points to 4.33%, with the 2-10 spread at 52 basis points

**Geopolitical Developments**

The Hormuz stalemate persisted despite diplomatic efforts, while domestic security concerns escalated following violence at the White House Correspondents' Dinner.

- Iran's proposal to separate Hormuz reopening from nuclear negotiations gained little traction with US officials
- Trump stated that Iran's oil storage capacity would be full "in a couple of days," increasing pressure for a resolution
- A shooting at the White House Correspondents' Dinner exposed security failures, with the attacker having conducted multi-year weapons preparations
- European natural gas prices declined on hopes for diplomatic progress, though medium-term energy vulnerability remains elevated

**Corporate Earnings & Deals**

The Magnificent Seven companies representing $14.3 trillion in market capitalization report results Wednesday and Thursday, with markets focused on AI capex sustainability.

- Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Alphabet report Wednesday, followed by Apple on Thursday
- Over 80% of US companies have beaten earnings expectations, though analysts note this may reflect significantly lowered bars
- United and American Airlines confirmed their merger discussions have ended, leaving the door open for other industry consolidation
- European EV sales surged 37% year-over-year in March, driven by regulatory mandates rather than fuel price considerations

**Market Positioning & Outlook**

Professional investors showed growing caution despite retail enthusiasm, with hedge funds reducing oil exposure even as prices surged.

- Open interest in oil futures remained flat versus eight weeks ago despite the 16% price surge over five days
- Credit spreads remained tight and volatility low, with traders describing markets as "asleep" to geopolitical risks
- The dollar weakened 0.1% as rate cut expectations for 2026 built, despite strong US economic momentum
- Gold showed tactical weakness, failing to rally on conflict escalation after recent retail deleveraging
