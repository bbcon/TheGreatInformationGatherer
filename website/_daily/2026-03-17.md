DAILY BRIEF:
TSMC's 37% January sales surge validated continued AI infrastructure demand as Nvidia outlined a $1 trillion revenue target through 2027. The RBA delivered a second consecutive 25bp hike to 4.1% in a split 5-4 vote, explicitly citing war-driven inflation risks ahead of Fed and ECB decisions this week. Iran's escalation against Gulf energy infrastructure pushed Brent crude to $104 while closing the Strait of Hormuz, forcing Middle East producers to cut 1.5-3 million barrels per day.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Iran's targeting of UAE and Saudi energy infrastructure marked a significant escalation beyond US bilateral tensions, with operational impacts mounting across the Gulf region.

- Brent crude rose 4.5% to $104/barrel, WTI reached $97.50 approaching the $100 threshold
- UAE gas production fell 20% following strikes on the Shaar field, affecting 5% of global sulfur supply
- Fujairah port operations halted after fire, the second major UAE energy facility hit in one week
- Middle East oil output cuts reached 1.5-3 million bpd from infrastructure attacks
- Strait of Hormuz closure affected 16+ ships, though transit remains possible with elevated risk

Israel eliminated Iran's National Security Council chief and Basij paramilitary leader in what sources described as the most significant Iranian leadership decapitation since the Supreme Leader. The leadership losses prompted asymmetric retaliation against Gulf energy assets, expanding conflict geography beyond US-Iran dynamics.

**Central Banks & Monetary Policy**

The RBA's 25bp hike to 4.1% in a closely divided 5-4 board vote set a hawkish precedent for other major central banks facing similar war-driven inflation pressures.

- Governor Bullock explicitly cited "secondary inflation effects" from the Iran war as a policy driver
- Australian inflation forecasts revised higher to a 5% peak from 4.2% previously on energy costs
- AUD 2-year yields declined 1bp post-decision despite the hike, as markets interpreted the split vote as less hawkish than feared
- Fed decision Wednesday expected to hold rates but forward guidance critical given US Q4 GDP revised down to 4.7%
- ECB Thursday decision likely to include alternative scenarios for prolonged energy shock in new forecasts

Private credit stress emerged as Morgan Stanley warned of 8% default rates from AI disruption in the software sector, compounded by elevated leverage and approaching maturity walls.

**Technology & AI Development**

Nvidia's ambitious $1 trillion sales target through 2027 drove semiconductor rallies across Asia, with the company expanding beyond GPUs into CPUs and inference chips via the Grock acquisition.

- Samsung gained 3% on HBM4E debut and hybrid copper bonding technology advances
- TSMC benefited from the broader Nvidia ecosystem expansion
- China tech names rallied with Alibaba up 3% on AI unit consolidation plans
- Asian equity volumes remained 20% below average despite price gains, suggesting low conviction trading

Leap Motor reported its first annual profit of $78M on 596K unit deliveries in 2025, targeting 1 million units in 2026 with 850K in China and 150K overseas markets.

**Emerging Market Pressure**

Energy-importing economies faced mounting pressure as oil's sustained elevation strained current accounts and forced policy responses.

- Philippine peso approached 60 versus the dollar, prompting central bank intervention
- Indonesian equities erased gains back to pre-2021 levels despite FX intervention efforts
- Remittances from the Middle East totaling $600M annually (8-10% of GDP) for the Philippines face disruption risk
- China's yield curve steepened with the 1Y-30Y spread reaching its widest level in months as inflation expectations built

**Geopolitical Realignment**

Trump's postponement of the Xi summit citing war priorities masked deeper structural issues in US-China relations, with Paris talks yielding only a "concept of a plan" for trade mechanisms.

- NATO allies including Germany, France, and Italy rejected US requests for Strait of Hormuz escort missions
- Only Baltic states and Finland expressed support for military involvement
- China welcomed the summit delay given lack of substantive deliverables and risk of Middle East tensions overshadowing talks

**Supply Chain Risks**

Naphtha shortages threatened Asian supply chains with Japan holding only 20 days of reserves versus 200+ for crude oil.

- South Korea considering export controls on petrochemical feedstocks
- Helium supplies from Qatar face disruption, impacting semiconductor manufacturing
- Plastics, food packaging, and automotive sectors vulnerable to extended supply disruptions
