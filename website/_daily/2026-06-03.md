DAILY BRIEF:
The US proposed 10-12.5% tariffs on 60 trading partners under Section 301 authority, citing forced labor concerns in a legally sounder framework than previous levies. AI momentum accelerated with SpaceX planning a record $75 billion IPO and Marvell surging 33% after Jensen Huang's endorsement. Partners Group gated redemptions at 5% quarterly after receiving 9.8% redemption requests, highlighting stress in private markets.

**Trade Policy & Tariffs**

The Trump administration unveiled Section 301 tariffs targeting forced labor practices across major economies, with hearings scheduled for July 7.

- 10% tariffs proposed for Canada, Mexico, EU, Taiwan, and UK
- 12.5% tariffs for China, India, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, and Switzerland  
- Legal framework considered more robust than prior levies struck down by Supreme Court
- 4-6 week comment period before implementation creates negotiation window

**Equities & AI Developments**

Global equity momentum extended with the S&P 500 notching its ninth consecutive gain while AI themes dominated sector performance.

- MSCI ACWI posted 11th straight day of gains, approaching longest streak since 2004
- Marvell jumped 33% after Jensen Huang cited it as potential "next trillion-dollar company"
- SpaceX announced $75 billion IPO at $135/share, doubling Saudi Aramco's record
- Japan outperformed with Nikkei +2.5% on foreign inflows of $74 billion YTD
- China mixed: CSI 300 +0.9%, Hang Seng -0.6% as Tencent gave back gains despite AI agent launch

**Private Markets Stress**

Partners Group's redemption gate highlighted broader pressure in alternative investments as retail investors sought liquidity.

- 9.8% redemption requests on $8.6 billion global value fund
- Quarterly redemption cap set at 5%, primarily affecting Asia-Pacific investors
- Partners Group shares fell 18% in Zurich trading
- Redemption anxiety spreading from private credit to private equity structures

**Central Bank Developments**

Federal Reserve officials signaled potential for earlier rate hikes as labor market strength persisted, while the BoE adopted increasingly hawkish rhetoric.

- US job openings jumped to highest level in nearly two years
- Cleveland Fed's Hammack suggested policy "may soon need to act" with neutral rate at 4.00-4.25% vs current 3.625%
- BoE's Bailey warned against waiting for second-round inflation effects, citing 2022 delays
- September BoE hike fully priced but officials hint at earlier action

**Currency & Commodities**

Dollar strength resumed on tariff headlines and shifting rate expectations, pressuring emerging market currencies.

- USD/JPY approached 160 handle with BOJ intervention expected but likely ineffective
- Australian dollar and Chinese yuan weakened on trade policy concerns
- Copper and aluminum hit near-record highs on AI infrastructure demand
- Oil gained 2.7-3% on Middle East tensions (WTI $96.35, Brent $98.59)

**Geopolitical Tensions**

Middle East ceasefire remained fragile after US strikes on Iranian command centers following ballistic missile attacks.

- Kuwait airport operations halted due to regional conflict
- OECD revised global growth forecast to 1.8% in downside scenario
- Interim deal negotiations continue despite sticking points on Lebanon and uranium enrichment

**Key Risks & Outlook**

Valuation concerns mounted as equity multiples reached "excessively enthusiastic" levels driven purely by AI narratives, while policy uncertainty created tactical opportunities.

- VIX remained subdued despite high individual stock dispersion
- Memory chip supply constraints expected through 2030 per SK Hynix
- Token cost pressures likely to squeeze margins at Chinese hyperscalers
- Korea and Taiwan concentration risks persist with 50% index weights in top holdings
