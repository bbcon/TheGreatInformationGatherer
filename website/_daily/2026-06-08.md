DAILY BRIEF:
Israel-Iran missile exchanges pushed Brent crude to $97/barrel amid Red Sea shipping threats. Friday's blowout US payrolls report eliminated Fed rate cut expectations, with markets now pricing at least one hike by year-end. Asian equities plunged on the dual pressures, with Korea's KOSPI triggering circuit breakers on an 8% decline.

**Geopolitics & Oil**

Israel struck Iranian military targets including the Mashad petrochemical complex after Tehran's missile attacks, further imperiling the fragile ceasefire on day 100 of the conflict. Houthis threatened Red Sea blockades of Israeli-linked vessels, creating risks for critical Saudi export routes.

- Brent crude surged 4.5% to $97/barrel, WTI to $93
- December Brent contracts remained below $90, signaling backwardation and near-term geopolitical premium
- Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, choking Saudi capacity from 10M bpd to 7M bpd production
- OPEC+ spare capacity largely trapped in Persian Gulf region
- Energy stocks only positive European sector as refining margins expanded

**Fed Policy Repricing**

Friday's payrolls blowout fundamentally shifted Fed expectations, with Goldman Sachs abandoning rate cut calls and BNP Paribas now pricing three hikes starting December. The strong labor market data leaves the Fed boxed in ahead of Wednesday's CPI release.

- Market now prices 50-100bps of hikes by year-end, up from cuts previously
- 2-year Treasury yield rose 12bps cumulatively since Friday to ~5.0%
- 30-year yield hit 5.0% handle, though moved less than front-end (bear flattening)
- 2s10s spread at 38bps as hike expectations dominated
- Real yields driving the move higher, suggesting Fed inflation credibility intact

**Asian Equity Rout**

Tech-heavy Asian indices suffered their worst session since March as the dual pressures of geopolitical tensions and Fed repricing triggered leveraged retail unwinding.

- MSCI Asia Pacific fell 3.4% in worst session since March
- Korean KOSPI plunged 8% triggering circuit breakers, later recovering to -7%
- Taiwan declined 4.5%, Nikkei down 4%
- Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell 7% on AI bubble concerns post-Broadcom guidance
- Single-stock Korea ETFs posted double-digit losses as forced options selling amplified downside
- Chinese CSI 300 outperformed at -1.5% with defensive positioning in financials

**FX & Rates**

EM Asian currencies remained under broad pressure despite central bank intervention efforts, while dollar-yen pushed above 160 raising intervention risks.

- Korean won rebounded 2% after authorities unveiled currency support measures
- Indonesian rupiah hit record low at 18,134, Philippine peso at 62 handle
- Dollar-yen above 160 with intervention risk rising
- EUR/USD flat around 1.08, fairly valued with weakening fundamentals
- UK 2-year gilt yield rose 6bps (double Germany's 3bps) as BOE hike expectations increased

**Key Data & Events**

Wednesday's US CPI release emerges as the critical near-term catalyst, with consensus at 4.2% headline—potentially cementing Fed hike expectations if it prints above 4%.

- ECB decision Thursday expected to deliver 25bps hike to 2.25% deposit rate
- Wage growth showed no month-over-month acceleration in payrolls, potentially allowing Fed to look through strong headline
- SpaceX IPO Friday represents largest ever at $200B+ valuation amid volatile conditions
- Supply wave threatens market indigestion: $85B Alphabet equity raise, Meta considering similar

**Risks & Outlook**

The convergence of geopolitical escalation, inflation concerns, and tech sector deleveraging creates a challenging backdrop. However, analysts maintain the AI selloff represents a technical correction within a structural bull market, while oil's failure to spike above $100 despite Iran strikes suggests demand weakness and strategic reserve buffers are containing the geopolitical premium.

Markets face an impossible Fed scenario: cutting into 4% inflation appears untenable, yet hiking into midterms carries political risks. The coming week's CPI data will likely determine whether current hike expectations prove prescient or excessive.
