DAILY BRIEF:
Oil plunged 5.6% below $100/barrel on optimism around a potential US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, lifting global equities to near-record highs. China launched dual regulatory crackdowns targeting illegal cross-border trading and coal mine safety following the deadliest mining blast since 2009. Consumer sentiment deteriorated sharply to 44.8, highlighting the growing divergence between market euphoria and economic fundamentals.

**Oil & Geopolitics**

Oil markets rallied aggressively on deal optimism, though structural challenges remain unresolved.

- Brent crude fell to $97.80 (-5.6%), breaking below the $100 psychological level for the first time since the Hormuz crisis began
- WTI approached $90, though prices remain 55% above pre-conflict levels
- UAE estimates 3-4 months minimum timeline for 80% production normalization even if strait reopens immediately
- Insurance costs, demining operations, and logistics bottlenecks expected to persist for months
- Prediction markets swung from 81% to 48% deal probability over the weekend, highlighting fragility

**Equities & Sentiment**

Asian markets surged on geopolitical relief while US consumer confidence deteriorated sharply.

- Nikkei and Topix hit record highs, gaining approximately 3%
- S&P 500 futures rose 0.9%, European futures up 1%
- Japan's AI server and component makers rallied double-digits following Lenovo's earnings beat
- Taiwan attracted over $2bn in inflows Friday, with rotation from TSMC into niche AI infrastructure plays
- Consumer sentiment collapsed to 44.8 (University of Michigan), near multi-year lows despite elevated equity valuations
- Saxo estimates S&P 500 trading 2,000 points above fair value of 5,700, with froth concentrated in non-tech sectors

**China Regulatory Actions**

Beijing intensified oversight across multiple sectors following recent incidents.

- Coal mine blast in Shanxi province killed 82 people, triggering coking coal futures to daily limit up
- Safety inspections risk spreading nationwide, potentially tightening thermal coal supplies and forcing higher imports
- Cross-border trading crackdown targeted Futu, Tiger Brokers, and Longbridge for unlicensed mainland operations
- CITIC estimates mainland retail investors hold ~$30bn in offshore holdings, raising forced liquidation risks
- Golden Dragon Index fell 2.2% Friday on broker crackdown uncertainty
- CSI 300 gained 0.6% as chip stocks surged on Huawei's reported 1.4nm breakthrough

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

Policy divergence widened between White House expectations and market pricing.

- Traders fully priced a 26bp Fed hike for 2025, contradicting White House advisor Kevin Hassett's expectation for cuts following oil price decline
- ECB's Lagarde signaled likely inflation outlook revision higher in June, paving way for potential rate hikes
- 10-year JGB yields fell 6bp to 2.70% on safe-haven demand; US Treasuries closed for Memorial Day
- Bond markets pricing term premium repricing rather than inflation fears, with 5y5y expectations remaining anchored

**Commodities & Food Crisis**

Supply disruptions continued reverberating through agricultural markets.

- Fertilizer prices surged 25-55% for urea, with natural gas up 60-85%
- Grain prices extended gains: corn +9%, soybeans +25%, wheat +8%, rice +2-9% since conflict began
- Day 90 of Hormuz closure approaching critical threshold affecting global crop planting decisions
- Freight costs jumped 43% due to shipping disruptions

**Currency & EM Markets**

Risk-on sentiment supported emerging market assets while structural forces favored select currencies.

- USD declined 0.3% as geopolitical tensions eased temporarily
- CNY strengthened 3% year-to-date despite 40-50bp rate differential widening, supported by productivity gains
- Indonesian equities faced pressure after FTSE Russell removed four stocks for free float violations
- RBI governor noted rupee "may be undervalued" following record lows, with intervention ongoing

**AI & Technology**

Momentum continued across AI-related sectors despite intensifying price competition.

- Taiwan and Korea tech stocks benefited from infrastructure investment themes
- DeepSeek announced 75% permanent discounts, pressuring domestic Chinese AI ecosystem profitability
- Huawei's chip breakthrough lifted SMIC and Huahong Semi 5-10%
- NASDAQ 100 approached 29,900 fair value level with momentum intact
