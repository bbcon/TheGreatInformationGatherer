DAILY BRIEF:
Trump extended the Iran ceasefire indefinitely following the collapse of peace talks in Islamabad, though the Strait of Hormuz blockade remains in place with continued sporadic attacks. UK inflation accelerated to 3.3% in March, driven by surging oil prices as Brent crude touched $100 before retreating. Fed Chair nominee Kevin Worsh pledged independence during his confirmation hearing but signaled a potential shift away from forward guidance, creating uncertainty around Treasury volatility.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

The ceasefire extension provided limited market relief as fundamental tensions persist. Iranian state media reported unconfirmed signals of US willingness to lift the naval blockade, sending Brent down 1.2% to approximately $97.

- Strait of Hormuz remains contested with both sides maintaining blockades despite ceasefire
- December WTI contracts still trading above $75, indicating sustained risk premium
- Floating oil storage declining sharply as Asian importers drain stockpiles
- Satellite data shows thousands of buildings hit in Tehran, with reconstruction costs projected in the hundreds of billions
- No agreement reached on uranium enrichment, missile programs, or militant group support

**Inflation & Central Banks**

UK inflation data highlighted energy shock transmission as oil price increases flow through to consumer prices. The Federal Reserve confirmation process remains uncertain amid political standoffs.

- UK CPI rose to 3.3% in March from 3.0% prior, driven by energy costs
- Fed Chair nominee Worsh emphasized independence but rejected forward guidance framework
- Worsh floated shifting from PCE to trimmed mean inflation measures and eliminating the dot plot
- Confirmation hinges on Trump dropping DOJ investigation into current Fed leadership
- Senator Tillis blocking nomination vote until investigation resolution
- Only 40% probability of Fed rate cuts priced for 2024
- 2-year Treasury yield at 3.77%, minimal reaction to Worsh testimony

**Equities & Earnings**

Asian markets showed mixed performance with profit-taking in recent outperformers, while US equity futures remained resilient despite energy headwinds.

- S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures higher, with asset managers holding record net long positions per CFTC data
- Taiwan outperformed (+0.5-0.8%) on broader AI stack exposure versus Korea's memory-only semiconductor play
- Hong Kong underperformed (-1%, Hang Seng Tech -2%) amid China tech profit-taking
- Korea defense stocks surged 10% (LG Defense) on regional tensions
- United Airlines rose despite cutting outlook as investors focused on ceasefire extension
- Capital One fell 2.5% after missing estimates and increasing loan loss provisions

**Fixed Income & FX**

China emerged as an unexpected safe haven with both currency and bonds outperforming regional peers, while broader dollar weakness reflected ceasefire optimism.

- Chinese yuan at 6.82, reaching 3-year highs despite broader EM FX weakness
- China 10-year government bond yields fell to 1.73%, 6-month lows with analysts targeting 1.60%
- Chinese government bonds now yield below Japanese government bonds for first time
- Dollar index down 0.3%, reversing prior session gains
- Japanese yen hovering near 160 as carry trades resume despite BOJ intervention risks
- Only 13 basis points of Fed cuts priced for 2025, reflecting market skepticism

**Corporate Developments**

Technology and consumer sectors showed mixed signals amid broader economic uncertainty and shifting demand patterns.

- CATL declined 2% despite unveiling 6-minute fast-charging battery technology, with Citigroup reducing stake
- OpenAI discontinued Sora video service after losing $5 per 30-second video, highlighting AI economics challenges
- Airlines facing significant operational disruption with Lufthansa cutting 20,000 flights due to doubled jet fuel prices
- iQiyi's AI film strategy questioned following OpenAI's video economics struggles

**Emerging Market Impact**

Developing economies face mounting pressure from the dual impact of higher energy costs and reduced remittance flows.

- IMF projects African growth slowdown to 4.3% from 4.5% due to remittance disruption and oil shock
- Philippines credit outlook cut to negative by Fitch citing declining public investment and rising energy costs
- Indonesia expected to hold rates to defend rupiah at record lows
- Southeast Asian economies particularly vulnerable given energy import dependence

**Risk Assessment**

Markets appear to be trading in a narrow range despite headline volatility, with December crude contracts suggesting expectations for eventual normalization. However, the combination of persistent geopolitical tensions, potential Fed communication changes, and emerging consumer credit stress signals warrant cautious positioning. The UK faces particular fiscal pressure with the Resolution Foundation warning that severe energy shock scenarios could eliminate 75% of Chancellor Reeves's fiscal headroom.
