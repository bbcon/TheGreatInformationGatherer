DAILY BRIEF:
Brent crude fell 5% to $100/barrel after reaching $115 earlier in the session, providing relief to equity markets that rallied 1% despite ongoing Middle East tensions. China's stronger-than-expected January-February data supported risk appetite, with industrial production rising 6.3% versus 5% in December. The Federal Reserve begins its two-day meeting tomorrow facing a policy paralysis scenario as oil-driven inflation pressures conflict with growth concerns.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Oil markets showed significant volatility as geopolitical developments continued to dominate price action.

- Brent crude settled near $100/barrel, down 5% from session highs of $115
- WTI fell below $94, though both benchmarks remain 40% above pre-conflict levels
- Strait of Hormuz effectively closed to most oil tankers, with Iran allowing only select vessels through
- UAE oil infrastructure struck this week, escalating regional tensions
- Trump demands NATO and Chinese assistance securing shipping lanes, threatening to delay April Xi summit

**Federal Reserve & Monetary Policy**

The Fed faces unprecedented dual mandate tension as oil shocks create stagflationary pressures just as the central bank begins its March meeting.

- Two-year Treasury yields rose to 3.70% as traders priced out all Fed cuts for 2025
- PCE core inflation at 3.1% expected to be revised higher due to oil shock
- Bloomberg Economics models suggest sustained $100 oil adds 0.1% to full-year inflation
- Former Minneapolis Fed President Stern expects Fed "on hold until more clarity on war duration"
- 22 central banks meeting this week, with RBA likely to deliver back-to-back hikes Tuesday

**Equities & Technology**

Markets rallied on falling oil prices, though gains moderated after closer examination of corporate guidance.

- S&P 500 gained 1% to 6,699, still 3% below pre-conflict levels
- Nasdaq advanced 1.2% led by technology names
- Nvidia rose 1.6% after reaching session highs of 4.8%, but gave back gains when its $1 trillion revenue target through 2027 revealed timeline extension rather than acceleration
- MAG 7 stocks remain in technical correction territory, down ~7% from recent peaks
- Bitcoin approached $75,000, up 3% as risk appetite improved

**China & Asia-Pacific**

Chinese economic data provided a bright spot amid global uncertainty, though regional currency pressures intensified.

- Industrial production surged 6.3% year-over-year in January-February versus 5% in December
- Retail sales gained 3% compared to 1% in December, both beating consensus expectations
- Asian currencies under pressure as Japan, India, and Indonesia intervened to support their currencies
- Yen approached 160 versus the dollar, setting up potential BOJ action this week
- China's 6.5% energy dependence on Strait of Hormuz complicates Trump's demand for naval assistance

**Credit & Fixed Income**

Investment grade credit markets showed signs of stress as multiple headwinds converged.

- Investment grade CDS widening amid oil shock and increased new issuance supply
- Private credit sector facing intensified pressure with software exposure exceeding 20% of direct lending
- AI disruption threatening ARR-based valuations in private BDCs
- Ten-year Treasury yields declined 4-5 basis points to 4.22%, down from below 4% in late February
- European duration facing pressure as ECB may sound more hawkish given asymmetric oil impact

**Currency & Commodities**

Dollar strength showed signs of moderation as oil prices retreated from recent highs.

- Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index posted biggest drop in 1.5 months
- EUR/USD held near 1.08 ahead of central bank meetings
- Crowded underweight USD trades continuing to reverse as consensus positioning unwinds
- Fertilizer stocks declined 5.6% with CF Industries leading losses as Scotia warned valuations "overstretched"

**Outlook & Risks**

Multiple crosscurrents create heightened uncertainty for policy and market direction in coming sessions.

- Fed policy paralysis likely as oil shock raises inflation while threatening growth simultaneously
- Private credit liquidity concerns building with redemption pressures in direct lending funds
- Semiconductor supply chain vulnerability from Middle East helium and sulfuric acid disruptions
- No clear conflict off-ramp visible, though $100 Brent likely represents ceiling absent major escalation

The combination of geopolitical uncertainty, central bank policy constraints, and corporate earnings reality checks suggests continued market volatility ahead, with traditional risk-off trades complicated by inflationary pressures from energy disruption.
