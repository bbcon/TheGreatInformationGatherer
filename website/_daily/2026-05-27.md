DAILY BRIEF:
Markets hit fresh all-time highs as memory chip giants Micron and SK Hynix joined the trillion-dollar club on AI infrastructure demand. Oil plunged 5-6% on unverified reports of a US-Iran draft agreement, though no official breakthrough has been confirmed. The RBNZ delivered a hawkish surprise with a 3-3 committee split on immediate rate hikes, signaling faster policy tightening ahead.

**Equities & Earnings**

All four major US indices reached simultaneous records (S&P 500, equal-weight S&P, Russell 2000, Nasdaq 100) for only the second time since October. However, market breadth remains concerning with just 53-54% of S&P 500 stocks in uptrends, well below the healthy 65-70% threshold.

- Goldman raised S&P 500 year-end target to 8,000 on earnings momentum
- S&P 500 earnings growth expected at 20-23% for 2025, with tech up 40%+ year-over-year
- Only tech sector outperformed over the past 30 days, highlighting narrow leadership
- Credit spreads reached 1bp from post-2000 record lows on investment-grade bonds

**Memory Chip Surge**

Memory semiconductor names drove the rally as supply-demand imbalances and AI infrastructure spending created a structural shift from cyclical to secular growth.

- Micron gained 20% pre-market after UBS doubled its price target to $1,625
- SK Hynix crossed $1T market cap, up 1,000% over 12 months on single-digit P/E multiples
- Micron hit $1T market cap in a record 49 days (fastest 500B→1T transition ever)
- ByteDance planning $70-100B capex in 2025 versus $25B in 2024
- Memory chip shortages worsening with 150M smartphone equivalents of supply diverted to Nvidia's Vera CPU
- Samsung union approved wage deal with 73.7% voting yes, ending strike risk with average $340K bonuses

**Energy & Iran Negotiations**

WTI crude fell to $89.98 (-4%) and Brent to $96.10 on speculation about US-Iran agreement progress, though significant gaps remain on sequencing.

- Iranian TV reported unofficial US-Iran MOU draft including naval blockade lift
- Iran demands $24B asset unfreezing ($12B immediate, $12B over 60-day ceasefire) before nuclear discussions
- US gas prices dropped below $4.50/gallon for first time in three weeks
- Energy stocks declined: Exxon and Chevron fell on lower oil price expectations
- Airlines gained 2% (United, American) on reduced fuel cost outlook

**Central Bank Policy**

The RBNZ surprised markets with hawkish guidance despite holding rates steady, while Fed officials continued signaling concern about persistent inflation.

- RBNZ committee split 3-3 on immediate hike, with statement saying "OCR likely to need to rise more and sooner"
- OCR forecast lifted to 2.84% by end-2026, with inflation not returning to 2% target until Q3 2027
- NZ dollar gained 0.7% to 0.5875, with 2-year yields up 5bps
- Fed's Kashkari and Logan warned that 5+ years of inflation above target risks unanchoring expectations
- Markets pricing ~75% probability of BOJ hike at June 16 meeting
- Core PCE data due tomorrow expected at 0.3% monthly, 3.3% year-over-year

**China & Regional Markets**

Chinese industrial profits surged while regulatory tightening continued to weigh on sentiment despite strong fundamentals.

- Industrial profits rose 18.2% year-over-year for January-April, with April alone up 24.7%
- AI talent travel restrictions implemented for Alibaba and DeepSeek staff
- Cross-border trading crackdown on Futu/Tiger brokers continues
- KOSPI gained 4% despite 80% of stocks declining, gains concentrated in Samsung/Hynix
- Beijing DeepZero Tech IPO surged 265% on AI-marketing positioning

**European Developments**

European car sales showed continued strength while Airbus resolved delivery disputes with China.

- European car sales up 7% in April, third consecutive monthly gain
- EVs gained 38%, now matching gasoline sales volume at 255k units each
- Airbus deliveries to China resumed after CEO's April Beijing visit resolved regulatory delays
- China deliveries fell 66% in January-May to 16 aircraft versus 47 prior year

**Risks & Outlook**

Tomorrow's PCE data will likely reinforce the Fed's hawkish pivot, with core inflation expected to remain a full percentage point above target. Memory chip valuations appear stretched despite single-digit multiples, with 8-10x earnings growth unlikely to sustain. Iran deal uncertainty persists with markets potentially pricing in overly optimistic outcomes given the "serious gaps" that remain in negotiations.
