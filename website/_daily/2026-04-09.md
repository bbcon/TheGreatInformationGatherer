**Markets pulled back from yesterday's ceasefire rally as the Iran-US agreement showed immediate signs of strain.** Oil rebounded 3% after yesterday's historic 20% plunge as the Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed despite the truce announcement. Asia equities fell 0.7-1% while US Treasury futures reversed their entire rally, with 10-year yields returning to 4.29%.

**Oil & Energy**

The physical oil market's resilience exposed the fragility of yesterday's futures collapse. Despite the ceasefire announcement, fundamental supply constraints persist.

- Brent crude gained 2.7-3% to $97-98/barrel, recovering from yesterday's drop from $117 to $91
- Dated Brent held firm at $124/barrel versus futures at $98—an unprecedented $26 premium signaling genuine physical tightness
- Strait of Hormuz traffic remained negligible with fewer than 6 vessels attempting passage in 24 hours post-ceasefire
- Approximately 800 ships remain stranded in the Persian Gulf, including 42 Japanese vessels
- Iran demanding $1-2/barrel transit fees through crypto/Chinese banking channels, creating sanctions compliance issues for Western shippers
- Goldman maintains Brent will average above $100 through 2026 if the Strait remains disrupted for another month
- US gasoline prices at $4.16/gallon nationally, continuing their multi-week climb

**Equities & Risk Assets**

Asian markets reversed course as ceasefire optimism faded, though AI infrastructure demand showed resilience.

- MSCI Asia fell 0.9% with CSI 300 down 0.9%, Hang Seng -0.7%, and Nikkei -0.6%
- S&P futures declined 0.4% after yesterday's 2.5% rally
- Samsung reported preliminary Q1 profits up 8x year-over-year, validating continued AI capex demand
- Heavy foreign buying into Taiwan and Korea tech: TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix benefited from AI infrastructure narrative
- Chinese software stocks fell 3% as Meta's Muse Spark and Anthropic's Claude agents launch intensified AI competition concerns
- Jefferies cut Alibaba target by 13% citing competitive AI spending pressures
- Energy stocks rebounded while technology reversed yesterday's ceasefire-driven gains

**Central Banks & Monetary Policy**

Fed policy expectations shifted hawkishly as oil's rebound threatened the inflation outlook.

- Fed minutes confirmed rate hikes remain on the table if the Middle East conflict extends
- One-year CPI swaps at 3.2%, with potential to stay elevated if oil averages $100+ versus falling below 3% if oil returns to $80-90 range
- RBNZ held rates at 2.25% but Governor Orman warned of "decisive action" if core inflation accelerates, putting July hike on the table
- Bank of England raised UK inflation expectations by 1 percentage point due to elevated energy prices
- Core PCE data due today at 1:30pm UK time represents a critical test of Fed resolve

**Geopolitical Developments**

The ceasefire showed immediate structural flaws as both sides disputed terms and fighting continued.

- US and Iran appear to have agreed to different ceasefire frameworks—US claims Lebanon excluded while Iran says included
- Israeli forces launched strikes on 100+ Hezbollah targets in Lebanon in what observers called the "bloodiest day" of the war
- JD Vance leads US delegation to Islamabad Saturday for direct talks, but prospects appear dim given competing 15-point (US) versus 10-point (Iran) negotiating positions
- Iran claims "several violations" of ceasefire terms while demanding maximalist concessions including Strait sovereignty and proxy funding rights
- Hungary election polls show opposition leading by 10 points, potentially ending Orban's 16-year tenure

**Currency & Fixed Income**

Safe-haven flows returned as ceasefire durability came into question.

- USD gained 0.06-0.07% as risk-off sentiment re-emerged
- One-week USD risk reversals continued showing upside hedging demand
- US Treasury futures completely reversed yesterday's rally, with the curve flattening as front-end yields stayed elevated
- European bank stocks faced pressure from bear-flattening yield curves amid persistent oil uncertainty

**Outlook**

The massive $26 premium of physical oil over futures signals genuine supply disruption that transcends speculative positioning. With CTA and vol-target funds below the 20th percentile, positioning remains extremely clean, suggesting limited natural buyers on further weakness. The ceasefire's immediate breakdown in implementation—particularly around Strait access and Lebanon's status—suggests markets may have overpriced the peace dividend in yesterday's rally.
