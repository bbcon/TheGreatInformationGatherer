Market futures rose modestly ahead of Nvidia earnings as software stocks rebounded from AI disruption concerns. Japan's yen weakened sharply to 155.63 after PM Ishiba nominated two dovish BOJ board members, triggering a selloff in long-dated JGBs. Australia's inflation beat shifted RBA policy expectations toward a near-certain rate hike.

**Equities & Earnings**

US markets posted modest gains with the S&P 500 up 0.3% and Nasdaq advancing 0.4% as investors positioned ahead of Nvidia's results.

- Software sector outperformed (+0.8%) despite ongoing AI disruption fears, with relief from Anthropic partnership announcements
- Workday fell 10% on weak guidance as AI monetization concerns dominated despite strong fundamentals  
- TJX disappointed with FY27 EPS guidance of $4.93-5.02 versus $5.18 consensus, putting its 18-year winning streak at risk
- Lowe's declined 3.5% on weak full-year outlook, with management citing housing macro "remains pressured"
- HP dropped 6% as AI PC demand was offset by memory cost pressures and tariff headwinds
- Cava surged 11% as a rare restaurant bright spot, with visit frequency down but ticket size increasing

Asian markets showed strength with South Korea's KOSPI crossing 6,000 for the first time, making it the 9th largest equity market globally. Taiwan saw its largest foreign inflows in 20 years.

**Central Banks & Monetary Policy**

Japan dominated central bank developments as political appointments shifted BOJ expectations.

- PM Ishiba nominated two dovish, reflationist board members, signaling reduced appetite for rate hikes
- JGB yields rose sharply: 10-year and 30-year both up over 10 basis points as curve steepened
- Market now pricing lower probability of BOJ rate increases despite persistent inflation pressures

Australia's central bank moved into focus after inflation significantly beat expectations, with the RBA now pricing approximately 100% probability of a 25bp hike at the next meeting.

Fed officials maintained cautious positioning, with Chicago Fed's Goolsbee expressing restraint on inflation despite steady labor conditions.

**Fixed Income & Credit**

US 10-year yields held near 4.05% in a range-bound pattern, with bond markets showing skepticism toward "roaring economy" narratives despite policy tailwinds.

- Credit spreads remained tight, driving relative value opportunities in Treasuries
- Private credit stress emerged as funds attempted unusual secondary market sales, raising contagion concerns
- UBS increased worst-case default forecasts to 15% from 13% on AI disruption risks
- Structured credit markets offered selective opportunities amid municipal and mortgage-backed security volatility

**Foreign Exchange**

The dollar weakened broadly as capital flowed into Asian currencies and commodity-linked FX.

- Japanese yen weakened to 155.63 (touching above 156 intraday) following dovish BOJ board nominations
- Korean won and Taiwan dollar strengthened on equity inflows and tech sector leadership
- Australian dollar emerged as the strongest G10 performer following inflation surprise and RBA policy shift

**Semiconductors & Technology**

The AMD-Meta partnership validated competition in AI infrastructure, with the deal including billions in AI equipment purchases plus warrants for 160 million AMD shares. This reinforced AMD's position as a credible Nvidia alternative and supported broader semiconductor outperformance versus MAG7 stocks.

Nvidia faces elevated expectations ahead of earnings, with the stock having underperformed the SOX index significantly since August (+10% versus +50%).

**Policy & Geopolitical**

Trump's State of the Union offered no material new policy substance, focusing on domestic messaging ahead of midterm elections. Trade policy remained in flux with a 10% baseline tariff now in place and 15% rates expected "in days," creating uncertainty around previously negotiated EU and UK deals at lower rates.

Consumer confidence data revealed sharp partisan divisions, with Democrats at 41 versus materially higher Republican readings, reflecting political polarization in economic sentiment.

**Risk Factors**

Private credit secondary market activity suggests potential liquidity stress in credit markets, with implications for IG credit and ETF spaces. Memory chip cost pressures are broadening beyond individual companies to signal potential supply chain inflation. Iran tensions persist with military action potentially "within coming week" according to market sources, though oil price spikes would complicate affordability narratives.

The convergence of AI disruption concerns, monetary policy divergence, and geopolitical tensions creates a complex backdrop as markets await tonight's Nvidia results for direction on technology leadership and AI infrastructure demand.
