DAILY BRIEF:
Geopolitical tensions escalated sharply as Iran struck Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG facility following Israeli attacks on Iran's South Pars gas field, removing 20% of global LNG supply from the market. Markets repriced Fed policy expectations as Brent crude surged above $113, with 2-year Treasury yields jumping 10bp to 380bp as inflation concerns overrode growth worries. Asian equities declined broadly on energy security fears, with the Nikkei falling over 3% as import-dependent economies faced stagflation risks.

**Energy Infrastructure Crisis**

The conflict moved beyond symbolic strikes to critical energy infrastructure targeting that threatens global supply chains.

- Qatar's Ras Laffan facility produces 7mn tons of LNG monthly, with potential 4-6 month shutdown removing 30mn tons from global markets
- Iran's South Pars gas field, the country's largest supplying Turkey and Iraq, sustained damage in Israeli strikes
- Brent crude peaked above $113 before settling up 3.8% as Trump deescalation comments provided relief
- Brent-WTI spread widened to $16 on expectations of US Strategic Petroleum Reserve intervention to cap domestic prices
- Asian LNG importers face acute shortages with Taiwan, India, and Pakistan deriving 20-25% of gas supplies from Qatar

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

Central banks across G10 economies shifted to hawkish repricing as energy price shocks threatened inflation targets.

- Fed funds futures now price less than 45% probability of any 2025 rate cut, down from 50%
- 2-year Treasury yields surged 10bp to 380bp in violent bear flattening as markets priced out easing expectations
- Powell acknowledged "uncertain" Middle East impact but maintained one cut projection despite market skepticism
- 2/10 spread compressed to 47bp as inflation fears dominated growth concerns, echoing 2022 dynamics
- US Treasury index on track for worst monthly performance in 17-18 months

**Central Bank Decisions**

Multiple central banks grappled with stagflation risks as energy price shocks complicated policy outlooks.

- Bank of Japan held rates at 0.75% in 8-1 vote, unusually flagging Middle East risks 3-4 times in statement
- BoJ Governor Ueda noted oil price rises will boost inflation but emphasized waiting for spring wage negotiations
- ECB and Bank of England decisions scheduled amid heightened uncertainty over energy price persistence
- Markets now price full 25bp UK rate hike by year-end, reversing dovish expectations from three weeks prior
- SNB faces intervention pressure as CHF safe-haven flows threaten deflation with rates already near zero

**Equities & Earnings**

Asian markets declined broadly as energy importers faced twin pressures of higher costs and currency weakness.

- MSCI Asia fell 3% with Nikkei leading declines at -3%+ as Japan's energy import dependence weighed
- Tencent dropped 6% despite 13-14% revenue growth as AI capex doubling to $5.2bn and reduced buyback program disappointed investors
- HSBC declined 2.5% on reports of potential 20,000 job cuts (10% of workforce) through AI-driven restructuring
- Chinese energy stocks gained 3-4% as the only green sector amid oil rally
- US equity futures reached session highs after Trump deescalation posts but volumes remained thin

**Foreign Exchange**

Dollar strength accelerated as energy importers faced acute pressure from twin shocks of higher oil prices and Fed repricing.

- USD/JPY reached 160 intervention watch level despite BoJ rate hold
- Korean won breached 1,500 psychological level with central bank defense lines under pressure
- Philippine peso crossed 60 level despite intervention threats from BSP
- CNY remained relatively stable as China's energy security appeared less threatened
- EUR faced pressure as ECB confronts stagflation risk from energy shock

**Risk Outlook**

The conflict's evolution from symbolic strikes to infrastructure targeting raises stakes significantly for global markets.

- Iran threatened Saudi and UAE facilities as "legitimate targets" according to IRGC statements
- Trump's "knew nothing" post about South Pars strike and retaliation threats signal deescalation efforts
- Every $10 oil increase translates to -0.1pp impact on China GDP growth
- European industry faces 2-month window from shock to crisis based on historical precedents
- 94-95% of Japan's oil imports originate from Middle East, creating acute vulnerability

The market's violent repricing reflects genuine concerns about sustained energy price elevation rather than temporary disruption, with central banks forced to balance growth risks against inflation threats in an increasingly complex policy environment.
