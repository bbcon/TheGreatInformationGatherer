Oil markets extended their historic rally as the Iran conflict entered its fifth week, with Brent crude reaching $116/barrel amid escalating supply disruptions. Asian equities underperformed on energy import dependency concerns while central banks face mounting policy dilemmas. Chinese EV leader BYD warned of a "brutal knockout stage" for sector competition despite recovering from early losses.

**Energy & Commodities**

Crude oil maintained its unprecedented monthly surge with no diplomatic resolution in sight.

- Brent crude: $116/barrel (+60% month-to-date, largest monthly gain on record)
- WTI: $101/barrel (Brent-WTI spread widening to ~$15)
- Oil supply disruptions expanding beyond Strait of Hormuz as Houthis target Red Sea shipping
- Saudi exports via Yanbu port (7M bpd) at risk, potentially forcing African rerouting with $15-20/barrel premium
- US gasoline approaching $4.00/gallon threshold
- Analysts assign 40% probability to $200 oil scenario if Hormuz closure extends through June

Aluminum markets surged on Middle East supply shock.

- Aluminum: +5-6% after Iranian strikes on Gulf smelters
- Emirates Global Aluminum and Aluminium Bahrain facilities damaged (9% of global production)
- Chinese producers rallying: Chalco +7-9%
- Japan premiums reaching decade highs on supply constraints

**Equities & Risk Assets**

Global equities approached correction territory with Asian markets bearing the brunt of energy import concerns.

- MSCI All-Country World Index: -9.4% from peak (0.6% from technical correction)
- Nikkei: -4.5%, reflecting Japan's 90% Middle East oil import dependency
- South Korea Kospi: -3.5% (worst global performer this month, was best pre-war)
- China CSI 300: testing 200-day moving average at 4,500 for first time since Liberation Day
- Nasdaq 100: -11% from peak (entered correction Friday)
- VIX: sustained above 30 for first time since Liberation Day spike to 50

**Chinese Earnings & EV Sector**

BYD delivered mixed signals on China's EV market dynamics.

- BYD initially fell 5% before recovering to flat on Q4 profit plunge of 38%
- Chairman warned of "brutal knockout stage" as EV competition reaches fever pitch
- Warning signals margin compression across Chinese EV industry as demand growth slows
- Broader concerns about March/April PMI data potentially not capturing full economic impact of war

**Currency Markets & Central Bank Policy**

Dollar strength continued as safe-haven flows dominated, while Asian central banks face impossible policy choices.

- Dollar-yen: tested 160 handle, prompting Japanese intervention warnings
- BoJ officials signaling "bold action may be needed" (echoes July 2024 pre-intervention rhetoric)
- EM Asia FX: fourth consecutive week of declines on terms-of-trade shock
- RBI implementing $100M daily position limits for banks in rupee defense measures
- Market repricing front-end rate curves lower as central banks signal fiscal support over monetary tightening

Asian central banks particularly constrained by imported inflation versus growth trade-offs.

- Cannot afford rate hikes (would strengthen currencies, attract capital)
- Face rising imported inflation from energy costs
- Fed facing "triple whammy" of deportations, tariffs, and Iran war impacts

**Fixed Income & Safe Havens**

Treasuries caught modest bid despite rising inflation expectations as growth concerns emerge.

- 10-year Treasury yield: 438bp (-4bp), flight-to-quality demand
- 2s30s curve: compressed from 140bp to 100bp pre-war
- Gold: $4,523/oz (recovered from -15% drawdown), well above $2,900 fair value estimates
- Dual safe-haven bid in both dollar and gold suggests stagflation positioning

**Geopolitical & Supply Chain Risks**

Conflict escalation risks mounting with broader regional involvement.

- US deployed 3,500 Marines to region; Trump considering Kharg Island seizure operations
- Houthis fired first missiles at Israel, expanding conflict scope
- Qatar LNG facility damage: 17% capacity loss with 3-5 year repair timeline
- Middle East aluminum smelter attacks represent structural supply disruption
- Gulf states demanding "broader political solution" beyond ceasefire for Hormuz reopening

The convergence of energy supply shocks, growth concerns, and central bank policy constraints suggests markets may be transitioning toward a stagflationary regime, with inflation fears likely to reassert dominance before growth slowdown concerns materialize.
