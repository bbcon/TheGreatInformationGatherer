DAILY BRIEF:
Global markets staged a sharp rally as Trump backed away from his Iran ultimatum, granting five days for diplomatic talks via Truth Social. Oil fell below $100 per barrel for the first time in weeks despite the Strait of Hormuz remaining closed for 24 days. Treasury yields swung wildly before closing lower on geopolitical relief.

**Geopolitical Developments**

Trump's decision to extend diplomatic negotiations provided temporary market relief, though fundamental risks persist.

- Iran conflict enters day 24 with Strait of Hormuz effectively closed despite isolated transits
- Trump's original 48-hour ultimatum threatening Iranian power plants replaced with 5-day window for talks
- IDF reportedly detecting new missile launches, suggesting fragile ceasefire prospects
- Over 40 energy assets damaged across Middle East with 6-month recovery timeline per IEA

**Equities & Risk Assets**

Broad-based rally dominated as all 11 S&P sectors closed higher for the first time in months.

- S&P 500 +1.2% with 382 stocks advancing versus 122 declining
- Russell 2000 outperformed at +2.3%, suggesting renewed risk appetite
- Consumer Discretionary led at +2.5% on travel and leisure optimism
- Cruise lines surged 5-6% (Norwegian, Carnival, Royal Caribbean)
- Healthcare weakest sector at just +0.03%
- Palantir +6.8% as Maven AI becomes official Pentagon program
- Micron -4.4% for third consecutive day, now -13.5% from post-earnings highs on elevated capex concerns
- Estée Lauder -7.7% on reports of potential deal with Spanish beauty firm Puig

**Oil & Commodities**

Energy markets reversed sharply on de-escalation hopes despite ongoing supply disruptions.

- Brent crude fell to $99.70, WTI to $88.70 - first time below $100 in weeks
- TD Securities maintains $80 floor outlook rather than $60, citing structural supply constraints
- Goldman Sachs raised Brent forecast to $85 from $77, assuming 5% flow restoration over 6 weeks
- Saudi east-west pipeline running at 4x normal capacity (~3.2M bpd) providing partial relief
- Gold extended losses for ninth consecutive day, longest streak since October 2023

**Fixed Income**

Treasury markets experienced extreme volatility before settling lower on geopolitical relief.

- 2-year yield posted biggest intraday swing since August 2024 with 22bp peak-to-trough range
- 10-year yield -3bp, 30-year -2bp as markets unwound some stagflation positioning
- Markets had repriced Fed policy from 80% cut probability to 50% hike odds by October
- Bond selloff earlier driven by oil-induced inflation concerns over growth worries

**Central Bank Positioning**

Policy outlooks remain in flux as central banks balance energy-driven inflation against growth risks.

- Fed rate hike probability by October reached 50% before today's geopolitical relief
- ECB and BoE signaling readiness to hike despite growth concerns, citing 2022 energy shock experience
- South Korea nominated hawkish BIS economist Shin as BOK governor
- Bank of America removed China rate cut expectations on oil-driven inflation risks

**Asia-Pacific Impact**

Regional markets face acute vulnerability to extended energy supply disruptions.

- China trade surplus reached $1.2T in 2024 with exports to EU up 28% versus imports up 11%
- Korean won near 1,509 on energy import vulnerability
- KOSPI testing 5,500 support level amid memory and tech sector pressure
- Bank of America forecasts 0.7% China CPI, 0.3% PPI for 2026 on higher energy prices

**Outlook & Risks**

While markets celebrated diplomatic extension, fundamental supply constraints persist with limited visibility on resolution.

- Strait of Hormuz carries ~15 million bpd, equivalent to two major oil shocks combined
- Five-day diplomatic window provides limited time for substantive progress
- Treasury volatility suggests positioning remains fragile despite rally
- Gold's nine-day decline may signal oversold conditions if geopolitical risks re-escalate
- European economies remain particularly vulnerable given gas stockpile inadequacy and German/Italian industrial exposure

The market's risk-on response reflects relief over extended diplomacy rather than structural resolution of supply disruptions. With core energy infrastructure offline and repair timelines extending months, any diplomatic setbacks could trigger renewed volatility across asset classes.
