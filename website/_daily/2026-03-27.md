DAILY BRIEF:
Iran talks extended until April 6 failed to calm markets as oil surged above $110 and the NASDAQ 100 entered correction territory. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed with only 2 vessels per day versus 100+ pre-war, while Treasury yields hit 2026 highs as markets flip from pricing cuts to potential hikes. Private credit stress emerged with redemption caps at several managers despite Oak Tree meeting full requests.

**Energy & Geopolitics**

Oil markets continued pricing persistent disruption rather than resolution prospects.

- Brent crude rose to $113/barrel (+5% Friday session), WTI reached $100 (+6%)
- Physical markets trading $150+ versus futures disconnect signals severe supply constraints
- Strait of Hormuz closure represents 12 million barrels/day already lost, plus 20% of global LNG flows
- Iran's 5-point proposal demands sovereignty over strait, politically unpalatable to US/Israel
- Israel struck Iranian power plant Friday despite negotiation pause
- Demand destruction accelerating: Sri Lanka implementing 4-day work week, Philippines declaring energy emergency with 40-day supply remaining

**Fixed Income & Central Banks**

Treasury markets repriced policy expectations amid persistent supply shock concerns.

- 10-year yields climbed to 4.46% (+50bps since Monday's talks announcement), 2-year reached 4.01% - both new 2026 highs
- Fed funds futures now price 50% chance of rate hike by October, abandoning cut expectations entirely
- Real rates driving selloff rather than inflation expectations as markets price fiscal response to slowdown
- Weak Treasury auctions across 2Y/5Y/7Y maturities
- ECB pricing 75% chance of hike by year-end as officials signal "forceful response" to even modest inflation overshoot
- Fed officials (Cook, Barr, Jefferson) warning on inflation risks while policy remains "on hold indefinitely"

**Equities & Earnings**

Broad risk-off sentiment drove correction in growth indices with no sector leadership emerging.

- NASDAQ 100 fell 1.9% into correction territory (-10% from October peak)
- S&P 500 dropped 1.7% with 391 stocks declining versus 110 advancing
- March performance: S&P 500 -6% (lowest since early September), NASDAQ worst monthly since 2022
- Energy sector +1.87% as only bright spot: Exxon +3.4%, Halliburton +4%
- Tech under dual pressure: Microsoft worst quarter since 2008 as AI leadership questioned; memory chip "mini-DeepSeek moment" from resurfaced Turbo Quant paper
- Qualcomm downgraded to Market Perform on memory shortages pressuring smartphone sales, $4B Apple revenue rolling off 2027
- Citi -5% on regional bank acquisition speculation (denied by company)

**Private Credit & Liquidity**

Sector stress emerged as liquidity mismatches exposed vulnerabilities across platforms.

- Oak Tree meeting 8.5% redemptions in full while peers capping at 5%
- $5B+ locked up across various managers as surviving portfolios deteriorate
- BDC equities trading 20% below NAV amid forced selling concerns
- Higher-quality loans being repaid first, leaving managers with deteriorating credit profiles

**Commodities & FX**

Traditional safe haven assets failed to perform defensive roles amid complex macro backdrop.

- Gold selling despite ideal conditions (safe haven + inflation hedge) as overcrowded trades unwind
- Dollar strength sustained as relative haven despite US energy exposure
- VIX closed above 30 for first time since April at 31 handle
- Consumer sentiment hit 3-month low with gasoline prices (~$4/gallon national average) key driver

**Key Risks**

Multiple catalysts threaten further market deterioration beyond current pricing.

- 10,000+ additional US troops under consideration with first Marine unit arriving this weekend
- Weekend headline risk continues pattern of Friday selling
- TSA funding impasse threatens Easter travel disruption
- Oil above $110 for full year would reduce S&P 500 earnings estimates by ~5% (JPMorgan)
- Fertilizer supply orders due in 5-6 days with 20% import dependency
- Second-order inflation effects from aggressive fiscal response to slowdown

**Outlook**

Markets appear to be pricing risk rather than full disruption, with 60/40 portfolios posting worst monthly performance since 2022. Fed Chair Powell's Monday remarks will be critical for policy guidance as central banks navigate between anchoring inflation expectations and avoiding rate hikes into an energy shock.
