DAILY BRIEF:
Netflix abandoned its Warner Brothers bid while Paramount accepted a $31/share offer, clearing media M&A uncertainty. US 10-year yields broke below 4% for the first time since February 2024 despite hotter-than-expected PPI data. Block's decision to cut 50% of its workforce using AI efficiency gains sparked broader concerns about AI-driven labor displacement.

**Fed & Monetary Policy**

The Fed faces a prolonged standoff with the Department of Justice over renovation records, with the inquiry allegedly aimed at pressuring rate policy decisions. Despite January PPI rising 0.5% month-over-month versus 0.3% expected and core PPI accelerating to 3.6% year-over-year from 3.3% prior, Treasury yields fell sharply.

- 10-year Treasury yield: 3.96%, breaking below 4% threshold for first time in nearly a year
- 2-year yield: 340 basis points, front-end rally persisting despite hawkish inflation data
- 30-year mortgage rates: sub-6% for first time since 2022
- Fed officials noted AI may raise the natural rate of unemployment, complicating monetary policy

**Equities & Earnings**

US markets closed February with significant divergence between cap-weighted and equal-weighted indices. The S&P 500 fell 0.9% for the month, its worst performance since April 2024, while the equal-weight S&P hit consecutive record highs.

- S&P 500: -0.4% Friday, -0.9% for February
- Nasdaq 100: -2% month-to-date, entering bear market territory from recent highs
- Russell 2000: -1.7% Friday despite +6% year-to-date gains
- Netflix: +7-8% after abandoning Warner Brothers bid, investors rewarding capital discipline
- Dell: +11-22% on $50 billion AI server revenue guidance through fiscal 2027
- Block: +17-20% after cutting 4,000 jobs (50% of workforce) through AI automation
- Nvidia: -4-5.5%, erasing $260 billion in market value in single session

**Private Credit Stress**

Financial markets experienced broad selling pressure following private credit concerns. Blue Owl halted redemptions on one of its funds, while a UK mortgage lender collapse echoed systemic risks.

- Financials sector: -5% led by KBW Bank Index decline
- Goldman Sachs: -7.5%, Morgan Stanley -6.1% on private credit exposure fears
- Blue Owl: -6%, dragging Apollo and KKR lower
- Investment grade and high yield credit spreads: widest levels since November
- Apollo BDC: cut dividend to $0.31 from $0.38, marked down 3% of portfolio on consumer lending exposure

**International Markets**

Asian equities posted exceptional February performance driven by AI hardware demand and dollar weakness. The PBOC intervened to slow renminbi appreciation ahead of the National People's Congress.

- Korean Kospi: +20% in February, best monthly performance on record
- Shanghai Composite: targeting 10-year high at 4,165 level
- Hang Seng Tech: -23% from October peak, approaching 5,000 psychological support
- PBOC cut FX forward risk reserve to zero, making renminbi shorts cheaper
- Onshore renminbi: 6.8590 after intervention, PBOC targeting stability above 6.90
- Hong Kong property sales: 22-year high trajectory with 1,500 units sold for HK$5 billion

**Geopolitical & Policy Risks**

Iran nuclear negotiations continue with technical discussions scheduled for Vienna, though military deadline approaches. UK political instability intensified following Labour's third-place finish in a previously safe parliamentary seat.

- Iran talks: no breakthrough in Geneva, US demanding full dismantlement of three nuclear sites
- UK Gorton by-election: Labour finished behind Greens and Reform UK
- Anthropic faces Pentagon 5pm ET deadline over AI restrictions for surveillance applications
- China military purge: nine lawmakers removed from NPC in most extensive reshuffling since Mao era

**Commodities**

Oil maintained February gains above $70 despite easing geopolitical tensions. Memory chip prices continue surging, creating supply chain disruptions.

- Brent crude: $71-72, +8% for February on Iran risk premium
- Memory chip prices: triple-digit increases now represent 20-30% of smartphone costs
- Smartphone shipments: IDC forecasts -13% contraction, revised from -1% three months ago

The combination of AI-driven labor concerns, private credit stress, and persistent inflation despite falling yields suggests markets are navigating competing cross-currents as February concludes.
