**US inflation accelerated to 3.8% in April, driving Treasury yields to multi-decade highs and forcing markets to price potential Fed rate hikes by late 2026. President Trump arrives in Beijing today for critical summit talks with Xi Jinping, with Nvidia's Jensen Huang making a last-minute addition to the delegation after boarding Air Force One in Alaska. UK political uncertainty intensified as PM Starmer faces survival challenges ahead of today's King's Speech.**

**Inflation & Central Banks**

The April CPI print of 3.8% year-over-year marked the hottest reading since 2023, with core services inflation rising 0.5% month-over-month. The data triggered a sharp repricing across global rates markets, with the US 30-year yield hitting 5.02% - a multi-decade high - while the 10-year approached 4.50%.

- Markets now price approximately 9 basis points of Fed hikes by December 2026, a dramatic shift from previous cut expectations
- Incoming Fed Chair Kevin Walsh faces immediate pressure with his confirmation vote expected today
- Japan's 20-year JGB yield reached its highest level since 1997 at 3.5%
- UK 30-year gilt yields hit levels not seen since 1998 amid political turmoil
- Bloomberg commodity index significantly outpaces realized inflation, suggesting further upward pressure ahead

**Trump-Xi Summit**

Trump's arrival in Beijing sets the stage for high-stakes trade negotiations, with the surprise inclusion of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang dramatically shifting the agenda toward semiconductor export controls. China enters talks from a position of relative strength following robust economic data.

- China's chip exports surged 100% year-over-year to $31 billion in April, reflecting AI supply chain dominance
- The CNY strengthened to the 6.79 handle ahead of talks, with volatility collapsing and risk reversals turning bullish
- Effective US-China tariff rates have fallen to ~20% from peak levels of 125-140% in April 2025 following Supreme Court rulings
- Morgan Stanley upgraded China's GDP forecast to 4.8% and removed easing expectations on export momentum

**Equity Markets**

Asian markets showed resilience despite the inflation concerns, with tech stocks benefiting from optimism around potential US-China semiconductor détente.

- Korean Kospi swung from -3% to positive territory as retail buyers emerged
- Samsung reversed a 5% initial decline to close higher after the Huang news, despite ongoing labor disputes
- China tech hardware names outperformed on self-sufficiency themes, with optical transceivers and server companies leading
- JD.com gained 3% on earnings beat as food delivery subsidies eased
- NASDAQ futures rose on AI optimism despite broader rate concerns

**Energy & Commodities**

Oil markets consolidated near recent highs as Iranian supply disruptions continued, though prices pulled back slightly on diplomatic hopes.

- Brent crude fell 1.4% to $106 per barrel after three consecutive days of gains
- Iran's Kharg Island export terminal remains largely idle, with storage facilities filling
- Supertanker arrivals in China hit 6-year lows for the next 90 days despite strategic reserve buffers
- The Strait of Hormuz blockade persists, with two vessels attempting passage today

**Corporate Developments**

Earnings results highlighted divergent trends across sectors, with AI-related capex driving significant spending increases.

- Top 5 US hyperscalers are spending $100 billion on AI capex in 2025, with 40% flowing to Asian supply chains
- Alibaba's operating expenses rose 35% versus 4% revenue growth, signaling margin pressure from AI investments
- Samsung faces potential production disruptions with labor unions threatening an 18-day strike over profit-sharing demands
- Memory chip supply constraints are deepening, with HBM4 shortages already acute

**UK Political Crisis**

Prime Minister Starmer's position remained precarious ahead of today's King's Speech, with Health Secretary Streeting's potential resignation posing immediate risks.

- Andy Burnham emerged as a potential replacement candidate, though his past comments about not being "in hock to bond markets" raise fiscal concerns
- The political uncertainty contributed to gilt selloff, with 30-year yields at 1998 levels
- GBP traded at $1.35, pressured by the ongoing political chaos

**Outlook**

The combination of persistent inflation, rising yields, and geopolitical negotiations creates a complex environment for markets. Central banks globally face credibility challenges as bond markets question their ability to control inflation pressures. The Trump-Xi summit outcome could significantly impact tech sector dynamics and broader trade relations, while energy markets remain volatile amid ongoing Middle East tensions.
