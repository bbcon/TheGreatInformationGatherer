DAILY BRIEF:
Markets remained relatively calm despite overnight US-Iran military exchanges in the Strait of Hormuz, with oil rising modestly and equity futures showing resilience. A federal court struck down Trump's 10% global tariffs as unlawful, dealing a blow to the administration's trade agenda ahead of next week's planned Trump-Xi summit. April payrolls data today faces lowered expectations, with some forecasters predicting the first negative print in months.

**Geopolitical & Energy**

US forces engaged Iranian military targets after attacks on three destroyers in the Strait of Hormuz, though Trump characterized the response as a "love tap" and maintained the ceasefire remains intact. A 48-hour deadline for Iran's response to US proposals is approaching.

- Brent crude rose to $101/barrel, up roughly 1% on the session
- WTI traded below $95/barrel despite the tensions
- US gasoline prices hit $4.50/gallon for the first time since 2022
- Trump warned of a "big glow" if the ceasefire breaks, but markets appear sanguine about escalation risks

**US Data & Fed Policy**

April nonfarm payrolls are expected to show continued labor market softening, with consensus at +65k but some forecasters significantly more bearish.

- City projects -15k, which would mark the first negative print in months
- Unemployment rate expected around 4.3%
- Fed dissent emerged yesterday, with Boston Fed's Collins preferring statement revision to remove language "associated with presumption next move is cut" though she supported the hold decision
- SF Fed's Daly downplayed statement wording debate, saying "most people don't read our statement" and actions matter more than communication
- Markets now price equal probability of cuts versus hikes, with "higher for longer" largely priced in

**China Markets & Policy**

Chinese equities posted their best week of 2026 despite Friday's pullback, with Goldman upgrading targets on hardware momentum and attractive valuations.

- MSCI China up approximately 3% for the week before Friday's -1% decline
- Shanghai Composite came within 2 points of a fresh 10-year high
- Goldman raised CSI 300 target to 5,300 from 5,000, preferring A-shares over H-shares
- CNY broke through 6.80 for the first time in months, though PBOC fixing at 6.852 suggests resistance to further appreciation
- China CPI accelerated to 3.3% in March from 2.4%, driven by energy costs
- PBoC added 8.1 tons of gold in April, the most in over a year and 18th consecutive month of purchases

**Trade & Legal Challenges**

Trump's trade agenda faced a significant setback as a federal court ruled the 10% global tariffs unlawful, creating precedent for broader challenges.

- The ruling undermines administration leverage in ongoing China and EU negotiations  
- Total potential refunds could reach $200 billion if court decisions broaden
- Trump-Xi summit remains scheduled for next week despite Chinese concerns over the unresolved Iran situation
- Reported delegation may include Jensen Huang (Nvidia), plus executives from Qualcomm, Apple, Boeing, Blackstone, and Citi

**UK Politics**

Early local election results showed heavy Labour losses, raising questions about PM Starmer's position despite gilt market stability.

- Labour lost approximately 50% of defended seats to Reform UK
- Potential losses projected at 1,500-1,850 total seats
- UK 10-year gilt yields near 5%, hitting 28-year highs on fiscal concerns
- GBP/USD held steady at 1.3570, supported by broad dollar weakness offsetting political uncertainty

**Asian Markets**

Regional markets reflected mixed sentiment as geopolitical tensions competed with AI infrastructure momentum.

- Korea's current account surged to $37 billion, driven by AI and memory chip exports
- Philippines inflation spiked above 7% in April, the fastest since post-COVID surge
- Singapore property developers fell 1.75% on new executive condo restrictions requiring 10-year hold periods
- Taiwan and Korea semiconductor names remained resilient, with markets viewing next week's Nvidia earnings as a bigger catalyst than the Trump-Xi summit

**Corporate & Credit**

Credit markets showed selective stress, particularly in private markets and leveraged names.

- SoftBank CDS widened on leverage concerns, with S&P moving the outlook to negative
- Private credit faces transparency questions after Gundlach highlighted a fund marked down from 100 to 81 overnight
- Big Tech companies reported free cash flow at decade lows of $4 billion quarterly versus a $45 billion average
- Commerce Bank beat Q1 expectations with €913 million net income and raised full-year profit outlook to €3.4 billion

The combination of geopolitical uncertainty, legal challenges to trade policy, and mixed economic signals suggests continued market volatility ahead of key data releases and next week's diplomatic meetings.
