DAILY BRIEF:
Oil markets whipsawed as Trump's claims of "productive" Iran negotiations were denied by Tehran, with Brent recovering to $103/barrel after briefly touching sub-$100 levels. US and European equity futures turned negative despite early Asian gains as doubts mounted over genuine deescalation prospects. Private credit stress emerged with Apollo capping retail fund redemptions at 5% while Moody's downgraded a KKR fund to junk status.

**Oil & Energy Complex**

The energy market experienced extreme volatility as conflicting diplomatic signals overshadowed fundamental supply tightness. Trump's postponement of his Iran energy strike deadline to Friday sparked an initial 10% crude selloff in minutes, but prices recovered as Iranian officials denied any negotiations exist.

- Brent crude settled at $103/barrel, still ~$30 above pre-conflict levels
- WTI crude similarly elevated with refined products outpacing crude gains
- European jet fuel hit $220/barrel, approaching crisis levels
- 5 million barrels per day of petroleum products remain stuck in the closed Strait of Hormuz
- Refining margins reached highest levels since the Ukraine war as Gulf facilities suffered damage requiring "several months" to repair
- Qatar LNG production cuts expected even if the strait reopens

Asian energy security concerns intensified with the Philippines down to 40 days of oil reserves as of March 20. Korea accelerated renewable energy storage installations to reduce LNG dependency.

**Equities & Risk Assets**

Asian markets initially rallied on deescalation hopes but momentum stalled as US and European futures turned negative. The risk-off sentiment returned as diplomatic uncertainty persisted.

- Hang Seng gained 1.5% initially before fading to +0.9%
- HSI Tech rose 1.5% led by Alibaba, Meituan, and Tencent
- CSI 300 opened 1% higher but turned flat by close
- Nikkei and Topix held gains at +0.7% and +1.5% respectively
- US and European equity futures declined despite the Asian strength

Gold's traditional safe-haven role continued to break down, with the metal declining for a 10th consecutive session despite geopolitical tensions. Options positioning showed the steepest inverted skew in a year as put buying surged.

**Central Bank Positioning**

Major central banks maintained cautious stances following last week's coordinated rate holds, with policymakers split between addressing supply-driven inflation and supporting growth.

- Fed's Meyer emphasized it's "too early to alter policy" based on "short-term headlines"  
- ECB's Vujcic warned of stagflation risks, calling for "agility and vigilance"
- BoE cut expectations evaporated as inflation concerns override growth worries
- BOJ May hike expectations strengthened despite softer CPI data

Goldman Sachs adjusted emerging market rate forecasts, now pricing hikes for India while pushing back Indonesia rate cuts as stagflation fears mounted globally.

**Credit Markets & Private Markets**

Signs of stress emerged in private credit markets, raising concerns about broader liquidity conditions in less-regulated segments.

- Apollo capped redemptions at 5% for its retail credit fund amid 11.2% withdrawal rates
- Moody's downgraded a KKR/Future Standard private credit fund to junk (BA1) status
- One-third of US insurance assets are allocated to private credit, creating interconnected risks regulators haven't fully addressed
- Software companies comprising 25-30% of private credit face AI disruption with loans at 2-3x current valuations

**Currency & Fixed Income**

The dollar resumed its strengthening trend as diplomatic uncertainty persisted, with the DXY gaining 0.4%. EM carry trades suffered their worst month since 2022 as global volatility spiked.

- USD/CNH traded below 690 but dollar strength weighed on broader EM currencies
- European government bonds benefited from flight-to-quality flows
- Credit spreads widened modestly as private market stress spilled over

**Economic Data**

February European car sales rose 1.7% year-over-year despite France's 15% decline, supported by EV subsidies in Germany, Spain, and Italy. Japan's CPI showed the slowest pace in four years, weaker than expected due to utility subsidies.

**Outlook**

The lack of credible Iranian off-ramps suggests the Strait of Hormuz closure may persist, with Tehran holding significant leverage. Gulf states are hardening rhetoric, with Saudi Arabia's patience described as "not unlimited" and the UAE labeling strait weaponization as "economic terror." The Pentagon is reportedly considering deployment of 3,000 airborne troops with an 18-hour timeline, adding to 2,500 ground troops already en route.

Key catalysts include today's PMI releases, with European data expected to show softening while US figures remain critical for growth trajectory assessment. Food security concerns are mounting as fertilizer and diesel shortages threaten agricultural yields 6-12 months forward.
