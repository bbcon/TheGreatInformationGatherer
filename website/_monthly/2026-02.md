February 2026 witnessed significant shifts across monetary policy expectations, technology sector leadership, and geopolitical risk as markets navigated competing forces of economic strength and emerging structural disruptions. The S&P 500 reached fresh records early in the month before declining toward 5,800 as AI infrastructure concerns intensified, while precious metals experienced historic volatility amid Fed policy uncertainty.

**Fed Policy Recalibration Under Warsh**

Kevin Warsh's nomination as Fed Chair early in the month fundamentally altered monetary policy expectations, triggering a reassessment across asset classes that persisted throughout February.

- Warsh's hawkish credentials, including historical opposition to QE and preference for smaller Fed balance sheets, ended speculation about more dovish alternatives
- Initial market reaction was swift: precious metals collapsed 8-10% on the nomination announcement as debasement trades unwound violently
- Treasury curve steepened as markets priced reduced accommodation, with rate cut expectations pushed from June to September
- Mid-month FOMC minutes shocked consensus by showing officials discussing potential rate hikes rather than cuts, citing AI productivity gains as justification for higher neutral rates
- Several Fed officials noted AI productivity improvements were "undeniable" but duration remained uncertain, signaling structural policy thinking shifts
- By month's end, BNP Paribas projected zero rate cuts in 2026, contrasting sharply with early February pricing of two cuts

Economic data presented a fundamental disconnect with dovish market expectations throughout the month.

- December retail sales disappointed broadly, falling flat versus expansion expectations across 8 of 13 categories
- January jobs data doubled payroll expectations at 130,000 versus 65,000 consensus, pushing unemployment to 4.3%
- January CPI met expectations at 2.5% core year-over-year, with tariff effects visible in appliances (+1.3%) and computers (+3.1%)
- GDP nowcast remained above 3.6% with unemployment stabilized, creating challenging conditions for the Fed's dual mandate

**Historic Precious Metals Volatility**

Gold and silver experienced their most violent swings in decades as speculative positioning around Fed independence and currency debasement reached extremes before unwinding dramatically.

- Gold breached $5,000 early in the month, reaching $5,514 mid-month before suffering its worst single-day decline since 1983
- The 8% Friday collapse in the first week alone wiped out parabolic Q4 2025 gains, with prices falling toward the $2,840-$4,494 range
- Silver's moves were even more extreme, hitting $120 mid-month before plunging 29% in a single session to $80 - the worst drop since 1950
- Ten-day volatility reached highest levels in decades for gold, while silver volatility hit 45-year highs
- CME raised margins twice during violent selloffs, accelerating forced liquidation across leveraged positions
- Chinese retail investors faced heavy losses with ETFs halted and physical gold queues forming in Hong Kong
- The gold/silver ratio normalized to ~60 from extreme sub-50 levels, suggesting structural rebalancing after speculative excess

The extreme moves highlighted how concentrated positioning in debasement trades had become, with coordinated Fed-BOJ intervention early in the month initially supporting the narrative before Warsh's nomination served as a catalyst for widespread unwinding.

**AI Infrastructure Investment Crisis**

Technology earnings revealed a critical fault line in artificial intelligence investing as markets began differentiating between companies showing clear revenue returns versus those burning capital without evident monetization paths.

The divergence became apparent across major technology names throughout the month.

- ASML delivered stunning Q4 bookings of €13.2 billion versus €6.85 billion expected, nearly doubling estimates and validating semiconductor demand
- Meta gained 8-10% despite announcing $135 billion capex (87% increase) after Q1 revenue guidance crushed estimates
- Microsoft fell 6-10% as Azure growth of 38% disappointed against 66% capex increase to $38 billion
- Alphabet triggered sector-wide concerns with $185 billion 2026 capex guidance versus $120 billion consensus
- Amazon compounded fears with $200 billion AI spending plan, causing an 11% after-hours plunge despite 24% AWS growth
- Combined hyperscaler capex reached $650 billion annual run rate, raising fundamental questions about return timelines

Memory shortages provided a bright spot amid infrastructure concerns.

- DRAM prices surged 120% year-over-year with NAND up 90%, creating supply bottlenecks
- TSMC January sales jumped 37% year-over-year to record levels, validating chip demand
- Applied Materials gained 11% after strong earnings confirmed equipment demand remains robust

Software sector devastation spread beyond initial AI displacement fears to broader disruption concerns.

- Software ETF posted five consecutive weekly declines, entering bear market territory with IGV down 30% from September highs
- Nine worst S&P performers year-to-date were SaaS companies, each declining 25%+
- Wealth management firms suffered as AI tax planning tools threatened traditional models
- The "SaaS apocalypse" narrative gained credibility as AI capabilities reached commercial viability thresholds

**Dollar Weakness and Coordinated Intervention**

The month began with unprecedented Fed-BOJ coordination that drove the dollar to four-year lows, though this trend partially reversed as hawkish Fed expectations reemerged.

- Joint currency intervention signals between the Federal Reserve and Bank of Japan marked the first such coordination since 2002
- USD/JPY plunged from 159 to 152 following coordinated rate checks, with the yen strengthening 2% within minutes
- Trump's explicit endorsement of dollar weakness accelerated the decline, with DXY falling 1.3% in a single session to four-year lows
- EUR/USD broke through 1.20 resistance to touch 2021 highs near 1.22 as dollar weakness broadened
- Asian currencies rallied broadly: Korean won gained 1.6%, Singapore dollar reached strongest levels since 2014

However, the intervention's effectiveness waned as Fed policy expectations shifted.

- Warsh's nomination partially reversed dollar weakness, with the greenback gaining 0.75% as debasement trades unwound
- Japan's election victory later in the month provided additional yen support as policy uncertainty increased
- Prime Minister Takaichi's landslide delivered the largest mandate since WWII, fundamentally altering Japan's policy landscape
- BOJ normalization expectations accelerated with April hike probability jumping to 80% and rate expectations rising to 100bp from 75bp

**Geopolitical Risk Premium Emergence**

Late-month developments in Iran created the most significant geopolitical risk premium since the Ukraine invasion as Trump administration strategy evolved from diplomacy to military ultimatum.

- Iran-US nuclear talks began mid-month in Geneva with initial flexibility signals but collapsed as positions hardened
- Trump issued a 10-15 day military action timeline in the final week, deploying the largest Middle East military buildup since the Iraq War
- Two carrier strike groups positioned in the region as Gulf states warned against using their territory for strikes
- Brent crude surged from $68 to $72 per barrel by month's end, with analysts estimating a $5 geopolitical premium
- Defense stocks outperformed in Japan and Korea as regional allies prepared for potential spillover effects

**Looking Ahead**

March opens with multiple catalysts that could extend February's themes or create new market dynamics. The Iran military ultimatum timeline approaches mid-month, potentially reshaping geopolitical risk premiums significantly. Fed policy remains in flux as Warsh's confirmation process begins amid continued economic strength that challenges dovish market positioning. The AI infrastructure investment cycle faces its first major test as companies must demonstrate returns on unprecedented capex commitments, while precious metals markets work through speculative excess built around debasement themes that may prove premature.

The month highlighted growing complexity in policy coordination as currency intervention, Fed independence, and international tensions created competing cross-currents that markets struggled to reconcile into coherent narratives.
