February marked a month of significant policy transitions and market recalibrations as Trump's dollar weakness campaign collided with Kevin Warsh's hawkish Fed nomination, while AI infrastructure spending reached unsustainable levels that triggered broad technology sector repricing. Geopolitical tensions escalated sharply by month-end as Iran nuclear diplomacy collapsed into military ultimatum.

**Dollar Debasement Campaign and Policy Contradictions**

The Trump administration's explicit endorsement of dollar weakness created unprecedented coordination with international central banks early in the month, before being complicated by subsequent Fed Chair nomination dynamics.

- USD/JPY plunged from 159 to 152 in the first week following coordinated Fed-BOJ intervention signals, marking the first joint currency action since 2002
- DXY fell to four-year lows mid-month as Trump stated he was "not concerned" about dollar weakness, with EUR/USD breaking through 1.20 to touch 2021 highs near 1.22
- Asian currencies rallied broadly with the Korean won gaining 1.6% and Singapore dollar reaching strongest levels since 2014
- The coordination represented a potential alternative to tariff escalation, though effectiveness proved temporary

Kevin Warsh's nomination as Fed Chair on February 6 created logical tensions with the dollar weakness strategy, partially reversing debasement trades.

- The dollar gained 0.75% on nomination day as markets repriced away from more dovish alternatives like BlackRock's Rick Rieder
- Warsh's historical opposition to QE and preference for "gradual and predictable" balance sheet reduction signaled major policy shifts
- FOMC minutes later revealed officials discussing potential rate hikes, with several citing AI productivity gains as justification for a higher neutral rate
- By month-end, BNP Paribas projected zero rate cuts in 2026, contrasting sharply with continued market pricing of 2-3 cuts

**Historic Precious Metals Volatility**

Gold and silver experienced both parabolic gains and violent reversals as markets grappled with Fed independence concerns and currency debasement positioning.

- Gold breached $5,000 early February, reaching $5,514 by the first week before suffering an 8% single-day collapse - the worst decline since 1983
- Silver hit $120 mid-month before plunging 29% in one session to $80, marking the worst drop since 1950
- The moves reflected massive speculative positioning built around Fed independence concerns, with ten-day volatility reaching highest levels in decades for gold
- CME raised margins twice during the selloff period, accelerating forced liquidation
- Chinese retail investors faced heavy losses with ETFs halted due to premiums and physical gold queues forming in Hong Kong
- Gold maintained elevation above $5,000 through month-end on persistent safe-haven demand amid Iran tensions

**AI Infrastructure Spending Crisis**

Technology earnings revealed an unsustainable trajectory in AI capital expenditure, triggering fundamental questions about return timelines and monetization across the sector.

- Combined hyperscaler capex reached $650 billion annual run rate, with Alphabet's $185 billion 2026 guidance and Amazon's $200 billion AI spending plan representing 60% year-over-year increases
- The software sector entered bear market territory with the IGV ETF down 30% from September highs, while nine of the worst S&P performers year-to-date were SaaS companies declining 25%+
- Memory chip shortages intensified dramatically, with DRAM prices rising 600% and NAND up 90%, creating infrastructure bottlenecks
- AI disruption fears metastasized beyond software into wealth management, with Charles Schwab falling 7.4% and Raymond James declining 8.8% on AI tax planning tools

Despite infrastructure concerns, underlying demand metrics remained robust.

- TSMC January sales surged 37% year-over-year to record levels
- ASML delivered stunning Q4 results with bookings of €13.2 billion versus €6.85 billion expected
- Applied Materials gained 11% after strong earnings validating equipment demand
- The divergence suggested markets were moving beyond blanket AI enthusiasm toward rigorous assessment of capital allocation efficiency

**Japan's Political Revolution**

Prime Minister Takaichi's landslide election victory in mid-February delivered the largest mandate since WWII, fundamentally altering Japan's policy landscape and currency dynamics.

- The LDP secured a two-thirds supermajority sufficient to override upper house vetoes on constitutional changes
- Proposed sales tax suspension on food worth ¥5 trillion ($31B annually) despite claims of no additional JGB issuance needed
- The Nikkei surged 4% on election results to record highs, with USD/JPY falling sharply from 157 to 152 by month-end
- April BOJ hike probability jumped to 80% from previous June expectations, with rate expectations rising to 100bp from 75bp as officials suggested current rates may be "too accommodative"
- 40-year JGB yields dropped 10bp to 3.625% on policy uncertainty, while MOF intervention warnings intensified

**Geopolitical Escalation and Risk Premium**

The month concluded with the most significant geopolitical risk escalation since the Ukraine invasion as Trump-Iran diplomacy collapsed into military ultimatum.

- Iran-US nuclear talks began promisingly in Geneva mid-month with initial flexibility signals, but collapsed by February 21 when Trump issued a 10-15 day military action timeline
- The administration deployed the largest Middle East military buildup since the Iraq War, positioning two carrier strike groups in the region
- Brent crude surged from $68 to $72 per barrel by month-end, with analysts estimating a $5 geopolitical premium now embedded
- Gulf states including UAE and Saudi Arabia warned against using their territory for potential strikes, complicating military options
- Defense stocks outperformed in Japan and Korea as regional allies prepared for potential spillover effects

**Private Credit Stress Emergence**

Blue Owl's suspension of withdrawals from a $1.5 billion retail private credit fund on February 21 reignited liquidity concerns across the alternative asset sector.

- The firm suspended withdrawals indefinitely while selling $1.4 billion in loans near par value despite marking them at a 75% NAV discount
- The development exposed potential duration vulnerabilities and liquidity mismatches in retail-accessible private credit vehicles
- Alternative asset managers including Apollo, KKR, and Blackstone fell over 6% on contagion fears
- Software sector exposure remained a particular concern given ongoing AI disruption across the $1.8 trillion private credit market

**Looking Ahead**

March opens with multiple critical inflection points converging. The Iran military ultimatum timeline approaches mid-month with oil markets pricing increasing risk premium. The Supreme Court ruling on Trump's tariff authority could fundamentally alter trade policy implementation. Meanwhile, the disconnect between robust US economic fundamentals and persistent dovish Fed pricing appears increasingly unsustainable, particularly given Warsh's hawkish credentials and FOMC officials' newfound focus on AI productivity gains potentially raising the neutral rate. The emerging private credit stress adds another layer of complexity to financial stability considerations as the Fed navigates competing political pressures and economic realities.
