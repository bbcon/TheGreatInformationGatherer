**US markets extended their eighth consecutive weekly gain despite elevated oil prices and shifting Fed policy stance, as incoming Chair Worsh prepares to inherit the most challenging monetary environment since 1987. Asian equities led global gains on AI infrastructure momentum while emerging market central banks pivoted toward tightening as inflation broadens beyond energy sectors.**

**Fed Chair Transition & Yield Surge**

Markets navigated a critical Fed leadership transition as Kevin Worsh's swearing-in ceremony on Friday marked the beginning of a new monetary era amid the highest yields for an incoming chair since the Greenspan period.

- 30-year Treasury yields reached 5.17% on Wednesday, triggering $15 billion in block trades at 3x normal volume before moderating to 4.55% on the 10-year by Friday
- Treasury volatility surged 20% over the first half of the week as positioning panic gripped fixed income markets
- Markets completed a dramatic repricing, now fully expecting a Fed rate hike by January 2026, reversing previous easing expectations through March 2027
- FOMC minutes revealed internal division with 9 of 12 voting members maintaining an easing bias at the May meeting, effectively deferring the policy pivot to Worsh
- Equity risk premium turned negative mid-week with S&P earnings yield falling below Treasury yields, pushing fair value estimates down to 5,700
- Worsh inherits a Fed facing broad inflation pressures, with 45% of CPI components running at 5%+ annualized rates beyond just energy

**Asian Markets & AI Infrastructure Boom**

Regional equities demonstrated resilience throughout the week, culminating in strong Friday gains driven by accelerating AI infrastructure investment and Middle East peace speculation.

- Korea's KOSPI extended its remarkable run with a 2% Friday gain, pushing year-to-date performance to 90% with 17 of 21 weeks positive
- Wednesday saw initial declines with MSCI Asia Pacific falling 1.1% and Korean markets dropping 1.7% on Samsung strike concerns, before reversing course
- Lenovo surged 14% on Friday to 26-year highs as AI infrastructure revenue jumped 84% year-over-year to 38% of total revenue
- Taiwan semiconductor names rallied on agentic AI themes, though TSMC's 40%+ index weight created technical constraints for fund additions  
- Chinese stocks showed relative outperformance mid-week, with domestic chip stocks rallying for three consecutive days
- Hyperscaler capex expectations doubled to $800 billion for 2026 from $375 billion previously, driving infrastructure stock momentum

**Emerging Market Central Bank Pivot**

Central banks across emerging Asia executed a coordinated shift toward monetary tightening as inflation pressures intensified beyond commodity-driven shocks.

- Bank Indonesia delivered a 50bp rate hike earlier in the week, signaling broader regional policy recalibration
- Philippines emerged as a key focus by Friday, with the central bank considering an emergency rate hike amid warnings of falling "behind the curve"
- Philippine peso strengthened on emergency hike speculation, bucking broader EM currency weakness
- Indonesia, India, and Philippines currencies had faced pressure mid-week from the yield surge and oil shock dynamics
- Markets now price 57 basis points of Bank of England rate hikes versus Bloomberg Economics' one-hike base case
- Japan provided a contrasting narrative with core inflation falling to 1.4%, a four-year low, complicating the Bank of Japan's rate hike trajectory

**Energy Markets & Geopolitical Tensions**

Oil markets remained structurally elevated throughout the week despite periodic peace negotiation optimism, as supply disruption concerns overshadowed diplomatic developments.

- Brent crude fluctuated between $104-110, with Wednesday seeing a 2% decline to $102.44 for WTI July contracts while December contracts held at record levels
- The Strait of Hormuz remained closed with 88 commercial vessels turned back by Friday
- Iran's proposal for a permanent tolling system was rejected by the US as "unacceptable," complicating resolution prospects
- Closure probability through June fell to 30% by Wednesday, the lowest since the conflict began, before uncertainty returned
- Trump maintained pressure with Friday-Sunday deadline threats for renewed strikes, while NATO considered intervention by early July with 800 vessels and 20,000 seafarers stranded
- US gasoline prices approached $5/gallon nationally with potential $6+ spikes if the closure extends

**Technology Sector Dynamics**

The semiconductor and broader technology sector navigated multiple crosscurrents, from labor disruptions to earnings expectations and evolving AI research priorities.

- Nvidia's Wednesday after-hours earnings loomed large with Q1 revenue expected up 80% year-over-year to $79 billion, with options pricing suggesting a 5-12% post-earnings move
- Samsung's confirmed general strike on Thursday threatened global memory chip supply, with fabrication plants potentially requiring six months to restore full production if taken offline
- AI research focus began shifting from language models to physical applications, with world models for robotics and drug discovery timelines shortened to 1-3 years for B2B applications
- Quantum computing stocks rallied 20% Friday following Trump administration investment announcements
- Market positioning showed "full-on FOMO right tail chase" according to RBC, with historic call buying volumes creating skew inversion patterns
