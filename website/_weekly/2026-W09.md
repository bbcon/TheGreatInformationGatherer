The Supreme Court's rejection of Trump's tariff framework dominated market dynamics this week, forcing emergency trade policy implementation while spurring a global rotation toward Asian equities and away from US tech concentration. Private credit stress emerged as a systemic concern by Friday, while AI infrastructure demand showed resilience despite growing labor displacement fears.

**Trade Policy Upheaval Reshapes Global Markets**

The Supreme Court's Monday ruling striking down Trump's reciprocal tariff system created immediate policy chaos and market realignments. The administration's pivot to emergency powers produced lower effective tariff rates globally, benefiting Asian exporters while undermining dollar strength.

- Trump implemented 10% global baseline tariffs by Tuesday, down from the initially threatened 15% rate, with a 150-day Congressional renewal deadline
- China's effective tariff rate fell to 24% from 31%, triggering sustained rallies in Chinese equities toward 10-year highs
- $170 billion in collected duties now face potential refund litigation, complicating Treasury issuance plans
- FedEx became the first major corporation to sue over refund processes on Tuesday
- Asian markets capitalized on the relief, with Korea's KOSPI posting its best monthly performance on record at +20% for February

The policy uncertainty created clear winners and losers across regions. Hong Kong property extended its longest rally since 2003, while European equities outperformed US markets for the fifth consecutive month. The dollar weakened broadly as capital flowed toward beneficiary economies, with the Chinese yuan reaching 35-month highs despite PBOC intervention signals.

**AI Infrastructure Demand Persists Amid Labor Displacement Concerns**

Technology earnings revealed a complex narrative of continued AI infrastructure investment alongside growing concerns about workforce displacement. TSMC's 37% January sales surge and Nvidia's exceptional results validated ongoing demand, even as markets showed exhaustion with AI concentration plays.

- Nvidia delivered Q4 revenue of $68 billion (up 73% year-over-year) and Q1 guidance of $78 billion, yet the stock fell 4-5% on Thursday
- Memory supply constraints emerged as a critical bottleneck, with DRAM costs surging 100% quarter-over-quarter according to HP
- Samsung and SK Hynix reportedly have memory capacity booked through 2027-28, supporting Korean market outperformance
- Block's Friday announcement of cutting 4,000 jobs (50% of workforce) through AI automation sparked +17% stock gains but broader labor concerns
- Smartphone shipments now forecast to contract 13% due to memory shortages, revised from -1% three months prior

The divergence between infrastructure demand and application layer concerns became evident in sector performance. Semiconductors fell 3.5% on Thursday despite strong earnings, while software stocks remained under pressure from AI displacement fears. The MAG7 now trades at only a 20% premium to the S&P 500, down from a 40% average and the smallest premium in seven years.

**Private Credit Stress Triggers Financial Sector Selloff**

Friday's developments revealed emerging stress in private credit markets, sparking broad-based financial sector weakness and raising systemic risk concerns. Blue Owl's redemption halt and broader forced selling pressured public credit markets.

- Blue Owl halted redemptions on one fund while marking down 3% of its portfolio on consumer lending exposure
- Goldman Sachs fell 7.5% and Morgan Stanley declined 6.1% on private credit exposure fears
- Apollo BDC cut its dividend to $0.31 from $0.38, signaling deteriorating credit conditions
- Investment grade and high yield credit spreads reached their widest levels since November
- Private credit funds attempted unusual secondary market sales, raising liquidity concerns

The stress emerged despite relatively benign economic data, suggesting structural vulnerabilities in the $4+ trillion private credit market. UBS increased worst-case default forecasts to 15% from 13% on AI disruption risks, while the forced selling dynamic pressured public credit markets and broader financials.

**Central Bank Divergence Accelerates Currency Realignment**

Central banks across major economies signaled increasingly divergent policy paths, driving significant currency movements and reshaping relative valuations. The Fed faced political pressure while maintaining hawkish positioning, even as Treasury yields paradoxically fell below key technical levels.

- US 10-year Treasury yields broke below 4% on Friday for the first time since February 2024, despite January PPI rising 0.5% versus 0.3% expected
- Japan's PM Ishiba nominated two dovish BOJ board members on Wednesday, weakening the yen to 155.90 and steepening the JGB curve
- Australia's inflation beat shifted RBA expectations toward near-certain rate hikes, strengthening the Australian dollar
- The PBOC intervened to slow renminbi appreciation, cutting FX forward risk reserves to zero on Friday
- Fed officials increasingly coalesced around an extended pause, with core PCE at 3% well above the 2% target

The currency realignment reflected both policy divergence and trade flow shifts. Asian currencies strengthened broadly as reduced US tariff rates improved terms of trade, while dollar weakness accelerated capital flows into previously overlooked markets.

**Market Structure Shift Toward International Diversification**

February concluded with clear evidence of a structural rotation away from US tech concentration toward international markets and cyclical sectors. The equal-weight S&P 500 hit consecutive record highs while the cap-weighted index posted its worst month since April 2024.

- MSCI World ex-US outperformed the S&P 500 by 11 percentage points over four months
- The Russell 2000 gained 6% year-to-date despite Friday's 1.7% decline
- Korean markets became the world's 9th largest by capitalization as the KOSPI crossed 6,000
- Taiwan saw its largest foreign inflows in 20 years amid semiconductor strength

**Week Ahead**

Key events include potential Iran military action with the administration's deadline approaching, China's National People's Congress for policy guidance, and continued fallout from private credit stress. The 150-day tariff deadline creates a policy cliff requiring Congressional action, while memory supply constraints may limit AI infrastructure buildout pace in coming quarters.
