**Macro Weekly Brief: June 8-14, 2026**

Geopolitical tensions dominated early week trading before peace deal momentum shifted sentiment dramatically by Friday. Markets navigated Fed policy repricing following strong payrolls data while Asian equities experienced extreme volatility driven by leveraged products. The ECB delivered its first hike in three years as inflation broadened beyond energy, setting up divergent central bank paths.

**Middle East De-escalation Drives Energy Reversal**

The week began with escalating Israel-Iran tensions pushing oil markets to crisis levels before peace negotiations gained concrete momentum by Friday. The rapid shift from conflict to potential resolution created one of the most dramatic energy reversals in recent memory.

- Monday saw Brent crude surge 4.5% to $97/barrel following Israeli strikes on Iran's Mashad petrochemical complex
- Strait of Hormuz closure reduced Saudi production capacity from 10M to 7M barrels per day
- By Friday, peace deal optimism drove Brent down 4.5% to $86 as President Trump declared "the war is over"
- Semi-official Iranian media reported a 14-point draft agreement including oil sanctions relief and Strait reopening within 30 days
- December Brent contracts consistently traded below $90 throughout the week, indicating markets viewed the geopolitical premium as temporary
- Goldman Sachs cut 2027 oil forecasts by $5 to the low-$80s range on Friday

The energy complex selloff accelerated Friday on de-escalation hopes, though execution risks remain with Israel-Lebanon tensions at 25-year highs and formal Iranian confirmation still pending. European gas futures fell 7% Friday on potential supply normalization expectations.

**Asian Equity Volatility Exposes Structural Fragilities**

Korean and broader Asian markets experienced extreme swings that revealed concerning structural dynamics around leveraged products and retail positioning. The week showcased how technical factors can amplify fundamental moves in increasingly concentrated markets.

- Monday's rout saw Korea's KOSPI trigger circuit breakers on an 8% decline before recovering to -7%
- Taiwan fell 4.5%, while China's CSI 300 outperformed at just -1.5% due to defensive positioning
- Tuesday brought sharp reversals with KOSPI surging 8% and SK Hynix jumping 14% on AI demand optimism
- By Friday, KOSPI gained another 7-8%, pushing year-to-date returns near 90%
- Leveraged ETF flows drove 60-70% of SK Hynix trading volume, highlighting technical amplification
- Korean market's VIX equivalent hit 90 despite strong performance, signaling stress in market structure

Single-stock Korea ETFs posted double-digit losses Monday as forced options selling amplified downside, while foreign investor flows proved highly volatile throughout the week. The concentration in AI-related names created feedback loops between sentiment and positioning.

**Fed Policy Expectations Shift Amid Inflation Concerns**

Friday's payrolls blowout fundamentally altered Fed expectations early in the week before officials pushed back on aggressive hike pricing. The disconnect between market pricing and Fed communications highlighted uncertainty over the central bank's reaction function.

- Markets initially priced 50-100 basis points of hikes by year-end following strong employment data
- 2-year Treasury yields rose 12 basis points to around 5.0% by Monday
- Wednesday's CPI release carried heightened significance with consensus at 4.2% headline
- 30-year yields hit the 5.0% handle early week, though the curve bear-flattened as front-end rates led the move higher
- By Friday, Fed expectations shifted back toward potential cuts as officials emphasized policy was already restrictive
- Real yields drove much of the move higher, suggesting Fed inflation credibility remained intact

The 2s10s spread compressed to 38 basis points as hike expectations dominated early week, while the 30-year remained "stuck" around 5.02-5.03% reflecting persistent deficit concerns.

**Central Bank Divergence Emerges**

The ECB delivered its first rate hike in three years while other central banks showed varying degrees of hawkishness, setting up potential policy divergence across major economies. Inflation broadening beyond energy drove the European shift.

- ECB raised rates 25 basis points to 2.25% deposit rate Thursday
- Services inflation remained "stubborn at 3.5%" according to ECB officials
- Bundesbank's Nagel indicated July hike likely if necessary
- Markets now price 50 basis points of additional ECB tightening this year
- Bank Indonesia surprised with 25 basis points to 5.5% to defend the rupiah
- Bank of Korea's Shin warned rates "need to be raised before too late"

The Bank of Japan may halt quarterly bond purchase reductions according to media reports, with potential rate increases delayed until October. This created a complex global policy landscape with the Fed potentially pausing while Europe and select emerging markets tighten.

**SpaceX IPO Tests Market Appetite**

SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history at $75 billion, though governance concerns emerged over unprecedented founder control structures. The deal's success occurred amid broader questions about market capacity for mega-offerings.

- IPO attracted over $10 billion in institutional orders with 4x oversubscription
- Retail orders exceeded $100 billion for just 20% allocation
- Musk retained over 1 billion shares with 10x voting power and lifetime CEO appointment
- Governance structure raised institutional concerns but didn't deter demand
- OpenAI filed confidentially for its own offering while Anthropic remains in pipeline
- Oracle disappointed on capex guidance, with analysts warning of potential "big selloff" after upcoming AI IPOs

The concentration of AI IPOs tests market liquidity while SpaceX's governance precedent may accelerate similar founder-control structures. Law firms reportedly already pitch copycat arrangements to clients.
