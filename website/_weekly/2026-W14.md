Markets navigated a volatile week as the Iran conflict entered its fifth week with oil reaching $116/barrel, while TSMC's AI demand surge and China's manufacturing resilience provided counterpoints to mounting stagflation concerns. Global equity destruction reached historic $11-12 trillion in March, exceeding COVID-19 losses, as central banks faced impossible policy choices between growth support and inflation containment.

**Energy Crisis Deepens Amid Diplomatic Whiplash**

Oil markets dominated headlines as supply disruptions expanded beyond the Strait of Hormuz closure, with conflicting signals creating extreme volatility throughout the week.

- Brent crude peaked at $116/barrel on Monday (+60% month-to-date, largest monthly gain on record) before whipsawing on Tuesday between $107-$113 on ceasefire speculation
- WTI held above $101-$102/barrel with Brent-WTI spread widening to ~$15 as US production advantages became apparent
- Iranian strike on Kuwaiti tanker off Dubai on Tuesday quickly undermined diplomatic optimism, highlighting the fragility of any near-term resolution
- Supply disruption reached 10 million barrels per day from Hormuz closure, with additional risks mounting from Houthi attacks targeting Saudi Yanbu port (7M bpd capacity)
- US gasoline breached $4.00/gallon for first time since August 2022, climbing from ~$2.98 pre-conflict
- Analysts maintained 40% probability of $200 oil if Hormuz closure extends through June, with potential escalation to $140-$160 if Red Sea shipping resumes disruption

The conflict's expansion beyond energy became apparent as Iranian strikes damaged Gulf aluminum smelters, disrupting 9% of global production and sending aluminum prices up 5-6% on Monday.

**Historic Equity Destruction and Stagflation Positioning**

Global markets approached or entered correction territory as energy shocks combined with growth concerns to create one of the most severe monthly selloffs in market history.

- MSCI All-Country World Index fell 9.4% from peak, just 0.6% from technical correction territory
- March equity destruction reached $11-12 trillion globally, exceeding COVID-19 losses despite shorter timeframe
- Asian markets bore the brunt due to energy import dependency: Nikkei -4.5% on Monday, Korea Kospi -3.5% (worst global performer month-to-date despite being best pre-war)
- Nasdaq 100 entered correction territory with -11% from peak, while VIX sustained above 30 for first time since Liberation Day spike to 50
- China CSI 300 tested 200-day moving average at 4,500 on Monday, though showed relative resilience by Tuesday on manufacturing data

Safe-haven positioning revealed stagflation fears as both dollar and gold attracted flows simultaneously, with gold holding above $4,500 for three consecutive sessions despite well above $2,900 fair value estimates.

**Central Bank Policy Dilemmas Intensify**

Monetary authorities faced impossible choices as energy-driven inflation surged while growth risks mounted, forcing a reassessment of rate expectations across major economies.

- Fed Chair Powell described inflation pass-through as potentially "transitory" on Tuesday, calming bond markets despite March CPI preview hitting 3.4% (+100bps from recent lows)
- Eurozone CPI jumped to 2.5% YoY in March - largest increase since 2022, with Germany accelerating to 2.6%
- UK inflation expectations hit 4.5% on 2-year zero-coupon swaps, 250bps above target after five years of misses
- Rate cuts disappeared from 2025 expectations as ECB priced two 25bp hikes and BoE faced similar pressure
- Asian central banks particularly constrained by imported inflation versus growth trade-offs, unable to raise rates due to currency and capital flow concerns
- Fed independence faced political pressure with Kevin Warsh nomination looming, creating additional policy uncertainty

Bond markets showed technical resilience despite inflation pressures, with 10-year Treasury yields down 3-4bps as flight-to-quality demand offset inflation concerns.

**Technology Sector Faces Memory Disruption**

While oil dominated headlines, technology markets grappled with both AI infrastructure validation and memory sector disruption from breakthrough efficiency technology.

- TSMC's 37% January sales surge on Tuesday validated continued AI infrastructure demand despite broader tech weakness
- Google's Turbo Quant technology (6x memory reduction) shifted vulnerability from high-bandwidth memory (HBM) to flash/NAND storage
- Micron plunged double-digits on Tuesday while Kioxia faced particular exposure despite hedge fund enthusiasm
- SOX Index remained flat year-to-date with Nvidia down 20% from October peak, entering bear territory
- Memory chip disruption added to broader tech sector rotation away from growth stocks amid rising rate expectations

**China Shows Surprising Resilience**

Chinese economic data provided one of the week's few bright spots, suggesting the economy may be weathering energy disruption better than feared.

- China Manufacturing PMI beat expectations at 50.4 versus 50.1 expected on Tuesday - highest in 12 months
- Non-Manufacturing PMI reached 50.1 versus 49.9 expected, indicating broad-based resilience
- BYD recovered from early Monday losses despite warning of "brutal knockout stage" in EV competition and 38% Q4 profit decline
- Individual stocks showed strength: Moutai +4% on first price hike in three years, Midea +3.8% on tech pivot
- Global long-only funds turned neutral on China equities in February for first time since 2022

However, concerns remained about whether March data captured the full economic impact of the conflict.
