Markets grappled with an escalating Iran conflict that evolved from anticipated brief military action into sustained regional warfare, driving oil prices toward $87/barrel and forcing central banks to reassess policy paths as energy-driven inflation concerns clashed with growth risks. Asian equity markets suffered historic volatility, led by Korea's worst single-day decline on record, while China's dovish growth pivot signaled structural economic acceptance amid external pressures.

**Iran Conflict Escalation Drives Energy Crisis**

What began Monday as anticipated brief military action transformed into sustained warfare with no clear resolution timeline, creating the most significant energy supply shock since the Gulf War.

- Brent crude surged from $78 to $87/barrel through Friday, posting its largest weekly gain since 2022
- The Strait of Hormuz effectively closed by Thursday, with transits collapsing from 40 ships daily to approximately one tanker by week's end
- Qatar declared force majeure on LNG exports Wednesday, removing a top-3 global supplier and driving European natural gas futures up 50% for the week
- US gasoline prices reached 18-month highs, adding an estimated 30-40 basis points to March CPI projections
- Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Rastanura (550,000 bbl/day) was struck Monday and remains offline
- Over 27,000 flights cancelled across Middle East hubs as Gulf airspace closures stranded hundreds of thousands of passengers

The conflict's scope expanded significantly through the week. Monday's operations targeting Iranian infrastructure evolved into broader regional warfare by Thursday, when a US submarine sank an Iranian warship - the first such incident since WWII. Qatar's energy minister warned Friday that crude could reach $150/barrel if hostilities continue, while Iran fired over 600 ballistic missiles at UAE targets Wednesday and maintained attacks despite US claims of operational control.

**Asian Equity Markets in Historic Turmoil**

Asian markets experienced their worst week since March 2020, with unprecedented volatility highlighting dangerous leverage and positioning imbalances across the region.

- Korea's KOSPI suffered its worst single-day decline in history Wednesday, plunging 19.5% before staging a dramatic 10% rebound Thursday
- Samsung and SK Hynix swung from 15% declines to 12% gains in consecutive sessions, illustrating extreme positioning unwinds
- MSCI Asia Pacific fell 7% for the week with record outflows totaling $11 billion
- Foreign investors pulled $3 billion from Korea and $3.6 billion from Taiwan as energy import vulnerabilities became apparent
- Korean won breached 1,500 per dollar, hitting Global Financial Crisis levels despite the KOSPI's 45% year-to-date gains before the correction

Market analysts noted margin lending had reached maximum levels before Korea's crash, with single-stock futures and options products potentially exceeding underlying market size. This created mechanical instability that amplified moves as leveraged positions unwound. Thailand's market fell 8% Tuesday before trading halts, while the Hang Seng broke below 5,000 and entered correction territory.

**Central Bank Policy Paths Complicated by Stagflation Risk**

The energy shock forced central banks globally to reassess easing plans as inflation concerns reemerged alongside growth risks from the expanding conflict.

- Fed officials showed increasing division, with Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari suggesting Wednesday the oil shock "could take rate cut off table" for 2025
- ECB markets now price 60-70% probability of rate hikes as the next move, completely reversing from cut expectations
- 10-year Treasury yields climbed 30 basis points for the week to 4.17% as inflation concerns mounted
- Current Fed pricing shows only two cuts as baseline if rapid de-escalation occurs
- European bond yields rose sharply as the ECB's Rehn advocated a "steady hand" approach to the supply versus demand shock debate

The policy challenge intensified Thursday as strong ADP payrolls (63,000 vs 50,000 estimate) and resilient US data contrasted with energy-driven headwinds. Indonesia and India central banks intervened overnight Wednesday to defend currencies against oil-driven inflation pressures, while Malaysia held rates unchanged Friday citing downside risks from Middle East conflict.

**China's Pragmatic Growth Pivot**

Beijing delivered its most dovish economic outlook in three decades during the National People's Congress, signaling structural acceptance of slower growth rather than crisis-level stimulus.

- GDP growth target lowered to 4.5-5% range from higher previous targets, the lowest since 1991
- Fiscal deficit maintained at record 4% of GDP rather than expanded, preserving elevated post-COVID support levels
- ¥1.3 trillion in ultra-long bonds allocated for strategic initiatives, including ¥300 billion for state bank recapitalization
- PBoC adviser emphasized structural adjustment over demand-side stimulus Thursday, explicitly ruling out "big bazooka" response
- China Manufacturing PMI declined to 49.0 from 49.3 Tuesday, the second consecutive month of contraction

The policy response appeared measured despite market pressure, with the PBOC fixing the yuan at 6.88 Thursday - 20 pips below market estimates in an aggressive signal against currency weakness. China ordered refiners to halt fuel exports Friday amid energy security concerns, suggesting the conflict's impact on policy priorities extends beyond growth targets.

**Defensive Positioning and Safe Haven Dynamics**

Traditional safe haven assets showed mixed performance as the dollar reasserted dominance over gold and other alternatives.

- Dollar posted its strongest week since the November 2024 election, gaining most against energy-importing currencies
- Defense contractors outperformed throughout the week, with BAE Systems up 6%, Rheinmetall +5%, and Lockheed Martin +5% by Monday
- Gold gained early in the week but remained volatile and off session highs as dollar strength capped advances
- Investment grade credit spreads widened from generational lows of 71bp to 84bp
- Private credit faced withdrawal pressures with Blackstone raising $150 million from partners to cover BCRED redemptions

The yen and Swiss franc failed as traditional havens given Japan and Europe's energy import dependence, while VIX approached 25 - reflecting sustained but not panic-level risk aversion.

**Week Ahead**

Key developments that could drive market direction include:

- February payrolls Friday (forecast: 30,000 due to healthcare strikes)
- Unemployment rate expected at 4.4%, matching December levels
- Any signs of Iran conflict de-escalation or Gulf State involvement
- China's National People's Congress concluding with final policy details
- Private credit fund flows as semi-liquid strategies face redemption tests

The combination of geopolitical energy shock and persistent inflation pressures suggests markets may face extended stagflationary dynamics, particularly challenging for energy-importing economies across Asia and Europe.
