**Weekly Macro Brief: May 4-10, 2026**

Markets navigated a complex week marked by escalating Middle East tensions, a significant legal setback to Trump's trade agenda, and mixed signals from labor markets and Fed policy. Despite overnight US-Iran military exchanges on Friday, risk assets showed resilience while energy markets registered only modest gains, suggesting either complacency or confidence that broader escalation remains unlikely.

**Geopolitical Tensions Test Market Resilience**

The week culminated in direct US-Iran military confrontation in the Strait of Hormuz, yet markets displayed surprising calm in response to what could have been a major risk-off catalyst.

- US forces engaged Iranian targets Friday after attacks on three destroyers, though Trump characterized the response as a "love tap"
- Brent crude rose modestly to $101/barrel, up roughly 1% on Friday's session
- WTI remained below $95/barrel despite the strategic waterway being threatened
- US gasoline prices reached $4.50/gallon for the first time since 2022
- A 48-hour deadline for Iran's response to US proposals approached Friday's close
- Trump maintained the ceasefire remains intact while warning of a "big glow" if it breaks

The muted market response suggests either that investors view escalation risks as contained, or that geopolitical risk premiums have been systematically underpriced. The contrast between headline severity and market reaction represents a key disconnect heading into next week.

**Trade Policy Faces Legal and Diplomatic Headwinds**

Trump's trade agenda encountered significant challenges on both legal and diplomatic fronts, creating uncertainty around the administration's economic leverage.

- A federal court on Friday struck down Trump's 10% global tariffs as unlawful, dealing a major blow to trade policy
- The ruling creates precedent for broader challenges, with potential refunds reaching $200 billion if decisions expand
- The legal setback undermines administration leverage ahead of next week's planned Trump-Xi summit
- Chinese concerns over the unresolved Iran situation add diplomatic complexity to trade negotiations
- The reported delegation includes Jensen Huang (Nvidia) plus executives from Qualcomm, Apple, Boeing, Blackstone, and Citi

This convergence of legal defeats and geopolitical tensions complicates the administration's negotiating position just as critical trade talks approach. Markets appear to be pricing reduced odds of comprehensive trade deals given these constraints.

**Labor Markets and Fed Policy Signal Divergent Paths**

Friday's employment data faced dramatically lowered expectations amid growing signs of labor market softening and emerging Fed dissent over forward guidance.

- April payrolls consensus dropped to +65k, with some forecasters predicting the first negative print in months
- City projected -15k, representing a sharp deterioration from recent trends
- Unemployment rate expectations centered around 4.3%
- Fed discord emerged as Boston Fed's Collins preferred removing language "associated with presumption next move is cut"
- SF Fed's Daly downplayed communication debates, noting "most people don't read our statement"
- Markets now price equal probability of cuts versus hikes, with "higher for longer" largely established

The combination of weakening labor data and Fed officials questioning dovish guidance suggests policy uncertainty may persist longer than previously expected. This dynamic creates challenging cross-currents for rate-sensitive assets.

**China's Mixed Momentum Amid Policy Shifts**

Chinese markets posted their strongest week of 2026 despite Friday's pullback, driven by hardware momentum and attractive valuations, while policy indicators showed continued gold accumulation and currency strength.

- MSCI China gained approximately 3% for the week before Friday's -1% decline  
- Shanghai Composite came within 2 points of a fresh 10-year high during the week
- Goldman upgraded CSI 300 target to 5,300 from 5,000, preferring A-shares over H-shares
- CNY broke through 6.80 for the first time in months, though PBOC fixing at 6.852 suggested resistance
- China CPI accelerated to 3.3% in March from 2.4%, driven by energy costs
- PBoC added 8.1 tons of gold in April, the largest addition in over a year and 18th consecutive monthly purchase

The divergence between strong market performance and mounting geopolitical complexity around trade talks illustrates the tension between domestic momentum and external uncertainties facing Chinese assets.

**Corporate and Credit Markets Show Selective Stress**

Credit conditions revealed pockets of strain, particularly in leveraged names and private markets, while technology companies faced cash flow pressures despite AI optimism.

- SoftBank CDS widened on leverage concerns as S&P moved the outlook to negative
- Private credit transparency questions emerged after Gundlach highlighted a fund marked down from 100 to 81 overnight
- Big Tech reported decade-low free cash flows of $4 billion quarterly versus $45 billion historical average
- Commerce Bank exceeded Q1 expectations with €913 million net income and raised full-year outlook to €3.4 billion
- Korea's current account surged to $37 billion, driven by AI and memory chip exports
- Taiwan and Korea semiconductor names remained resilient ahead of next week's Nvidia earnings

The contrast between AI-driven optimism and underlying cash flow pressures suggests markets may be overlooking fundamental deterioration in favor of thematic momentum.

**Week Ahead**

Key developments to monitor include:

- Iran's response to the 48-hour US deadline
- Trump-Xi summit outcomes and trade policy developments  
- Nvidia earnings as a broader AI infrastructure catalyst
- Further Fed communication around policy guidance
- UK political stability following Labour's local election losses

The week ahead presents multiple binary outcomes that could significantly shift market positioning, particularly given the apparent disconnect between headline risks and current asset pricing.
