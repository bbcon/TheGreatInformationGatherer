Markets grappled with Iran's escalating blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, which disrupted 10 million barrels per day of oil flows and pushed Brent crude above $100 for the first time since 2022. The supply shock triggered a dramatic repricing of central bank policy expectations, with Fed rate cuts eliminated from 2025 forecasts as stagflation risks emerged. Asian semiconductor producers faced additional pressure from a critical helium shortage that threatens advanced chip manufacturing.

**Oil Supply Crisis Deepens Despite Policy Response**

The Strait of Hormuz crisis evolved from initial disruption on Monday to what the IEA termed "the worst supply disruption in history" by Thursday. Iran's appointment of hardliner Mujtaba Khamenei as Supreme Leader on Monday signaled regime continuity, dashing hopes for quick resolution.

- Brent crude surged from $113 on Monday to over $101 by Friday, marking 65% year-to-date gains
- WTI climbed from $118 Monday peaks to $95 Friday close as backwardation steepened
- Physical flows through Hormuz dropped to just 16% of normal capacity by Thursday
- Gulf producers including UAE, Kuwait, and Iraq halted operations due to storage constraints
- Shanghai crude futures gained 17% Monday, reflecting regional supply premium

The 400 million barrel Strategic Petroleum Reserve release announced during the week failed to suppress prices, with markets recognizing the intervention addressed stock levels rather than flow disruptions. RBC Energy expects $128/barrel within 3-4 weeks, while Goldman Sachs projects Q4 Brent at $93 for a 60-day disruption scenario versus $76 for 30 days.

Oil spending as a percentage of global GDP reached 10-10.5% by Monday, approaching the historically critical 12% demand destruction threshold.

**Central Bank Policy Repricing Accelerates**

The energy shock triggered the most dramatic central bank repricing in years, with Fed rate cuts completely eliminated from 2025 expectations by Friday. The policy challenge intensified as traditional safe-haven Treasury demand failed to materialize despite geopolitical turmoil.

- 10-year Treasury yields rose from 4.20% Monday to 4.27% Friday, gaining ground throughout the crisis
- 2-year yields reached 7-8 month highs at 3.65% as rate cut expectations collapsed
- ECB now pricing potential hikes compared to cuts expected weeks prior
- Bank of England shows over 50% probability of rate increase, reversing from fully priced March cut
- MOVE volatility index hit 9-month highs reflecting unprecedented rate market uncertainty

Over 10 central banks meet next week facing the worst-case stagflation scenario of supply-driven inflation amid growth concerns. The Fed's dual mandate appears impossible to navigate with core wage growth running at 3.5% annualized while oil shocks threaten economic activity.

Goldman Sachs pushed first Fed cut expectations to September 2025, while markets price zero cuts for the entire year.

**Asian Markets Face Semiconductor Supply Chain Crisis**

Asian equity markets entered correction territory Monday with regional benchmarks down 5%, though showed resilience later in the week despite emerging supply chain risks. A critical helium shortage from Qatar's Ras Laffan facility closure threatens advanced semiconductor production.

- Korea triggered circuit breakers Monday with KOSPI falling 8%, but surged 6% Tuesday on brief de-escalation hopes
- Taiwan declined 5.5% Monday, gained 3.5% Tuesday as tech buying resumed
- Asian currencies collectively fell 0.5% Monday, with Philippine peso and Indonesian rupiah hitting record lows
- Chinese yuan fixed at 6.93 Monday, weakest since November 2024

The helium crisis poses existential risks to advanced chip manufacturing, with no substitutes available for 5nm and below production nodes.

- Qatar supplies 33% of global helium production through the now-closed Ras Laffan facility
- Asian chipmakers have approximately 3 months of stockpiles before production constraints hit
- TSMC, SK Hynix, and Samsung all declined Friday on supply concerns despite strong earnings
- TSMC reported 30% year-over-year sales growth Tuesday, validating AI infrastructure demand

**Trump's Mixed Signals Create Policy Uncertainty**

President Trump's conflicting statements on Iran war resolution created historic oil volatility Tuesday, with Brent crude collapsing from $120 to $90 intraday before recovering. The whipsaw reflected market desperation for de-escalation signals despite persistent physical supply disruptions.

- Oil experienced unprecedented intraday ranges following Trump's "very soon" war-end comments Tuesday
- Physical supply disruption continued regardless of rhetoric, with only Iranian and Chinese vessels transiting Hormuz
- Markets shifted from pricing "days not weeks" resolution to "weeks not months" by Thursday
- G7 emergency meetings discussed coordinated responses, but achieved limited results

The rhetoric-reality disconnect highlighted market vulnerability to political messaging while underlying supply fundamentals remained unchanged. Iran's hardline leadership showed no compromise signals throughout the week.

**European Stagflation Risks Intensify**

European markets faced particular vulnerability given energy import dependency and weakening industrial fundamentals. The auto sector posted dismal earnings that highlighted pre-existing structural challenges.

- VW operating margins collapsed to 2.8%, lowest since 2015 and matching Dieselgate-era weakness
- Porsche margins fell to 0.3% from 14.5% prior year, effectively unprofitable
- Memory chip costs surged 300% across EV manufacturers, pressuring margins
- European gas futures rose 15% Monday on supply concerns

EUR/USD came under pressure as energy import bills surge while domestic growth slows. The ECB's surprising hawkish repricing reflects concerns that energy inflation could become entrenched in wage negotiations.
