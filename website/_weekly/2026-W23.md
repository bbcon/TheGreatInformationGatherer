**Weekly Macro Brief: June 1-7, 2026**

The week marked a significant inflection point for global markets as AI momentum stalled amid disappointing chip guidance, while emerging market currencies faced mounting pressure from renewed dollar strength. Trade policy uncertainty intensified with broad-based US tariff proposals, and private credit markets showed early signs of distress as major funds restricted redemptions.

**AI Euphoria Meets Reality**

The artificial intelligence rally that dominated 2026 encountered its first major test as Broadcom's disappointing guidance triggered violent unwinding across semiconductor positions.

Early in the week, AI optimism reached fever pitch with several milestone developments:

- SpaceX announced a record $75 billion IPO on Wednesday, doubling Saudi Aramco's previous record
- Marvell surged 33% after Jensen Huang cited it as a potential "next trillion-dollar company"
- Memory chip suppliers continued rallying on SK Hynix projections of supply constraints through 2030

However, the narrative reversed sharply by Friday when Broadcom's weak AI chip outlook sparked broad selling:

- Korea's KOSPI plunged 5.5% on Friday with Samsung down 4% and SK Hynix falling 6%
- Leveraged semiconductor ETFs amplified volatility, declining 15% and creating technical selling pressure
- Taiwan and Japan chip suppliers fell 3-7%, extending losses across Asian tech markets
- Nasdaq futures dropped 1%, with valuation concerns mounting as multiples reached "excessively enthusiastic" levels

**Trade Policy Uncertainty Resurfaces**

The Trump administration's Section 301 tariff proposal on Wednesday introduced fresh trade policy risks, targeting forced labor practices across 60 trading partners with a more legally robust framework than previous levies.

The scope proved broader than expected:

- 10% tariffs proposed for Canada, Mexico, EU, Taiwan, and UK
- 12.5% tariffs for China, India, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, and Switzerland
- July 7 hearings scheduled with 4-6 week comment period creating negotiation window
- Legal framework considered more durable after Supreme Court struck down earlier tariffs

Currency markets immediately reflected the heightened trade tensions, with dollar strength resuming and emerging market currencies under pressure throughout the remainder of the week.

**Emerging Market Currency Crisis**

Asian currencies faced their most severe pressure in months, with intervention risks rising across the region as the dollar rallied on both tariff headlines and shifting Fed expectations.

Indonesia emerged as the epicenter of stress:

- Rupiah touched 18,000 per dollar on Friday, marking fresh record lows
- Options markets pricing 40% probability of 19,000 by December as selling intensified
- Korean won approached weakest levels since 2009, compounding Friday's equity losses

Regional contagion was evident:

- USD/JPY approached the 160 handle with BOJ intervention expected but likely ineffective
- Chinese yuan held relatively stable at 6.77, outperforming regional peers
- Australian dollar weakened on trade policy concerns despite solid domestic data

Central banks faced increasing pressure to defend currencies through rate hikes rather than intervention, with several Asian monetary authorities signaling potential policy shifts.

**Private Credit Markets Under Stress**

Alternative investment markets showed early signs of distress as major funds restricted redemptions, highlighting liquidity concerns in the private credit sector.

Partners Group became the first major casualty on Wednesday:

- 9.8% redemption requests received on $8.6 billion global value fund
- Quarterly redemption cap implemented at 5%, primarily affecting Asia-Pacific investors
- Shares fell 18% in Zurich trading as contagion fears spread

The stress broadened by Friday:

- Blackstone limited withdrawals from BCR, its largest private credit fund
- Regulators including the Fed and SEC increased scrutiny as credit cycle turns
- Investors demanded greater transparency on asset markings amid liquidity pressures
- Redemption anxiety spreading from private credit to private equity structures

**Federal Reserve Policy Pivot**

Fed officials signaled a meaningful shift toward earlier rate hikes as labor market strength persisted, with markets now pricing potential tightening in early 2027 rather than cuts.

Key developments supporting the hawkish turn:

- US job openings jumped to highest level in nearly two years on Wednesday
- Cleveland Fed's Hammack suggested policy "may soon need to act" with neutral rate at 4.00-4.25% versus current 3.625%
- Fed's Daly called for patience on Friday and warned against premature forward guidance
- US payrolls data due Friday could provide critical direction for policy expectations

International central banks faced similar pressures:

- BoE's Bailey warned against waiting for second-round inflation effects, citing 2022 delays
- September BoE hike fully priced with officials hinting at earlier action
- RBI expected to hold rates at 5.25% despite inflation below 4% target
- BOJ rate hike expectations rising amid persistent yen weakness

**Week Ahead**

Key events that could drive market direction include:

- US payrolls data providing clarity on Fed policy trajectory
- Section 301 tariff hearings beginning July 7 with potential for early negotiations
- Asian central bank meetings with intervention and rate decisions in focus
- Private credit fund redemption deadlines creating potential liquidity stress
- Iran nuclear negotiations timeline uncertainty keeping energy markets elevated

The convergence of technology sector headwinds, emerging market currency stress, and shifting monetary policy expectations creates a challenging environment for risk assets. Bond markets appear positioned for volatility in either direction depending on labor market data, while the private credit sector faces its first major liquidity test since the post-pandemic tightening cycle began.
