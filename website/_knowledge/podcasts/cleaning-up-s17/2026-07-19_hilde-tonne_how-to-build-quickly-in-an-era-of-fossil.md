# How To Build Quickly In An Era of Fossil Fuel Shocks

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest:** Hilde Tonne - Chair of Arup Group, former CEO of Statnett (Norway's transmission operator), with extensive background in telecommunications and infrastructure

**Main Topics:**
- The energy transition's progress and regional variations
- Infrastructure development and the need for systems thinking
- Grid modernization and transmission challenges
- AI/data centers' impact on energy systems
- How to accelerate infrastructure deployment while maintaining sustainability

**Key Thesis:** The energy transition requires **holistic systems thinking** from the design phase, integrating energy, water, transport, and digital infrastructure. Success depends less on technology or capital availability and more on **reforming regulatory frameworks and decision-making processes** that date from the 1960s-70s.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The State of the Energy Transition

**Liebreich's framing:** Is the transition actually happening, and at what pace?

**Tonne's perspective:** The transition is definitively happening but at vastly different speeds globally:
- **China:** Moving decisively due to energy security, affordability, competitiveness, and AI energy needs
- **Europe:** Strong on renewable generation (particularly post-REPowerEU) but **bottlenecked by grid infrastructure**
- **US:** Currently stalled on coordinated transition efforts
- **Asia (excluding China):** Likely to leapfrog with newer technologies

**Key insight:** "This is not about technology development or capital... This is about decision-making, regulatory frameworks, permitting and bureaucracy."

The conversation shifted from viewing the transition as primarily about climate to recognizing it's fundamentally about **energy security, competitiveness, economic growth, and affordability**.

### Grid Infrastructure: The Critical Bottleneck

**The challenge:** Europe needs approximately **€1.5-5 trillion** in grid and distribution investments (Tonne initially misstated as billion, quickly corrected).

**Why transmission lags generation:**
- Private capital readily available for renewable generation
- Transmission remains highly regulated with frameworks from the 1960s
- **Regulated returns insufficient** to attract private capital at needed scale
- Permitting processes can take 12+ years for single transmission projects

**Tonne's prescription (from her Statnett experience):**
- Fundamental **mindset shift** needed among transmission system operators (TSOs)
- Regulatory framework modernization essential
- **Private capital must enter** the transmission equation
- Need to accelerate permitting without abandoning necessary consultation

**Supporting evidence:** Norway has 15 interconnections that automatically export when external prices are higher, demonstrating the system can work - but communication and public acceptance remain challenges.

### Total Design Philosophy

**Arup's approach:** Rooted in Ove Arup's founding vision of "total architecture," bringing together ~150 disciplines to deliver holistic solutions.

**Practical application - Copenhagen Metro M4 line:**
- Challenge: Reduce embodied carbon in metro construction and operations
- **Result: 40% reduction** in carbon footprint
- **Method:** Digital modeling from the start, eliminating unnecessary safety buffers through integrated design across disciplines
- "We said we don't need that buffer. We don't need that buffer. We don't need that buffer. Because when this goes together and we're excellent designers, we can take down the risk by seeing that system holistically."

**The procurement challenge:** Liebreich highlighted the "smart cities" problem - brilliant integrated visions fail because **no single procurement authority** exists to buy holistic solutions. Water, transport, energy, and sewage departments all operate in silos.

**Tonne's response:** This requires continuous leadership and cultural reinforcement. "It's in our DNA to think total design... but it needs to be led out." Arup's people "love complexity" and working across disciplines, but this must be professionally enabled while empowering teams.

### AI, Data Centers, and Energy System Stress

**The dual nature of AI:**
- **Demand side:** Massive, concentrated loads from data centers creating grid queue backlogs
- **Supply side:** AI can optimize energy systems through granular pricing, efficient heat pumps, EV charging networks

**The distributional problem:** Benefits will be distributed across the entire network in small increments, while **challenges are concentrated** in large, centralized data center loads.

**Liebreich's concern:** The US is moving at "warp speed" with temporary gas generation and private deals to acquire nuclear plants for data centers - essentially "the wild west."

**Tonne's position on cost allocation:**
Speaking "in the mindset of the CEO of Statnett," hyperscalers should pay for:
- Grid upgrades
- Backup power
- Extreme costs of accelerated services
- Grid balancing costs

**Key quote:** "Remember who's paying for the grid. It's you and me and everyone. It's the taxpayers."

**The optimization imperative:** Both sides need optimization - tech companies on latency and efficiency, grid providers on infrastructure. This requires **shared risk with proper incentives** on both sides, viewing data centers and energy transition as interdependent: "You cannot have a decarbonization of the world without dematerializing through digital and you cannot have digital and AI without the energy transition."

### European Competitiveness and Offshore Wind Costs

**Liebreich's challenge:** Doesn't offshore wind at €70-100/MWh lock in high electricity costs for Europe compared to China (€30-40/MWh) or US (€30-40/MWh delivered to commercial customers)?

**Tonne's counter-arguments:**
1. **Technology trajectory:** Floating offshore wind will drive costs down dramatically (she believes floating will eventually be cheaper than bottom-fixed depending on depth and distance)
2. **Systems thinking required:** Can't evaluate offshore wind costs in isolation
3. **Total system benefits:**
   - Energy security (reduced import dependence)
   - Lower total energy costs through electrification
   - Increased competitiveness
   - Better outcomes for industry and consumers

**Geographic expansion:** Offshore wind isn't just Europe - Japan, Korea, China, Irish Sea, and Baltics all represent major opportunities.

**Nuclear comparison:** Tonne argues offshore wind and nuclear costs aren't directly comparable given different roles in the energy mix and deployment timelines.

### Accelerating Infrastructure Development

**The China comparison:** China moves fast partly because it operates "at war speed" without extensive environmental impact assessments or consultations.

**Tonne's nuanced position:**
- **Supports consultation:** "I believe in consultation... This is about talking to people and creating infrastructure development that is good for people." (Arup employs ~100 psychologists for this work)
- **Opposes circular bureaucracy:** "I'm pretty sure we could stop the working in circles and things lying on shelf processes"
- **Demands regulatory modernization:** Frameworks from 1960-70 need updating for digital age

**The real bottleneck:** Not TSOs necessarily going slow, but decision-making processes designed for a world where infrastructure was decided by civil servants and politicians using national budgets, not private capital.

**Solution:** Move toward **public-private collaboration** with regulatory and decision-making processes "fit for purpose" for faster deployment.

**Example from aerospace:** Professor Rob Miller's Whittle Lab reduced innovation-to-engine deployment from ~10 years to potentially 3 months through 3D printing, modeling/simulation, and streamlined certification processes.

### Resilience in the New Energy System

**The buffer problem:** Moving from coal piles, gas line pack, gas storage, and strategic petroleum reserves to an electrified system removes traditional buffers.

**Tonne's confidence in greater resilience:**

**Positive factors:**
- **Local/regional production** eliminates import dependencies
- **Mesh grids** with multiple interconnections dramatically increase resilience
- Self-storage and cross-border balancing capabilities
- Lower overall risk than offshore oil platforms and fossil fuel production

**Required investments:**
- **Flexibility and storage** (batteries, potentially electrolyzers)
- **Digital solutions** (creating cyber security exposure)
- **Physical security** for distributed critical infrastructure
- Hybrid systems with multiple delivery pathways

**Key insight:** "It's not happening by itself" - resilience requires intentional investment and design.

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### Total Design/Systems Thinking Framework

**Core principle:** Start with holistic design integrating all disciplines from the beginning, rather than optimizing components separately.

**Application process:**
1. Begin with long-term vision and future-proof solutions
2. Bring together diverse experts (structural, electrical, societal, etc.)
3. Identify and eliminate unnecessary buffers through integrated understanding
4. Optimize across materials, procurement, energy, water, transport simultaneously
5. Calculate total system outcomes, not component costs

**Result:** Lower energy costs, sustainable material choices, integrated systems - but only if started early in design phase.

### The Regulatory Modernization Framework

**Diagnosis:** Current frameworks designed for:
- 1960s-70s technology and capabilities
- National budget-funded infrastructure
- Civil servant/political decision-making
- Pre-digital world

**Prescription:**
- Eliminate circular processes and shelf-sitting
- Update for digital capabilities
- Enable private capital participation
- Maintain necessary consultation while removing bureaucratic delays
- Create "fit for purpose" processes for modern deployment speed

### The Energy Transition Motivation Shift

**Old framing:** Climate-focused, emissions reduction, somewhat altruistic ("we need to save the world")

**New framing (Tonne's "helicopter view"):**
- **Energy security** (local production)
- **Competitiveness** (economic advantage)
- **Economic growth** (through lower costs)
- **Affordability** (for people and industry)

This reframing makes the transition about immediate economic and security interests rather than distant environmental concerns.

## 4. Contrarian Takes

### Private Capital Must Enter Transmission

**Consensus view:** Transmission is a natural monopoly best handled by regulated utilities with government backing.

**Tonne's position:** The scale of needed investment (€1.5-5 trillion in Europe alone) requires private capital, which won't come without regulatory reform to improve returns and reduce timeline risk.

### Floating Offshore Wind Will Be Cheaper Than Fixed

**Current consensus:** Bottom-fixed offshore wind is cheaper than floating.

**Tonne's prediction:** Technology development in floating offshore wind will make it cheaper than bottom-fixed, depending on depth and distance from shore. She sees this as still in development but inevitable.

### Consultation Isn't the Problem

**Common criticism:** Environmental reviews and public consultation slow infrastructure deployment (implicit in "China moves fast without them").

**Tonne's counter:** "The hampering part is the regulatory systems are taking into account things we don't have to take into account anymore." Consultation can and should continue, but bureaucratic processes need modernization. The problem isn't talking to people - it's outdated decision frameworks.

### The New Energy System Is More Resilient

**Concern:** Removing fossil fuel buffers (coal piles, gas storage, strategic reserves) makes the system more fragile.

**Tonne's argument:** Despite new vulnerabilities (cyber security, distributed physical assets), the new system is "totally much more resilient" due to local production, mesh grids, and elimination of import dependencies. The risk in offshore oil platforms and fossil fuel production is actually higher.

### Hyperscalers Should Pay for Grid Upgrades

**Current practice:** Grid costs socialized across all users.

**Tonne's position:** Data centers creating massive, concentrated loads should pay for grid upgrades, backup, accelerated services, and balancing costs - not taxpayers. This represents a significant departure from traditional utility cost allocation.

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Infrastructure Development

**Immediate actions:**
- **Start with total design:** Integrate all disciplines from project inception, not sequentially
- **Digitize from the start:** Use sensors, data, modeling to eliminate over-design buffers
- **Procure materials early:** Lock in sustainable choices even if initially costly
- **Calculate total system benefits:** Don't evaluate components in isolation

**Example application:** Any new metro, building, or industrial facility should begin with integrated energy, water, transport, and materials planning using digital tools.

### For Grid Operators and Regulators

**Priority reforms:**
1. **Modernize regulatory frameworks** - eliminate 1960s-era requirements incompatible with digital capabilities
2. **Streamline permitting** - maintain consultation but remove circular processes
3. **Enable private capital** - adjust regulated returns and risk allocation
4. **Implement user-pays for large loads** - data centers and other concentrated loads should fund their grid impacts

**Timeline imperative:** With 2030 only 5 years away, these reforms cannot wait for perfect solutions.

### For Energy Transition Strategy

**Investment priorities:**
- **Grid infrastructure** is the bottleneck, not generation capacity
- **Flexibility and storage** are essential, not optional
- **Digital solutions** for optimization across the system
- **Cyber and physical security** for distributed infrastructure

**Geographic focus:**
- Europe: Fix the grid bottleneck
- Northern Europe: Offshore wind (both bottom-fixed and floating)
- Southern Europe: Solar and wind with batteries
- All regions: Interconnection and mesh grid development

### For Private Sector

**Data center developers:**
- Expect to pay for grid upgrades, backup, and balancing
- Optimize for latency to reduce energy requirements
- Engage early with grid operators on long-term planning

**Infrastructure investors:**
- Transmission investment opportunities opening as regulatory frameworks modernize
- Offshore wind technology still developing (especially floating)
- Integrated solutions (energy + water + transport) offer advantages

**Industrial companies:**
- Electrification reduces total energy costs despite higher electricity unit costs
- Early adoption of sustainable materials and processes creates long-term cost advantages
- Hard-to-abate sectors need design-phase solutions, not retrofits

### For Understanding Macro

**Regional competitiveness:**
- Europe's transition speed depends on grid investment, not generation
- China's integrated approach (energy security + AI needs) driving faster deployment
- US currently fragmented with "wild west" data center development

**Cost dynamics:**
- **Don't compare component costs** (offshore wind vs. nuclear) - evaluate total system costs
- Energy security and local production have economic value beyond unit costs
- Upfront investment in holistic design reduces total lifecycle costs

**Timeline reality:**
- Technology and capital are available now
- **Regulatory and bureaucratic reform is the critical path**
- Private sector moving faster than public sector frameworks allow
