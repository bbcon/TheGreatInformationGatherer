# The Electrification Staircase: A Framework for Climate Action | Cleaning Up Podcast Summary

## 1. Episode Overview

This episode, recorded live in Brussels, introduces the **Electrification Staircase**—a new framework for understanding which applications can be electrified now versus later. Host Michael Liebreich is joined by the tool's co-authors:

- **Adrian Heal**, Director General of the Electrification Alliance
- **Tom Butler**, Associate with the Regulatory Assistance Project (heat and buildings expert)
- **Will Drake**, Associate with Liebreich Associates and EcoPragma Capital (transport specialist)
- **Dr. Silvia Madeddu**, Solutions Architect at Schneider Electric, formerly of Potsdam Institute

**Key Context**: Every climate scenario requires electrification to grow from today's ~30% of energy use to 60-80%+. Yet progress is slowed by narrative wars claiming electrification "doesn't work" for various applications, often promoting hydrogen as the alternative. The Electrification Staircase aims to cut through this confusion, similar to how the Hydrogen Ladder clarified hydrogen's limited role.

**Main Thesis**: Most emissions come from applications that are **already commercially viable to electrify today**. Policy and investment should focus on scaling these proven solutions (Rows A-C) while supporting R&D for harder applications (Rows D-F).

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Structure of the Staircase

**The Framework**: Six rows organizing applications by commercial readiness and timeline:

- **Row A**: Commercial now in most places (no-brainers)
- **Row B**: Commercial now in many places
- **Row C**: Commercial now in some places
- **Row D**: Likely commercial in 2030s (pilots/demonstrations today)
- **Row E**: Likely commercial in 2040s
- **Row F**: Beyond 2040s (highly speculative)

**What "Commercial" Means**: The team defined this as economically viable without support, or with modest carbon pricing (€50-100/tonne CO2), not requiring €500-1000/tonne support. This sparked significant debate among authors and will be refined in future iterations.

### Buildings and Heat: The Low-Hanging Fruit

**Tom Butler's Key Insight**: Heat pumps represent one of the clearest wins because they don't generate heat—they move it, achieving 3-4x efficiency versus combustion.

**Row A (New Buildings)**: 
- Heat pumps in new single and multi-family homes are absolute no-brainers
- Modern building standards mean better insulation and heat retention
- Heating systems can be designed from scratch for electric operation
- Equipment costs can be financed into mortgages
- **Butler**: "Any building constructed today in a cold climate without a heat pump from day one is a complete failure of imagination, finance, and regulation"

**Row B (Retrofits)**:
- Existing buildings present complications but remain viable
- May require radiator upgrades or fabric improvements for optimal performance
- Heat pumps work without these upgrades, but improvements lower operating costs
- Natural replacement cycles matter—retrofit when replacing old boilers anyway

**The Time Dimension**: Unlike the Hydrogen Ladder's simple vertical scale of "will work/won't work," the staircase incorporates timing—retrofits work now but are best done during planned renovations.

### Industrial Heat: Debunking the Temperature Myth

**Madeddu's Groundbreaking Research**: Her 2020 Potsdam Institute paper examined technical potential to electrify European industry, finding that **temperature is not the barrier** people claim.

**Available Technologies**:
- Plasma torches: 10,000°C
- Induction furnaces: 3,000°C  
- Resistance furnaces: 1,000°C+
- Arc welding (Liebreich's favorite simple example)

**Row B-C Industrial Applications** (Commercial now in many/some places):

**Glass Furnaces**: 
- Electric glass furnaces have existed for decades for high-end products
- Historically used for niche applications (premium spirit bottles)
- Can achieve **50% energy reduction** versus gas through precise, targeted heating
- Butler: "Even without heat pump efficiency (4:1), if you can target heating more precisely (1:1 but focused), you can halve energy use"

**Steel Downstream Processes**:
- Reheating and forming operations can use induction and resistance heating
- Creates more pleasant, safer working environments
- Workers no longer need heat shields in sweltering conditions
- Improves productivity and reduces workforce churn

**Paint Lines, Paper Drying, Textiles**: All commercially viable with heat pumps and electric heating today.

**The Precision Advantage**: Liebreich's kettle analogy—gas hob (30-40% efficient), electric kettle (80%), microwave for one cup (95%). Electrification isn't like-for-like replacement; it's fundamentally more precise and efficient.

### Transport: The 80% Solution Already Exists

**Will Drake's Central Point**: The bottom three rows (A-C) represent **80% of transport emissions**, with Row A alone accounting for 50%.

**The Battery Revolution**:
- Past 15 years: 93% price drop, 2x energy density, 2x lifetime
- Eight years ago, electric trucks didn't exist commercially
- Today: Available for urban distribution, regional hauling, and long-haul

**The Stereotype Problem**: 
Drake challenges the "Rotterdam to Slovenia non-stop" trucking myth—this represents just **0.2% of heavy-duty trucking**. Reality is Valencia-Madrid, London-Birmingham, or urban last-mile delivery.

**Marginal Improvements, Massive Impact**:
- Increasing truck range from 300km to 400km seems modest
- But 400km enables a full 4.5-hour legal driving shift before mandatory breaks
- This unlocked Europe's 6th busiest route (Valencia-Madrid) in just the past year

**Rail Electrification**:
- India: 99% of wide-gauge railway now electrified
- UK Great Western Railway: Operating battery-electric branch line trains
- Perfect use case: A-to-B shuttling allows optimized charging infrastructure
- Weight doesn't matter on rails, so heavy batteries are fine
- **The Australian Gravity Train**: Loads full at mines, drives downhill to port, charges battery via regenerative braking while unloading, returns uphill empty—nearly perpetual motion

**Row A (Cars, Buses, Light Commercial Vehicles, Urban Trucks)**: Fully commercial, should be scaling rapidly everywhere.

**Row B (Regional Trucks, Branch Line Trains)**: Commercial in many places, expanding rapidly.

**Row C (Long-haul Trucks, Ferries)**: Commercial in some places, particularly where charging infrastructure exists.

### Heavy Industry: The 2030s-2040s Challenge

**Madeddu on Rows D-E**: The most energy and carbon-intensive sectors—cement, steel, aluminum, chemicals—face multiple challenges beyond temperature.

**Cement**:
- Calcination (1,000°C): Multiple pilots including Leilac
- Clinker making (1,400°C): Heidelberg testing plasma torches
- Technology exists, but scale-up is the challenge

**Primary Steel**:
- Electric arc furnaces (secondary steel) are limited by scrap supply and quality
- **Iron electrolysis** (companies like Boston Metal, ArcelorMittal pilots): Completely disruptive approach
- Can't retrofit—requires entirely new production paradigm
- Needs multi-billion dollar investments to reach commercial scale

**Chemicals**:
- BASF Verbund site in Germany: Demonstration plant for resistive heating of steam crackers
- Target: 90% decarbonization
- Still at pilot stage

**Aluminum**:
- Alcoa testing electrification of alumina calcination in Australia
- Electrolytic cell lines offer some load flexibility

**The Scale Challenge**: 
Madeddu: "Cement plants produce 10,000 tons per day. We're not talking about kilowatts—we're talking about megawatts at huge scale. Capital investment and derisking are critical, or we won't have first movers."

**The Supply Challenge**: Where do these massive loads connect to the grid? Unlike tech data centers worth trillions, these industries operate on single-digit margins in good years.

### The Flexibility Question

**The Problem**: Heavy industry operates continuously at massive scale—similar load profiles to AI data centers but with 2-3% margins instead of trillion-dollar balance sheets.

**Emerging Solutions**:
- **Cement**: Can't flex clinker making, but grinding/milling raw materials offers flexibility
- **Steel**: Electric arc furnaces load, melt, unload—small windows for demand response
- **Aluminum**: Some electrolytic cells in production lines can cycle on/off

**The Paradigm Shift**: 
Madeddu: "We're moving from an era of constant fossil fuel availability to variable renewable electricity. We must adapt to this variability. Flexibility enables monetizing this variability, but it requires changing business-as-usual."

Liebreich adds: Early in his career, glass furnaces ran for 15-25 years without shutting down. The renewable energy system is pushing back toward batch processing—take a holiday during dark doldrums (dunkelflaute).

### The Hydrogen Debate

**The Contentious Discussion**: Authors debated vigorously where hydrogen electrolysis fits.

**The Conclusion**: No location exists where electrolysis is commercial without >€100/tonne CO2 support—far above the "modest" threshold.

**Niche Use Cases That Might Work**:
- Distributed hydrogen needs (labs, generator cooling)
- Currently served by expensive tube trailer delivery
- Local electrolysis could become competitive by 2040s
- Placed on **Row E** (2040s commercialization)

**Liebreich's Position**: "Trying not to be ideological—there are use cases. The question is when they commercialize." The team collectively decided 2040s, though "some voices were louder than others."

### Transport's Harder Cases

**Row D (2030s)**:
- River shipping
- Short-haul aviation (exciting but still loss-making for a decade)
- Coastal shipping (some electric container ships on short routes, but not yet common)

**Row E (2040s)**:
- Medium-haul aviation
- Blue water (ocean) shipping with very large vessels

**Row F (Beyond 2040s)**:
- Long-haul aviation (Liebreich: "Not science fiction, but I'm not sure I can put it even on Row F")
- Novel chemical synthesis using electricity to secure carbon and create chemicals
- High value might justify extremely expensive processes

**The Acknowledgment**: The team wanted to recognize people working on far-future applications without claiming certainty about timelines.

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Staircase vs. The Ladder

**Key Difference**: The Hydrogen Ladder was purely vertical (probability of working). The Electrification Staircase adds a **time dimension**—when applications become commercial matters as much as whether they work.

**Application**: 
- Rows A-C: Focus on **scaling and removing barriers**
- Rows D-E: Focus on **R&D, pilots, and de-risking investment**
- Row F: Focus on **fundamental research**

### The 80/20 Principle in Action

**Drake's Transport Insight**: 80% of transport emissions are in Rows A-C (relatively straightforward to solve), while the top rows represent small emissions but very difficult problems.

**Policy Implication**: "Which should you focus on as a policymaker? Clearly, doing the stuff that's the bulk of emissions that you can accelerate today."

This applies across sectors—don't let the hard 20% paralyze action on the easy 80%.

### The Network Effects of Electrification

**Liebreich's Key Point**: "The key to getting electricity prices down is actually electrification. When we made more phone calls, we got cheaper phone calls."

**The Mechanism**:
1. Heat pumps and EVs create flexible demand
2. Flexible demand enables more renewable deployment
3. More renewables lower wholesale prices for everyone
4. Lower prices make energy-intensive applications more viable
5. Volume spreads network costs (transmission, distribution, batteries, synchronous condensers)

**Implication**: Dealing with easy stuff makes hard stuff easier later. It's a virtuous cycle, not a zero-sum game.

### Beyond CAPEX/OPEX Analysis

**Madeddu's Framework for Industry**: Traditional levelized cost of heat (LCOH) comparisons are insufficient for heavy industry decisions.

**What's Missing**:
- **Resilience value**: Shielding from geopolitical shocks and fuel price volatility
- **Transition risk**: Avoiding exposure to future carbon prices and stranded assets
- **Access to capital**: Eligibility for decarbonization funds (e.g., EU Industry Decarbonization Bank)
- **Workforce benefits**: Safer, more pleasant working conditions reduce churn
- **Neighborhood benefits**: No local pollution

**Application**: "Electrification is not just a procurement strategy, it's a resilience strategy. We need to include these assessments in financial planning."

## 4. Contrarian Takes

### Temperature Is Not the Barrier

**The Consensus View**: Heavy industry can't electrify because it requires high temperatures electricity can't reach.

**Madeddu's Counter**: "We have plasma torches reaching 10,000°C, induction furnaces at 3,000°C, resistance furnaces above 1,000°C. Temperature is not the issue."

**The Real Barriers**: 
- Technology readiness level (pilots vs. commercial scale)
- Capital intensity and investment risk
- Supply chain readiness (grid connection for massive loads)
- Business model disruption (batch vs. continuous processes)

### The Trucking Stereotype Is Wrong

**The Consensus View**: Long-haul trucking requires 16-hour non-stop drives, making electrification impossible.

**Drake's Counter**: That stereotype represents 0.2% of heavy-duty trucking. The reality is regional and urban routes perfectly suited to current electric truck capabilities.

**The Data**: Analysis of 3 billion kilometers of route data confirms most trucking fits within current battery-electric truck range, especially considering mandatory driver breaks.

### Hydrogen Electrolysis Is Not Commercial Anywhere

**The Hydrogen Industry Narrative**: Green hydrogen via electrolysis is commercially viable and scaling rapidly.

**The Authors' Position**: No location exists where electrolysis is commercial without carbon pricing >€100/tonne CO2—well above "modest" support levels.

**The Nuance**: Small niche applications (distributed hydrogen needs) might become commercial by 2040s, but this is fundamentally different from the "hydrogen economy" narrative.

### China Understood This Years Ago

**Liebreich's Observation**: "The sense I get is that China understood all of this quite some time ago and has built the supply chains accordingly."

**Implication**: While Western countries debate narratives, China has been executing on electrification at scale, creating dominant positions in batteries, EVs, heat pumps, and electric industrial equipment.

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Policymakers

**Immediate Actions (Rows A-C)**:
1. **Remove barriers** rather than provide subsidies
   - Heal: "Local planning permission problems with heat pumps are needless barriers"
   - Focus on regulatory streamlining for proven technologies
2. **Mandate electrification in new construction**
   - Any new building without heat pumps is "a failure of imagination, finance, and regulation"
3. **Align replacement cycles with electrification**
   - Target boiler replacements, vehicle fleet turnover
4. **Grid planning for known loads**
   - Butler: "System operators need to know what loads to expect. This tool helps them engage with industrial sites now."

**Medium-term Actions (Rows D-E)**:
1. **De-risk first-mover investments**
   - Heavy industry needs capital support for billion-dollar scale-ups
   - Carbon contracts for difference, loan guarantees
2. **Parallel R&D processes**
   - Don't wait on hard stuff to start easy stuff
   - Prepare hard applications to "jump on electrification" when ready
3. **Differentiate R&D from rollout**
   - Heal: "Policy makers need nuance around what to work on first, what to expect when, what is R&D versus rollout"

### For Industry

**Immediate Opportunities**:
1. **Low-temperature process heat** (breweries, textiles, food processing)
   - Heat pumps offer immediate cost savings
   - Improved working conditions reduce labor costs
2. **Downstream steel processes** (reheating, forming)
   - Induction and resistance heating commercially proven
3. **Glass, ceramics** (in favorable electricity price environments)
   - 50% energy reductions possible

**Strategic Positioning**:
1. **Disclose real economics**
   - Madeddu: "We need data on how much technologies actually cost, beyond just heat pumps and boilers"
2. **Include resilience value in business cases**
   - Fuel price hedging, carbon price protection, access to green finance
3. **Engage with grid operators early**
   - Massive loads need advance planning
4. **Explore flexibility revenue**
   - Even limited load flexibility can generate revenue in variable renewable systems

### For Investors

**High-Confidence Bets (Rows A-C)**:
- Heat pump manufacturing and installation
- EV charging infrastructure (especially commercial/fleet)
- Electric truck manufacturing and charging (Pragma Charge model)
- Industrial heat pump applications
- Battery technology improvements (even small gains have disproportionate impact)

**Higher-Risk, Higher-Reward (Rows D-E)**:
- Iron electrolysis for primary steel
- Electric cement kilns at scale
- Plasma torch applications for high-temperature industry
- Battery-electric short-haul aviation
- Coastal electric shipping

**Key Investment Thesis**: 
- Volume drives down costs in network industries
- First movers in Rows A-C create infrastructure that enables Rows D-E
- China's supply chain dominance is a competitive reality to navigate

### For Understanding Macro

**The Electrification Megatrend**:
- Electricity demand will grow substantially even as total energy use may decline
- Variable renewable generation requires flexible demand and storage
- Industrial competitiveness will increasingly depend on access to cheap clean electricity

**Geopolitical Implications**:
- Energy security shifts from fuel supply to electricity supply and grid resilience
- Manufacturing competitiveness tied to electricity prices
- Supply chain dominance (batteries, heat pumps, EVs) creates strategic advantages

**The Transition Timeline**:
- 2020s: Scale Rows A-C (80% of transport emissions, most building heat, low-temp industry)
- 2030s: Commercialize Rows D-E (heavy industry, harder transport)
- 2040s+: Tackle remaining challenges (long-haul aviation, novel chemistry)

### Actionable Takeaways

**For Individuals**:
1. Choose heat pumps for new construction or major renovations
2. Consider EVs for next vehicle purchase (especially for predictable daily driving)
3. Advocate for removal of local barriers (planning permission, utility connection rules)

**For Businesses**:
1. Audit energy use for Row A-C opportunities
2. Time equipment replacement to align with electrification
3. Engage with utilities on future load requirements
4. Build resilience value into investment cases

**For Advocates**:
1. Use the staircase to counter "electrification doesn't work" narratives
2. Focus attention on scaling proven solutions (Rows A-C)
3. Support appropriate policy for each row (barrier removal vs. R&D funding)
4. Avoid letting perfect (Row F) be the enemy of good (Rows A-C)
