# The Era Of Fossil Fuel Unreliability Has Begun | Ep254: Jennifer Granholm

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest Background:** Jennifer Granholm served as US Secretary of Energy under President Biden after two terms as Governor of Michigan. She steered implementation of the historic Inflation Reduction Act and now runs her own energy consultancy while serving as senior counselor for advisory firm DGA.

**Main Topics:** The conversation covers the geopolitical energy crisis following the Strait of Hormuz blockade, the legacy and partial dismantling of Biden-era clean energy policies, lessons learned from government service, and the path forward for Democrats and clean energy policy.

**Key Thesis:** Granholm argues that fossil fuels have become **structurally unreliable** due to geopolitical volatility and price instability, accelerating a global shift toward energy sovereignty through domestic clean energy. Despite Trump administration rollbacks, significant clean energy momentum remains embedded in US policy and markets.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Strait of Hormuz Crisis and Energy Unreliability

**The Framing:** Host Michael Liebreich opens by noting the collapse of peace negotiations and asking how the crisis is playing out domestically in the US.

**Granholm's Perspective:** She frames the blockade as more than a temporary disruption—it represents **"the deglobalization of the energy trade."** Gulf oil hasn't just become more expensive; it's become structurally unreliable. She argues this accelerates the "rewiring of global energy geography" as **oil is no longer fungible** and insurance may not cover transit risks through key chokepoints.

**Key Insight:** Granholm emphasizes that **fossil fuel unreliability stems not just from Middle East disruptions but from inherent price volatility**. The Energy Information Administration projects natural gas prices will double from 2024 to 2026—this volatility itself makes fossil fuels unreliable and drives diversification.

**Supporting Evidence:** She draws parallels to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, which forced Europe to diversify away from Russian energy. The Strait of Hormuz crisis represents the **"Asian equivalent"** of that shock, affecting supplies to China, India, Japan, Korea, and other Asian nations.

### Trump Administration Energy Policy Contradictions

**The Question:** How could Trump promise to halve energy prices while implementing policies that drive them up?

**Granholm's Analysis:** She catalogs the contradictions: forcing utilities to keep uneconomical coal plants online, eliminating incentives for the cheapest energy sources (wind and solar), removing consumer incentives for EVs and weatherization, and imposing inflationary tariffs on energy equipment. **"Every step that he has taken is counter to energy dominance"** and cedes territory to China.

**The Recklessness Factor:** Granholm emphasizes Trump appears to ignore his own advisers, including Vice President and Marco Rubio, who reportedly opposed the Iran escalation. **"The notion that you would do something that jacks up the price of gasoline in the United States significantly, especially during an election year, it's just really reckless."**

**Unintended Consequences:** The administration's behavior internationally, combined with fossil fuel unreliability, teaches the world that **"the only way that you can protect and you can have energy sovereignty is by focusing on homegrown energy solutions"**—which means renewables and clean energy, particularly for the EU and Asia.

### The Inflation Reduction Act: What Survived and What Didn't

**The Dismantling:** Liebreich challenges the early optimism from guests like Jigar Shah that the IRA was "locked in," noting significant rollbacks occurred.

**What Was Lost:**
- Offshore wind incentives eliminated
- Onshore wind and solar incentives removed
- Consumer incentives for EV purchases eliminated
- Ability to permit renewables on federal lands restricted

**What Survived (The Better News):**
- **Battery storage incentives remain intact** for utility-scale and long-duration storage
- Incentives for "firm clean power" (nuclear, geothermal, hydroelectric) still there
- **Manufacturing incentives preserved**, even for solar equipment
- Projects that achieved "safe harbor" status (construction started) retain their tax credits through July deadline

**The Math Still Works:** Granholm explains the stacking incentives: **30% base tax credit (transferable with prevailing wage) + 10% for energy communities + 10% for domestic content = 50% tax credit**. This makes the US "still irresistible" in these categories.

**Results:** Even with rollbacks, 2025 will see nearly 60 gigawatts of clean energy added to the grid—just shy of 2024's record 61 gigawatts. 2026 should plateau at similar levels. **"If you took away the Inflation Reduction Act and only had the one big beautiful bill... it would still be the largest investment in clean energy in US history."**

### The Manufacturing Boom and Its Fragility

**What Granholm Is Most Proud Of:** The IRA triggered **950 factory announcements** for clean energy manufacturing between passage and December 2024—**"the biggest manufacturing boom that the country has seen in decades."** As a former governor of industrial Michigan, she saw this as restoring the manufacturing DNA lost to offshoring.

**The Communication Failure:** Her biggest regret is that **"people didn't know about it."** There were many groundbreakings but not enough ribbon cuttings before the election. The benefits weren't visible or durable enough politically because factories take time to hire and produce.

**The Export Advantage Lost:** The IRA provided tax breaks even for exported products, creating hidden trade finance that made US manufacturers internationally competitive against China. **"Xi and his advisors in China are probably thinking, 'Can you believe this?'"** Trump eliminated this advantage, essentially handing dominance in batteries and EVs to China on a silver platter.

**The Global Consequence:** Liebreich observes that "most of world" markets (outside US, EU, China) are not going to choose American cars, trucks, or rolling stock. **"It just feels like the US has taken itself out of competing for those markets"** and is trying to push oil and LNG instead—exactly what those markets are trying to move away from.

### Data Centers as the Unexpected Clean Energy Driver

**The Demand Reality:** Data centers, particularly for AI, are driving massive electricity demand growth. Tech companies with net-zero commitments to shareholders are **"experimenting in all sorts of technologies"** and primarily deploying solar plus storage.

**Why It Works:** Even without some renewable tax credits, projects with sufficient battery storage remain economically attractive. The storage component makes data centers interesting to utilities as **potential grid assets** through demand flexibility.

**The Flexibility Value:** When data centers have storage and agree to demand flexibility, they can power themselves during grid capacity constraints—**"a very interesting proposition in terms of cost and in terms of economic development for communities."**

**Technology Development:** The episode references work by Varun Sivaram's company Emerald AI (in partnership with National Grid) on flexing data centers to "play nice with the grid."

### Political Strategy and Lessons Learned

**Breaking Eggs:** Granholm told Bloomberg she wishes the Biden administration had been more willing to **"break eggs"** like Trump has done—not break laws, but push envelopes and move faster. She advocates telling lawyers **"Don't tell me what I can't do. Tell me what I can do."**

**The Manchin Factor:** When asked about specific eggs she wishes she'd broken, Granholm diplomatically mentions Joe Manchin: **"Poor Joe Manchin. He's a great guy, but boy, did it take a while"** to get the IRA passed. Earlier action might have changed electoral outcomes.

**2026 Midterm Outlook:** Granholm is optimistic, noting Democrats have won special elections since Trump took office by an average of 13 points. She predicts Democrats will **"definitely"** take the House and has 50/50 odds on the Senate (requiring four seat pickups in red states).

**The Carville Doctrine:** Quoting James Carville: **"Don't just do something, stand there."** When your opponent is drowning, throw him an anvil. But she acknowledges this defensive posture won't be enough for 2028—Democrats need a positive vision.

**2028 Platform:** Affordability and cost reduction will be central, as demonstrated by recent gubernatorial wins. New Jersey Governor Mikey Sherrill's electricity rate freeze was popular. Granholm promises **"a whole array of initiatives"** on affordability, education, international standing, and anti-corruption.

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Petrostate vs. Electrostate Framework

**The Concept:** Granholm frames the competition as **"the United States is the petrostate and China the electrostate."** This captures the fundamental strategic divergence—the US trying to push fossil fuels globally while China dominates clean energy manufacturing and deployment.

**Application:** This framework helps explain why US policy contradictions matter strategically. By abandoning clean energy competitiveness, the US cedes the future economy to China while trying to sell products (oil and LNG) that customers increasingly want to avoid.

### Energy Sovereignty Through Domestic Resources

**The Principle:** After Ukraine and now the Strait of Hormuz, nations are learning that **true energy security comes not from diversifying suppliers but from diversifying energy types**—specifically, building domestic renewable capacity.

**The Mechanism:** Geopolitical disruptions → supply unreliability + price volatility → recognition that imported fossil fuels are structurally risky → investment in domestic renewables and electrification → energy sovereignty.

**Application:** This explains why even Trump's fossil fuel push may backfire globally. Asian nations experiencing the Hormuz crisis will accelerate electrification and renewables, not increase fossil fuel dependence. Some may use coal as a bridge, but **"coal is not going to be the long-term solution."**

### The Rockets and Feathers Pricing Model

**The Concept:** Energy prices **"go up like a rocket and come down like feathers."** This asymmetry means price spikes hit consumers quickly while relief takes months.

**Application:** Global oil price signals take months to fully work through to US pumps due to stockpiles and lags in the system. This means **inflation from the Hormuz crisis is "baked in"** regardless of near-term resolution, with political consequences extending through 2026 and beyond.

### The Safe Harbor Incentive Structure

**How It Works:** Projects that began construction (moved dirt) before the July deadline retain full tax credits even as new projects lose them. This creates a **pipeline of locked-in renewable projects** that will deploy over coming years.

**The Stacking Effect:** Base 30% transferable tax credit + 10% energy community bonus + 10% domestic content bonus = **50% project subsidy**. This math still makes many US clean energy projects highly competitive internationally.

**Application:** Investors like EDP are still putting 60% of renewable budgets into the US because safe-harbored projects with stacked incentives remain **"irresistible"** despite headline policy reversals.

## 4. Contrarian Takes

### Fossil Fuels Are Now Structurally Unreliable

**The Contrarian View:** Rather than framing fossil fuels as reliable baseload energy disrupted by occasional geopolitical events, Granholm argues we've entered **"the era of fossil fuel unreliability."** The volatility itself—not just supply disruptions—makes them unreliable.

**The Reasoning:** Price doubling projections, insurance unavailability for key shipping routes, geopolitical weaponization, and the multiplication of chokepoint risks mean fossil fuels can no longer serve as the foundation for energy security. This represents a fundamental regime change.

### Trump's Policies May Accelerate Global Clean Energy

**The Paradox:** While devastating for US competitiveness, Trump's actions may inadvertently **accelerate the global clean energy transition**. By making the US an unreliable ally, driving up fossil fuel prices, and demonstrating energy nationalism, he's teaching the world that energy sovereignty requires domestic renewables.

**The Evidence:** Allies are making deals with BYD for EVs. Asian nations are accelerating electrification after Hormuz. Europe doubled down on renewables after Ukraine. **"The lesson is that the traditional suppliers are not reliable. You got to build your own."**

### The IRA Survived Better Than Headlines Suggest

**Against Consensus:** While media coverage emphasized the dismantling of Biden climate policy, Granholm argues **"It's better news than people think."** The manufacturing incentives, battery storage support, firm clean power incentives, and safe-harbored projects mean 2025-2026 will see near-record clean energy deployment.

**The Nuance:** Battery manufacturers are pivoting from EVs to utility storage. Projects are still economic with 50% stacked tax credits. Data center demand is driving deployment regardless of some lost incentives. The result: **"It hasn't completely wiped it out."**

### Manufacturing Competitiveness Requires Export Subsidies

**The Insight:** Granholm emphasizes that IRA tax breaks for exported products were crucial for US manufacturing competitiveness—a form of **hidden trade finance** that made US factories viable against Chinese competitors. Removing this while imposing tariffs is counterproductive.

**The Implication:** This challenges the Trump administration's tariff-centric approach to manufacturing. You can't tariff your way to competitiveness; you need to subsidize production costs to compete globally, especially against China's integrated supply chains and scale.

### Democrats Should Learn to "Break Eggs"

**The Self-Critique:** Granholm's admission that Democrats should have been bolder and faster—willing to push legal boundaries and ignore lawyers saying "you can't"—challenges the party's traditional caution and process-orientation.

**The Standard:** She holds up Trump's Project 2025 preparation and rapid execution as a model for **speed and boldness** (if not legality or substance). Democrats need their own **"Deploy! Deploy! Deploy!"** mentality with better preparation for 2029.

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Markets and Investing

**Battery Storage Opportunity:** With incentives intact and manufacturers pivoting from EVs to utility storage, this sector remains highly attractive. The shift from "solar plus storage" to **"storage plus solar"** reflects the changing economics.

**Safe Harbor Projects:** Renewable projects that achieved safe harbor status before July deadlines retain full incentive stacks (up to 50% tax credits). These represent **locked-in value** regardless of policy headlines.

**Data Center Clean Energy:** Tech companies with net-zero commitments are deploying solar and storage at scale to power AI infrastructure. This demand is **policy-resistant** and will continue driving clean energy deployment.

**US Manufacturing Caution:** Without export incentives, US clean energy manufacturing faces structural disadvantages against China. Investors should be cautious about US manufacturing plays that depend on domestic-only markets, especially in EVs.

**Global EV Markets:** BYD and Chinese manufacturers are gaining ground in allied markets (UK, EU, Canada) as US automakers lose competitiveness. **"Four years of this and we will be out of the EV game entirely."**

**Fossil Fuel Volatility:** Natural gas prices projected to double 2024-2026. Oil price volatility and insurance costs for shipping through chokepoints create structural unreliability. This favors **diversification away from fossil fuel exposure**.

### For Understanding Macro

**Inflation Trajectory:** Energy price increases from Hormuz are **"baked in"** due to rockets-and-feathers dynamics and system lags. Expect inflation to remain elevated (above 3%) through 2026 with political consequences.

**Deglobalization of Energy:** The era of fungible global oil markets is ending. Regional energy systems will increasingly rely on **domestic resources and bilateral arrangements** rather than global commodity markets.

**China's Strategic Advantage:** US policy is inadvertently **"giving up the store to China"** in EVs, batteries, and clean energy manufacturing—the growth industries of the 21st century—while trying to defend 20th-century fossil fuel markets.

**Asian Energy Transition Acceleration:** The Strait of Hormuz crisis will drive Asian nations toward electrification and renewables faster than previously projected, similar to Europe's response to Ukraine. Some coal bridging, but **long-term trajectory is clear**.

**Political Volatility:** Energy price increases, inflation persistence, and visible policy contradictions create conditions for significant political swings in 2026 midterms and 2028 presidential election.

### For Policy and Advocacy

**Preparation for 2029:** Democrats need a **Project 2025 equivalent**—detailed plans ready for rapid implementation, willingness to push legal boundaries, and **"Deploy! Deploy! Deploy!"** mentality rather than cautious incrementalism.

**Messaging on Costs:** Affordability and cost reduction must be central to political messaging. Specific, visible actions like electricity rate freezes resonate more than abstract climate benefits.

**Manufacturing Strategy:** Future clean energy policy must include **export support**, not just domestic incentives, to compete globally. Tariffs alone cannot build competitiveness against integrated Chinese supply chains.

**Energy Sovereignty Framing:** The **"energy sovereignty through domestic renewables"** message resonates globally and should be emphasized over climate abstractions. It appeals to security, economic, and independence values.

**Communication Timing:** Granholm's regret about "groundbreakings not ribbon cuttings" highlights the need to **time policy benefits to be visible before elections**. Front-load visible wins rather than long-term investments.

### Actionable Takeaways

1. **Investors:** Focus on battery storage, safe-harbored renewable projects, and data center energy solutions. Be cautious on US EV manufacturing and fossil fuel price stability.

2. **Corporate Energy Buyers:** Lock in renewable PPAs while safe harbor projects retain full incentives. Explore demand flexibility and storage to maximize value and grid integration.

3. **Policy Advocates:** Prepare detailed implementation plans for 2029. Frame energy policy around affordability, sovereignty, and manufacturing rather than climate alone.

4. **International Actors:** Recognize US unreliability as both ally and energy supplier. Accelerate domestic renewable capacity and diversify away from fossil fuel imports.

5. **Political Strategists:** Focus 2026 messaging on costs and Trump policy contradictions. Develop positive 2028 vision around affordability, manufacturing, and energy security.
