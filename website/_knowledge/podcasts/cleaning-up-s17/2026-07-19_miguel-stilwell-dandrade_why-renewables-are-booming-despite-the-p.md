# Why Renewables Are Booming Despite the Politics: A Conversation with Miguel Stilwell d'Andrade

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest Background:** Miguel Stilwell d'Andrade is CEO of EDP and EDP Renewables, one of the world's leading clean energy companies. EDP operates across 20+ countries with a portfolio split roughly one-third networks (electricity distribution) and two-thirds renewables (hydro, wind, solar). The company has undergone a remarkable transformation from 80% thermal generation 20 years ago to over 90% renewable generation today—making it arguably the "greenest" integrated European utility.

**Main Topics:**
- The disconnect between political rhetoric and on-the-ground energy market realities
- Massive electricity demand growth driven by data centers and reindustrialization
- EDP's strategic investment focus (60% in US renewables)
- Energy affordability and competitiveness in Europe vs. the US
- Lessons from Portugal's April 2024 blackout
- The role of innovation and digitization in grid resilience

**Key Thesis:** Despite political headwinds and anti-renewable rhetoric, fundamental economics and surging electricity demand are driving unprecedented investment in renewable energy, particularly in the United States. The key is separating sentiment from reality on the ground.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Demand Revolution in US Power Markets

**The Question:** How can EDP be so bullish on US renewables given the political environment?

**Miguel's Perspective:** There's a critical distinction between political rhetoric and market fundamentals. While sentiment around renewables has been negative, **actual demand for power in the US is "phenomenal"**—representing a paradigm shift from decades of flat or declining demand.

**Key Insights:**
- Demand growth is driven by **data centers and reindustrialization**
- This has created **"some of the best projects and returns over the last 20 years"** in the US market
- Power purchase agreement (PPA) prices have risen from ~$30/MWh to ~$70/MWh
- The "big beautiful bill" from the Trump administration **preserved tax credits for renewables through 2030** (with safe harbor provisions)

**Supporting Evidence:** EDP is allocating 60% of its renewable investment to the US, which will increase the US share of their renewables business from 50-55% to approximately 65-67%.

### The Safe Harbor Mechanism

**Framework Explained:** Miguel detailed how the US tax credit system works:
- **Safe harboring** means if you procure equipment or start building by a certain date, you qualify for tax credits even if the project isn't completed until four years later
- **Production Tax Credits (PTC)**: Primarily for wind, paid over 10 years (~$25/MWh, inflated)
- **Investment Tax Credits (ITC)**: Primarily for solar, recovers 40-50% of capex upfront

**Application:** EDP has "locked in almost 5 gigawatts of projects" that can be built over the next few years with tax credits secured.

### The Price Inflation Paradox

**The Challenge:** PPAs have more than doubled from ~$30 to ~$70/MWh. Isn't this inflationary?

**Miguel's Explanation:** The increase isn't pure margin expansion—it reflects fundamental cost changes:
- **Cost of capital** went from near-zero (or negative in Europe) to significantly positive
- **Capex increased** due to tariffs and supply chain issues
- In the US, solar panels now cost **3x more than in Europe** due to tariffs
- Developer margins have "improved slightly" but most of the price increase is absorbed by higher costs

**Contrarian Element:** This challenges the narrative that renewable prices only go down. The context matters enormously—comparing pre-COVID zero-interest-rate environment to today's reality is misleading.

### European Energy Competitiveness

**The Debate:** Is Europe doomed to high energy costs?

**Miguel's Nuanced Take:**
- **Europe will never be competitive if dependent on imported gas**—it doesn't produce enough domestically
- **Regional variation matters enormously**: Comparing European averages to US averages is misleading
  - US has California/Northeast (high prices) vs. Texas/Midwest (low prices)
  - Europe has Central Europe (high prices) vs. Scandinavia and Iberia (low prices)
- **Iberia is highly competitive**: Portugal has 75%+ renewable generation with some of Europe's lowest wholesale prices; Spain has 55% renewables with similarly low prices

**The Critical Difference:** 
- US uses **tax credits** (taxpayer subsidizes energy consumer)
- Europe tends to **tax energy** (using energy bills for government revenue)
- This taxation distorts the underlying competitiveness of European renewable energy

**Practical Implication:** Miguel advocates for companies to consider moving factories **within Europe** (e.g., from Central Europe to Iberia) rather than to China or the US, if energy costs are the primary concern.

### Offshore Wind Reality Check

**The Contrast:** While Miguel is bullish on US solar and batteries, he's much more cautious on offshore wind:
- EDP has offshore projects through a 50-50 JV with Engie called Ocean Winds
- They had permits for a Massachusetts project but **paused in 2024**
- "There's been a very strong pushback against that technology in the US"
- The disconnect between rhetoric and reality that applies to solar/batteries **does not apply to offshore wind**

**Onshore Wind:** Also challenging for new projects due to permitting and interconnection issues, though **repowering existing projects** (replacing old turbines with more efficient ones) remains attractive.

### The April 2024 Iberian Blackout

**What Happened:** The entire Iberian Peninsula went offline for approximately 12 hours due to a voltage spike in southwest Spain that caused a cascading failure.

**Miguel's Experience:** 
- He was in a board meeting when it happened—able to set up crisis committee immediately
- First challenge was **diagnosis**: Was it a cyber attack? Foreign attack? Too much solar?
- Portugal was importing ~30% of its power from Spain at that moment (cheap solar)
- When Spain collapsed, Portugal collapsed

**Key Learnings:**

1. **The importance of physical inertia**: Need "hard assets providing inertia, frequency control" to the system—hydro, gas, or batteries
2. **The efficiency vs. resilience tradeoff**: "The pendulum swung very much towards efficiency and lowest cost provider. You need to pay for resilience—sometimes having backups, having additional margin even if it means paying a little bit more."
3. **Sophisticated ancillary services**: Need fast frequency response, synchronous condensers (generators that spin but don't generate most of the time, providing system inertia)
4. **Policing service delivery**: Some power plants allegedly weren't operating to standards
5. **N+1 redundancy insufficient**: System must handle large plants (like 1 GW nuclear) suddenly going offline

**Quote:** Referencing resilience expert Roger Dennis: "Resilience is incredibly expensive except in retrospect."

### Data Centers and Grid Impact

**The Scale:** The Start Campus data center being built next to EDP's decommissioned coal plant will represent **~20% of Portugal's total electricity consumption**—just one facility.

**EDP's Response:**
- Increasing network investment by **70% over the next 5 years** (€3.1 billion in Portuguese networks alone)
- Working on **additionality**: Helping data centers bring their own generation (solar, wind, batteries) so they don't burden existing consumers
- Currently Start Campus is connected to EDP's **distribution network** (not transmission), making them a direct customer

**The Risk:** Miguel worries about affordability backlash—"a domestic consumer is a voter, and a voter will pressure politicians" who will then impose extraordinary taxes, clawbacks, or regulatory interventions that harm the sector long-term.

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Rhetoric vs. Reality Framework

**Core Principle:** Separate political sentiment and media narratives from actual market fundamentals and on-the-ground developments.

**Application:**
- Political rhetoric may be anti-renewable
- But if businesses need power immediately and renewables can be deployed in 12-36 months vs. years for nuclear or limited gas turbine availability
- Then renewables will be built regardless of rhetoric

**Miguel's Observation:** "It's important to separate the sentiment, the rhetoric, with actually what's happening on the ground. On the ground you need power and you need it now."

### The Energy Trilemma Evolution

**Traditional Framework:** Clean, affordable, reliable power

**How Priorities Shift:**
- 2-3 years ago: **Clean** was paramount (ESG boom)
- Now: **Affordable and reliable** dominate the conversation
- The framing has shifted from "energy transition" to "**energy additionality**"—what supply can we bring to market quickly?

**Application:** Renewables win not (just) because they're clean, but because they can be deployed fast and provide energy independence.

### The Interconnection Paradox

**The Observation:** Iberia (Spain + Portugal) is **less interconnected with Europe than Ireland**—despite Ireland being an island.

**The Implication:** Even within Europe's supposedly integrated market, political and historical factors create surprising bottlenecks. France's nuclear fleet may have historically resisted interconnection to avoid competition from Iberian renewables.

**Broader Lesson:** Physical infrastructure reflects political choices, not just engineering optimization.

## 4. Contrarian Takes

### Nuclear Won't Solve Affordability Soon

**The Conventional Wisdom:** Nuclear is the answer to baseload, reliable, clean power.

**Miguel's Pushback:** "Nothing against nuclear, but quite frankly it's not going to solve the problem anytime soon in the next 5-10 years. If you have an affordability issue, nuclear is not going to be the answer anytime soon."

**Reasoning:** Deployment timelines are too long for current demand pressures.

### Gas Is Also Constrained

**The Assumption:** Gas can fill the gap as a flexible backup.

**Miguel's Reality Check:** "Gas to a great extent is also capped in terms of the number of turbines that can be produced and installed in any particular year."

**Implication:** Renewables are actually the **fastest deployable** option at scale.

### Europe Can Be Energy Competitive

**The Consensus:** Europe is doomed to high energy costs (Draghi report, Letta report).

**Miguel's Counterargument:**
- Europe will **never** be competitive if dependent on imported gas
- But **renewables produced in Europe can be highly competitive**
- "The sun shining in Iberia is the same sun as in the US"—and without tariffs, capex is lower
- The problem is **taxation and industrial policy**, not the underlying cost of renewable generation

### Offshore Wind Economics in Europe

**The Narrative:** Offshore wind is prohibitively expensive.

**Miguel's Nuance:** "Offshore if done right and in the right conditions can be a very good source of power." The North Sea is shallow and very windy—an "incredible resource."

**The Caveat:** It may only become "tremendous" once fully depreciated around 2050. Current costs (~€100/MWh) reflect higher capex and financing costs than the pre-COVID era (~€50/MWh).

### The Coal-to-Renewables Transformation Is Proven

**The Skepticism:** You can't reliably run a grid on 90%+ renewables.

**The Evidence:** EDP went from 80% thermal (mostly coal) to 92-93% renewable generation, while Portugal maintained some of Europe's **lowest wholesale prices**.

**Miguel's Claim:** "One of the good case studies of the energy transition actually lived as a company."

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Investors

**Capital Allocation:**
- **US renewables** receiving 60% of EDP's renewable investment despite political uncertainty
- Focus on **solar and batteries** rather than offshore wind in US
- **Repowering** existing wind projects (replacing old turbines) remains attractive

**Returns Environment:**
- "Some of the best projects and returns over the last 20 years" in the US currently
- But understand that higher PPA prices reflect higher costs, not just margin expansion

**Capital Markets Receptivity:**
- Long-term investors "see the macro trend in energy, in AI, in digital"
- EDP has successfully raised €4+ billion through EDPR vehicle over the years
- Current market sentiment is "very excited about the sector" due to demand growth

**Risk Factors:**
- **Affordability backlash** leading to regulatory intervention (clawbacks, extraordinary taxes, price caps)
- **Offshore wind** remains challenged, particularly in US
- **Onshore wind** new builds difficult due to permitting/interconnection

### For Understanding Macro

**Demand Dynamics:**
- Paradigm shift from flat/declining electricity demand to **material growth**
- Data centers are the primary driver (one facility = 20% of Portugal's consumption)
- Reindustrialization adding to demand pressure

**Inflation Dynamics:**
- Energy price inflation driven by:
  - Cost of capital normalization (from zero/negative)
  - Supply chain disruption and tariffs
  - Not primarily demand-pull inflation
- Expect **LNG glut** to potentially ease gas prices (though not good for climate)

**Geopolitical:**
- **Energy independence** increasingly valued over pure cost optimization
- Europe's dependence on imported gas is structural competitive disadvantage
- US tax credit system vs. European taxation creates different incentive structures

### For Grid Operators and Policymakers

**Resilience Investments:**
- Need to shift from pure **efficiency optimization** to **resilience**
- Invest in:
  - Physical inertia (hydro, gas, batteries, synchronous condensers)
  - Sophisticated ancillary services (fast frequency response)
  - N+2 or greater redundancy
  - Real-time monitoring and data systems

**Data Center Policy:**
- Require **additionality**—data centers must bring their own generation
- Avoid socializing costs to domestic consumers
- Invest heavily in distribution networks (EDP: +70% over 5 years)

**Interconnection:**
- More interconnection improves resilience
- But recognize political barriers (France-Spain example)
- Don't assume integrated markets are actually integrated

### Actionable Takeaways

**For Energy Companies:**
1. **Separate rhetoric from reality** in investment decisions
2. **Invest in automation** of construction and O&M to reduce labor costs
3. **Focus on flexibility** solutions (batteries, demand-side management)
4. **Take long-term view** on affordability to avoid regulatory backlash

**For Industrial Energy Users:**
5. Consider **relocating within regions** to access cheaper renewable power (e.g., Central Europe to Iberia)
6. Explore **additionality** arrangements for large loads

**For Policymakers:**
7. **Pay for resilience** explicitly rather than assuming efficiency = resilience
8. Use **tax credits rather than taxes** on energy to improve competitiveness
9. Require **data centers to bring generation** to avoid grid strain
10. Invest in **distribution networks** not just transmission
