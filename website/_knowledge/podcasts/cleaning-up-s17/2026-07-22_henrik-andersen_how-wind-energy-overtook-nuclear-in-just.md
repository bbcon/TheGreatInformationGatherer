# How Wind Energy Overtook Nuclear in Just Two Decades

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest:** Henrik Andersen, CEO of Vestas - one of the world's leading wind turbine manufacturers

**Background:** Andersen joined Vestas' board as a non-executive director in 2012-2013 during a severe financial crisis, became more involved in executive functions during the turnaround, and was appointed CEO in May 2019. He succeeded Anders Runevad, who now serves as Vestas' chairperson.

**Main Topic:** The remarkable transformation of wind energy from a marginal, subsidy-dependent technology to a mainstream, cost-competitive energy source that now generates ~10% of global electricity - matching and overtaking nuclear power.

**Key Thesis:** Wind energy's success stems from a combination of technological advancement, scale, sophisticated wind resource mapping, and consistent policy frameworks. However, the industry faces new challenges around supply chain resilience, geopolitical tensions, and the need for energy storage and system integration.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Scale and Scope of Vestas

**Company Profile:**
- **125-year history** (though wind turbine manufacturing began in the 1970s during the oil crisis)
- **37,000 employees** across 13 manufacturing countries
- Turbines operating in **80+ countries globally**
- Manufacturing hubs in US, Mexico, Brazil, Denmark, Germany, Poland, Spain, India, and China
- Formed through merger of Neg Micon and Vestas in late 1990s during industry consolidation

**Supply Chain Strategy:**
Andersen explained that Vestas follows a **localization principle for large components** while centralizing technology development:
- **Large physical components** (blades, nacelles, towers) are manufactured close to installation sites to avoid expensive transportation
- Blades now reach **116 meters in length** for offshore applications
- **Technology components** and design work remain centralized, primarily in Denmark
- **Co-design partnerships** with global suppliers (example: 25-year partnership with Korean company CS Wind for towers)
- Sold their Colorado tower factory to CS Wind but maintained capacity agreements

The host challenged whether all turbines are essentially made in Chinese factories under different brands. Andersen firmly rejected this: "That's almost sounds a little bit like a Trump claim. That's not right." He emphasized that components are **designed by Vestas and then sourced** from partners globally, not simply rebranded Chinese products.

### The Cost Revolution: From Subsidy Dependence to Grid Parity

**The Transformation Timeline:**

**2000-2010: The Subsidy Era**
- **99% of wind farms required government subsidies** before 2010
- Created public perception that wind would always need taxpayer support
- 2008 financial crisis led to austerity measures cutting subsidies
- This created an existential crisis for the industry (Vestas' "dark years" 2010-2013)

**2010-2020: The Technology & Scale Decade**
- Industry focused intensely on **reducing levelized cost of energy (LCOE)**
- Two key drivers: **technology advancement and scale**
- By 2020, **less than 1% of projects required subsidies**
- Wind became **cheaper than gas, cheaper than coal** - even before accounting for CO2 costs

Liebreich recalled standing up at a Bloomberg New Energy Finance Summit in 2011-2012 telling the audience to prepare for subsidy-free renewables: "They looked at me like I was mad." The crossover year was **2015** when renewables became truly grid-competitive.

**Current Pricing (2026):**

**Offshore Wind:**
- UK AR7 (Auction Round 7): **~£90/MWh** (~$125/MWh, ~€110/MWh)
- Cleared through competitive reverse auctions
- Benefits from large-scale projects (typically 1 GW at a time)
- Government-controlled seabed with streamlined permitting

**Onshore Wind:**
- Germany: **€65/MWh** (~£55/MWh) - down from €85 just 18 months ago
- US: **~$40/MWh** (with production tax credits factored in)
- Texas: As low as **$25-27/MWh** in some locations
- Egypt: **$30/MWh** in excellent wind regimes near Red Sea
- Saudi Arabia: **$14/MWh** (though Andersen questioned 30-year sustainability at that price)

**The German Example:**
Andersen highlighted Germany's success using what he calls **"the Chinese principle"** - scaling up volume:
- Went from ~1 GW/year to **over 10 GW/year** in last two years
- Price dropped from €85 to €65/MWh without major external changes
- Key insight: **"Scale up, do more volume"** - enables better utilization of cranes, people, logistics
- Consistent construction activity (daily, weekly, monthly) drives costs down

### Beyond Size: The Sophistication of Wind Resource Mapping

The host and Andersen discussed how the industry moved beyond simply making bigger turbines:

**Wind Atlas Evolution:**
- Early days: Having **any wind atlas was a big deal** (press release worthy)
- Now: **Comprehensive global wind mapping** using supercomputers
- Can predict wind characteristics for any point on the map
- Analyze: average wind speed, consistency (avoiding "two days of wind, eight days of no wind"), turbulence, seasonality

**Matching Turbines to Sites:**
- Different turbine designs for different **wind regimes**
- **Digital control systems** optimize performance for specific conditions
- This sophistication, not just scale, drove cost reductions

**Growth Acceleration:**
- First 100 GW of wind: **30 years**
- Next 100 GW: **7 years**
- (For context: Solar now installs **over 1 GW per day** globally, having overtaken wind in installation volume)

### The Interest Rate Impact

**The AR4/AR5 Disaster:**
Andersen provided a masterclass on how interest rates affect infrastructure projects:

- UK's **AR4 cleared at £37/MWh** (in 2012 money, ~£60 in current terms)
- Regarded as huge success at the time
- **AR5 targeted £44/MWh** 
- But interest rates had moved **from zero to 4%**
- Andersen: "I was bold enough to say that will attract no capital"
- **AR5 failed - no bids submitted**
- AR6 eventually cleared in **high £60s**
- Now AR7 at **~£90/MWh**

**Key Insight:** When you can finance 20-year projects at zero interest rates, you have no capital/financing costs. This led to **oversupply of capital and projects that shouldn't have been built**. The interest rate environment fundamentally changed project economics.

**The Importance of Consistency:**
Andersen emphasized that **annual auctions are critical**: "If you have two or three years where you're not building any projects, your competence goes somewhere else in the world. Your infrastructure or your supply chain goes somewhere else. And that's really not helpful."

### The Intermittency Challenge and Storage Solutions

**The Host's Challenge:**
Liebreich raised the "Beast from the East" scenario - a weather pattern from Siberia bringing **two weeks of very low wind and very low temperatures** to the UK. Even with two electric cars and a heat pump, households couldn't get through such a period on stored electricity alone.

**Andersen's Response - The "Energy Mix" Philosophy:**

"**Neither or. It's both.**" Key points:

1. **No single source should dominate:** "Don't do all countries that have gone single sourcing on an energy becomes unreasonable depending on a single source."

2. **Wind characteristics vary:**
   - **Offshore has much more regular wind** than onshore
   - Scotland and coastal areas have more consistent wind
   - Might be lower or higher, but "always wind"

3. **Storage is evolving rapidly:**
   - Denmark example: **95% of new car registrations are electric** (Q1 2026)
   - Many households now have "two batteries in your drive"
   - Should charge during high wind periods
   - But acknowledged this works better in wealthy Denmark with good interconnections

4. **The fundamental difference:**
   - Gas = **molecules**
   - Wind = **electrons**
   - "The higher we can get in electrification, the better wind will work"

5. **Biomass, wind, solar combination** needed
   - No single source should be 100%
   - Must maximize electrification for wind to work optimally

### The Nuclear Question

**Andersen's Surprising Position:**

As CEO of a wind company, Andersen made a **pro-nuclear argument**:

"**I'm in favor of all energy sources** because right now the biggest challenge for Europe was **we let the nuclear competence go because we stopped doing it**."

**The European Energy Security Problem:**

**Nuclear's Limitations:**
Liebreich countered that nuclear is a **terrible solution for backup** - you can't ramp it up for two-week wind lulls. 

**The Pragmatic Solution:**
Liebreich proposed his "**pragmatic climate reset**" - keeping **some fossil backup** for rare low-wind/low-solar periods. Andersen agreed: "If we can make the fossil last longer, why not spend average less of it, but make it last... a century rather than a few decades more, then we are better off."

Andersen noted that while wind went from 1% to 10% of **electricity**, it's still less than **2% of global energy supply**. "There's **98% to go**. So before you can't sleep over the two weeks where the wind is not blowing, we got to make the 98% last longer and stretch the wind in to cover a bigger thing than 2%."

(Note: Liebreich smiled at this, noting the "primary energy fallacy" - the 98% includes thermal waste from fossil fuel combustion, but they moved on.)

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The "Chinese Principle" for Cost Reduction

**Framework:** Scale + Consistency = Lower Costs

**Application:**
- Build **high volume annually**
- Ensure **constant construction activity** (daily, weekly, monthly)
- Results in better utilization of:
  - Cranes
  - Labor
  - Logistics infrastructure
- Germany demonstrated this: volume increase drove 24% cost reduction (€85→€65) in 18 months

### The Energy Mix Philosophy

**Framework:** Diversity + Electrification = Resilience

**Key Principles:**
1. **No single source dominance** - avoid dependency
2. **Match sources to characteristics:**
   - Wind (electrons) works best with high electrification
   - Gas (molecules) for backup and non-electric uses
   - Nuclear for baseload (where competence exists)
   - Biomass for flexibility
3. **Storage bridges intermittency**
4. **Interconnections provide geographic diversity**

**Application:** Policy makers should avoid "black and white" thinking and build **complementary systems** rather than choosing single solutions.

### The Auction System for Price Discovery

**Framework:** Regular, Predictable Auctions = Optimal Pricing

**Key Elements:**
1. **Reverse auction** mechanism (suppliers bid down)
2. **Regular schedule** (not sporadic)
3. **Normalized volumes** each year
4. **Long-term visibility** for supply chain

**Benefits:**
- Prevents subsidy waste
- Maintains supply chain readiness
- Drives continuous cost improvement
- Provides investment certainty

**Anti-pattern:** Multi-year gaps in auctions lead to supply chain migration and competence loss.

## 5. Practical Implications

**2. Interest Rate Sensitivity:**
- Recognize that infrastructure projects are **highly sensitive to financing costs**
- When rates rise from 0% to 4%, project economics fundamentally change
- Auction strike prices must adjust to interest rate environment

**3. Energy Mix Strategy:**
- Avoid **single-source dependency**

## Myth-Busting Section

The conversation included a rapid-fire myth-busting segment:

**Myth 1: Wind is intermittent so it's pointless**
- **Reality:** Offshore wind is much more consistent; storage technology advancing rapidly; energy mix approach solves this

**Myth 2: Wind turbines never pay back their carbon debt**
- **Reality:** Carbon neutral in **5-6 months**, then 29.5 years of carbon displacement (better than solar's 10-16 months)

**Myth 3: Turbine blades end up in landfill**
- **Reality:** Three recycling methods exist: 1) Crush and use in concrete/roads, 2) Decompose to original components (fiber, resin, carbon) for reuse, 3) Repurpose structures (though this is not scalable)

**Myth 4: Wind turbines kill birds**
- **Reality:** "Your cats and your skyscrapers are killing more birds than the wind." Larger birds are intelligent enough to circumvent turbines; offshore data shows birds navigate around wind farms

**Myth 5: Wind turbines cause cancer**
- **Reality:** Andersen: "I can't say how disgusted I am to hear about something because what if somebody even believed in that nonsense? It's not true, it's not factual, and it is definitely not right."
