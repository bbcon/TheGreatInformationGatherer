# Can a Transatlantic Electricity Cable Connect North America & Europe?

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest:** Laurent Segalen - co-host of Redefining Energy podcast, founder of Megawatt X clean energy investment platform, and veteran energy finance professional with experience at PWC, Natixis, Lehman Brothers, and Nomura Bank.

**Main Topic:** The feasibility and economics of long-distance electricity interconnectors, with particular focus on NATOL (North Atlantic Transmission One Link) - an ambitious project to connect Canadian and European power markets via a 5,000 km subsea cable.

**Key Thesis:** Energy sources scale not when discovered, but when society learns how to move and store them. Long-distance interconnectors leveraging existing offshore wind supply chains could provide energy security, reduce emissions, and create profitable arbitrage opportunities by exploiting time zone differences and weather pattern variations.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Origins of Segalen's Energy Security Focus

Segalen's career motivations stem from three formative experiences:

- **Age 13:** Visiting the East-West German border and witnessing Europe "scarred and separated," with Russian soldiers authorized to shoot border-crossers - instilling a deep concern for **security**
- **Age 15:** Cleaning oil-soaked beaches in Brittany after a major tanker spill, witnessing public fury at the French administration siding with oil company Amoco (later BP) - establishing his **environmental credentials**
- **9/11:** Losing two PWC colleagues (Cartel Bartels and Adam White) who were designing carbon markets for America and Europe - reinforcing the **security dimension** of energy policy

These experiences shaped Segalen's view that energy transition must address both environmental and geopolitical security concerns.

### The Uranium Trading Adventure

Segalen recounts an illuminating story about becoming Nomura's uranium trader almost by accident:

**The Setup:** Post-2008, the carbon market had collapsed to €1/ton. Seeing Goldman Sachs and Dutch banks trading uranium, Segalen convinced his boss (a former metals head) to enter the physical uranium market.

**The Key Insight:** Other banks traded uranium futures but avoided physical delivery. Segalen identified that **controlling physical storage** created market power in an illiquid commodity.

**The Execution:** 
- Used his French language skills to charm the manager of a French nuclear facility warehouse, securing official storage capacity
- Within 24 hours of obtaining a storage number, major miners (BHP, Rio Tinto, Paladin, Cameco) called wanting to sell
- Having no price discovery, he accidentally bought high and sold low to himself, building a position for ~$50 million
- The phone went silent for two months, causing risk management concern
- Suddenly buyers returned wanting to repurchase at higher prices ($45-52 vs. his $38 purchase price)
- Made $30-40 million profit

**The Punchline:** Six months later, Rio Tinto announced their Ranger mine in Australia had flooded. Segalen had unknowingly entered a long market that became short due to supply disruption. As he admits: "I was the luckiest and most useless uranium trader you ever saw."

**The Lesson:** This experience taught him that **physical storage and logistics create arbitrage value** - a principle that applies directly to electricity interconnectors and batteries today.

### The Economics of Interconnectors

Segalen explains why interconnectors are fundamentally profitable infrastructure:

**The Space-Time Framework:**
- Energy scales when you can **move it (space)** and **store it (time)**
- Interconnectors address the space dimension
- Batteries address the time dimension
- **Batteries pair well with solar**; **interconnectors pair well with wind** (which blows continuously for days then stops)

**The East-West Advantage:**
Host Worthington asks about the key economic metric for interconnectors (equivalent to $/MWh for generation).

Segalen explains:
- **Capex cost:** ~€10/MWh for a small interconnector
- **Revenue threshold:** Any price differential >€10/MWh makes the cable economically viable
- **Revenue stacking:** Capacity markets, ancillary services, congestion revenue, and energy arbitrage
- **Critical insight:** East-west cables exploit **Earth's rotation** - peak demand moves westward, creating continuous arbitrage opportunities
- North-south cables struggle because solar peaks occur simultaneously at the same longitude

**Example:** A Spain-UK cable wouldn't work well because "your noon is my noon - we both have negative prices from solar." But an Ireland-UK cable works because wind patterns are offset by 4 hours as weather systems cross the Irish Sea.

### Regulatory Innovation: Cap and Floor

Segalen describes the UK's **cap and floor regime** as world-leading financial innovation:

**How it works:**
- **Floor:** 3% return on equity guaranteed - enables debt financing even in worst-case scenarios
- **Cap:** 10% return on equity - excess returns above this are returned to ratepayers
- **Key innovation:** Getting both Ireland and UK to adopt the same regime, allowing **unified debt financing** rather than separate debt pools for each jurisdiction

**The Ireland-UK Success:**
Segalen brokered the Greenlink interconnector (Dublin-Liverpool):
- Had historical P&L data from the existing East-West interconnector (built early 1990s)
- The EuroTunnel interconnector (completed 2022) was supposed to pay for itself in 20 years - **it paid for itself in 20 months**
- Aligned regulatory regimes on both sides enabled efficient financing

### The NATOL Project: Connecting Canada to Europe

**The Genesis:**
Four years ago, watching the Ukraine war unfold and the Nord Stream pipeline sabotage, Segalen had an epiphany: "Pipelines are dating, cables are marriage - you need trust." 

Seeing Germany's trust in Russia shattered, he wondered: "Why not connect North America to Europe for energy security?"

**Initial Research:**
- Googled extensively - found no one working on it
- Discovered the 1866 telegraph cable: Newfoundland to Ireland, 3,500 km
- Realized the distance was comparable to existing projects (Morocco-UK proposals)
- Called his partner Simon (the "Excel sheet guy" to Segalen's "dreamer") who confirmed after 10 days: "maybe it's doable"

**Technical Feasibility:**

People might wonder: "How do you lay a 5,000 km electricity cable?"

Segalen's answer reveals **incremental innovation rather than breakthrough technology:**

- **Supply chain:** "Not really a problem bizarrely" - National Grid and cable manufacturers say "yeah, long, yeah, deep" without being fazed
- **Installation method:** Cable-laying vessels do 200 km sections, then a second boat links them together
- **Depth:** Need to reach 4,000m and avoid trenches; Sardinia-Sicily cable already goes to 3,000m - "I'm only 25% away"
- **Voltage evolution:** Current generation is 600,000 volts; need 800,000 volts for 5-6% transmission losses over 5,000 km (Chinese already at 1 million volts)
- **Key point:** "It's not like I discovered something wonderful in my lab and need to build a factory of 100,000 football fields. No, it's incremental - **I'm in the zone**."

**The Critical Question: Weather Correlation**

The fundamental economic question: Does wind blow at the same time in the North Sea and Eastern Canada?

Segalen credits **Ember** (climate think tank) with providing the data. Dave Jones and team published research showing:
- Wind patterns are **not correlated**
- Cold snaps are not correlated
- Heat waves are not correlated  
- Hydro systems (rainy vs. wet years) are not correlated
- **"It is a perfect arbitrage in terms of resources. It's perfect."**

**Current Status:**
- Physical delivery: "Bizarrely, I kind of know how to do it"
- Next phase: **Building a revenue model where legal/regulatory frameworks on both sides converge**
- Landing points: North Sea mesh network with artificial islands (Europe); Quebec or Maritime provinces like Labrador/Nova Scotia (Canada)
- Using French language skills again: "I go there and speak French to the Quebecois"

**Political Context:**
Worthington notes the timing is perfect - Mark Carney signaling Canada looking beyond its southern neighbor for partnerships. A Canada-UK "marriage" on clean energy makes geopolitical sense as a symbol of transatlantic solidarity.

### Carbon Markets: Lessons from the EU ETS

Worthington, who worked on EU carbon policy from a civil society perspective, asks about the early days when **overallocation** was a major concern.

**Segalen's perspective on why it happened:**

"Everybody cooked their books and said emissions would go up." The deal to get Germany onboard was giving free allowances initially - making progress "very very slow."

**Why it worked despite flaws:**
- **Industry support was essential:** "The environmental movement alone would not have pulled it out if we didn't have industry behind us"
- There was surplus power capacity, so utilities supported it as a way to decrease demand, especially the most polluting demand
- Steel and cement industries got free allowances to address competitiveness concerns (debate continues today with CBAM - Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism)

**The Brussels Politics:**
- Including just utilities would have been "the concern of energy"
- Adding industrial sectors made it "the concern of environment" - **pure politics**
- In retrospect, probably bad politics as it created inadvertent surpluses

**Parallel Policies Problem:**

Worthington asks: Did we even need carbon pricing given the Renewables Directive was providing subsidies?

Segalen's response: **"Nobody saw the cost decline coming"**
- Started in 2012 with no idea clean energy costs would fall so fast
- Governments offered €600/MWh subsidies, then realized production cost was €300
- By the time they reduced subsidies to €300, costs were at €150
- His first solar was $5/watt; now it's $0.09/watt
- Even insiders like him couldn't foresee it

**The Real Target:**
Early carbon prices (€5-20/ton = €5-20/MWh on coal, €10/MWh on gas) were really about **nuclear, gas, and coal** - not renewables. Wind was getting €200/MWh subsidies, so developers ignored the carbon price entirely.

"It was really parallel policies... Would we do it now? Probably not. But at the time it looked like the worst option, with the exception of all the others."

**Germany's Nuclear Decision:**
If the policy was meant to reward nuclear and gas, Germany's sovereign decision to shut nuclear and rely on gas (trusting Russian supply) undermined the carbon market's intended effect.

### Sodium Batteries: The Next Wave?

Segalen owns mobile batteries (in partnership with Conquest fund) that are rented to:
- Distribution network operators (DNOs) in winter for grid support
- EV charging stations
- Construction sites
- **New market:** Pre-charging large battery installations (Zenobe's idea)

**Current approach:** 
- Uses **CATL lithium-ion cells** (Chinese manufacturer that "broke the cost curve")
- Rest of system (inverters, fire suppression, racks, software) built in Europe
- European cybersecurity concerns prevent Chinese software

**When will sodium take off?**

The **lithium price** is the key trigger:
- 2022: Lithium hit $80/kg (crazy high)
- Crashed to $10/kg for 2.5 years
- Now: Back to $20/kg (medium-high)

At $20/kg, **price-sensitive Chinese manufacturers** may switch to sodium.

**Sodium advantages:**
- Can use almost the same production lines as lithium batteries (unlike flow batteries)
- Slightly better safety profile
- Lower cost when lithium is expensive

**Sodium disadvantages:**
- Less energy dense - batteries get bigger
- Doesn't work for cars
- **Works for ESS (Energy Storage Systems)** - grid-scale applications

**The Safety Debate:**

Segalen makes a crucial point: **"Every time you have a battery burning, there's always nickel in the battery."**

- Nickel is super dense (great for packing energy)
- Nickel burns
- You coat nickel with **cobalt** to prevent burning
- But cobalt faces "poor child labor in Congo" criticism

**Segalen's "conspirationist" take:**
- "For one cobalt mine there's a thousand illegal gold mines and nobody talks about it"
- No demonstrations at Tiffany's about gold sourcing
- Nobody asks ExxonMobil where their cobalt comes from (half the world's cobalt goes to oil refineries)
- "The attack on cobalt is a scandal" - he sees it as part of anti-EV narrative

Worthington agrees, noting the **absurd anti-EV stories** in Europe, including claims that EVs would be too heavy for multi-story car parks (ignoring that people already buy heavy SUVs).

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Movement and Storage Framework

**Core principle:** "An energy source scales not when it's discovered, but when people know how to move it and store it."

**Application:**
- **Space (movement):** Interconnectors, pipelines, transmission
- **Time (storage):** Batteries, pumped hydro, thermal storage
- **Technology pairing:** 
  - Solar + batteries = natural fit (predictable daily cycle)
  - Wind + interconnectors = better economics (multi-day weather patterns)

### The East-West vs. North-South Cable Economics

**East-West cables win** because:
- Exploit Earth's rotation
- Peak demand moves westward
- Continuous arbitrage as time zones shift
- Example: 1-hour time difference between France-UK creates significant trading opportunities

**North-South cables struggle** because:
- Solar peaks occur simultaneously at same longitude
- Less weather pattern differentiation
- "Your noon is my noon - we both have negative prices"

### The Dating vs. Marriage Analogy

**Pipelines = Dating:** Flexible, can be ended, but requires ongoing trust

**Cables = Marriage:** Long-term commitment, requires deep trust, but creates durable connection

**Application to geopolitics:** 
- Germany's "marriage" to Russian gas via Nord Stream pipelines failed when trust broke
- Transatlantic electricity cable would be a "marriage" between Canada and Europe based on shared democratic values

### The 25% Rule for Innovation

Segalen repeatedly notes he's "never far away" - about **25% beyond current capabilities:**
- Current deepest cable: 3,000m; needs 4,000m
- Current longest cable: ~3,500 km; needs 5,000 km
- Current voltage: 600,000V; needs 800,000V

**Key insight:** Successful infrastructure innovation is **incremental**, not revolutionary. "I'm in the zone" - close enough to existing technology that supply chains, expertise, and financing models already exist.

### Revenue Stacking for Infrastructure

Interconnectors don't rely on single revenue stream:
- **Capacity markets:** Selling firm capacity year-ahead, months-ahead, day-ahead
- **Energy arbitrage:** Price differentials between markets
- **Ancillary services:** Grid stability, frequency regulation
- **Congestion revenue:** Payments when transmission is constrained

**Application:** This multi-revenue model reduces risk and enables project financing with cap-and-floor regulatory support.

## 4. Contrarian Takes

### "I was the most useless uranium trader you ever saw"

Segalen's self-deprecating story about making $30-40M profit through sheer luck challenges the **myth of trader genius**. His success came from:
- Identifying a structural market inefficiency (lack of physical storage)
- Relationship building (flowers and chocolates to warehouse manager)
- Being in the right place when external shock (mine flooding) hit

**Implication:** Sometimes market success is about **positioning and infrastructure** rather than analytical brilliance.

### "The attack on cobalt is a scandal"

While acknowledging child labor concerns, Segalen argues the focus on cobalt in batteries while ignoring:
- Cobalt in oil refining (half of global cobalt use)
- Illegal gold mining (1,000x more prevalent than cobalt mining)
- Other supply chain issues

**His view:** This represents **selective outrage designed to slow EV adoption** rather than genuine concern for Congolese workers.

### Carbon Markets: "Would we do it now? Probably not"

Despite being one of the architects of EU ETS, Segalen admits the policy was flawed:
- Overallocation to industry
- Parallel subsidy policies that made carbon price irrelevant for renewables
- **But:** "It was the worst option, with the exception of all the others"

**Implication:** Perfect policy is impossible; **workable policy that gets industry support** is what matters.

### "Michael Liebreich is 75% right, but I'm still 20% Vaclav Smil"

Segalen positions himself in the energy transition debate:
- **Vaclav Smil camp:** Energy transitions are slow, additions not replacements, "great historian but gets the future wrong"
- **Clayton Christensen camp:** Disruption theory, S-curves, tech displacement (spiritual heir: Michael Liebreich)
- **Daniel Yergin:** "Son of Smil," prudent transition, fossil fuels for 300 years

**Segalen's cocktail:** Mostly Liebreich (rapid disruption) but acknowledges **"inertia, incumbency, corruption, stupidity"** - the human factors that create delay.

**His assessment:** "The past 15 years, [Liebreich has] got it more right than all the others."

### Clean Energy is "Clean" in Multiple Ways

Beyond environmental benefits, Segalen argues clean energy is:
- **Cleaner sector:** Less corruption, more transparent supply chains
- **Manufactured energy:** Benefits from production learning curves
- **Faster iteration:** Learning cycles accelerate cost reduction

**Implication:** The **"tragedy of incumbency"** (established fossil fuel power and political influence) is being overcome by the **efficiency advantages of manufactured energy**.

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Energy Infrastructure Investment

**Key criteria for interconnector viability:**
1. **Distance feasibility:** Within 25% of existing technical capabilities
2. **Weather correlation:** Low correlation = high arbitrage value
3. **Regulatory alignment:** Both jurisdictions must have compatible frameworks
4. **Revenue certainty:** Cap-and-floor or similar mechanisms to enable debt financing
5. **Supply chain readiness:** Leverage existing offshore wind installation capabilities

**Red flags:**
- North-south cables (less arbitrage potential)
- Misaligned regulatory regimes requiring separate debt pools
- Revolutionary technology requirements (vs. incremental innovation)

### For Battery Storage Investors

**Market segmentation:**
- **Mobile batteries:** Growing market for rental to DNOs, EV charging, construction, pre-charging large installations
- **Grid-scale storage:** Watch lithium prices - sodium becomes competitive above $20/kg lithium
- **Safety focus:** Avoid nickel-based chemistries for stationary storage; LFP (lithium iron phosphate) is safer

**Supply chain strategy:**
- Use Chinese cells (cost advantage)
- European balance-of-system (cybersecurity, political risk)
- Prismatic 280 amp-hour cells are current standard

### For Understanding Energy Transition Policy

**Lessons from EU ETS:**
1. **Industry support is essential** - environmental movement alone cannot drive policy
2. **Overallocation is politically necessary** to get buy-in, even if economically inefficient
3. **Parallel policies** (carbon pricing + subsidies) may be redundant but politically necessary
4. **Nobody predicted cost declines** - even insiders were shocked by solar/wind cost curves
5. **Bureaucratic momentum** - once established, carbon markets expand regardless of effectiveness

**Current EU expansion to heating/transport:**
Segalen is skeptical but understands the logic: "The moment you have a system, you have an administration, and the first thing an administration does is fight for its own survival."

### For Geopolitical Energy Security

**The NATOL project demonstrates:**
1. **Energy security through diversification:** Transatlantic cable reduces European dependence on any single supplier
2. **Democratic energy alliance:** Canada-Europe connection as alternative to authoritarian energy suppliers
3. **Weather diversification:** Uncorrelated wind/hydro/temperature patterns create resilience
4. **Symbolic value:** Infrastructure as expression of political solidarity

**Broader principle:** **Long-term energy relationships require trust** - cables are "marriages" that work best between stable democracies with aligned interests.

### Actionable Takeaways

**For investors:**
- Look for infrastructure plays within 25% of existing technical capabilities
- Prioritize projects with multiple revenue streams (capacity, energy, ancillary services)
- Seek regulatory alignment between jurisdictions before committing capital
- In batteries, watch lithium prices as trigger for sodium adoption

**For policymakers:**
- Align regulatory frameworks across borders to enable efficient financing
- Cap-and-floor mechanisms balance investor returns with consumer protection
- Industry support is more important than environmental purity for getting policies implemented
- Accept that parallel policies (carbon pricing + subsidies) may be necessary even if redundant

**For understanding energy markets:**
- Physical storage/logistics create value in commodity markets
- East-west interconnectors have structural advantages over north-south
- Weather pattern correlation is critical for interconnector economics
- Manufacturing learning curves are accelerating clean energy cost declines beyond expert predictions
