# Cleaning Up Podcast: Trump, Venezuela & The Future Of Clean Energy

## 1. Episode Overview

**Hosts:** Michael Liebreich and Bryony Worthington kick off Season 17 (2026) with their traditional state-of-the-world discussion, though this episode was recorded on January 6th following dramatic geopolitical events that forced them to scrap an earlier recording.

**Main Topics:**
- US capture of Venezuelan President Maduro and implications for energy markets
- The "electro-tech revolution" and its accelerating impact
- Europe's energy malaise and potential recovery
- The future of clean energy amid political turbulence
- Energy security in an increasingly volatile world

**Key Thesis:** Despite unprecedented geopolitical chaos and political headwinds against climate action, the **electrification revolution is proceeding largely independent of government policy**, driven by economics and energy security concerns. The world is entering a new phase where "all roads to the energy future go through electricity."

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Oil Price Paradox

**The Question:** Why is oil stuck at $60 despite extraordinary geopolitical turbulence?

**Key Insights:**
- Oil remains at ~$60 despite: Russia-Ukraine war destroying 1/3 of Russian refining capacity, US capture of Maduro, Iran riots, Saudi-UAE tensions in Yemen, and US threats to annex Canada (7th largest oil reserves)
- The world economy is **not** weak—strong growth in China, India (4%), Egypt (4%), and Africa
- Liebreich argues this reflects **the waning power of oil** as electric transportation absorbs demand
- China's oil demand fell **5% in 2025**—a massive reduction for the world's largest importer

**Liebreich's Analysis:** "Is oil just going to become a commodity like any other? Is the US playing a script from 30 years ago where oil was the big dominant commodity that everyone had to control, but actually the rest of the world's moving on?"

The hosts note that 30 years ago, the US faced "Hubbert peak" concerns about running out of oil. The fracking revolution changed everything, making the US the world's largest producer and **de-risking energy security**, which paradoxically freed up US foreign policy to be more aggressive.

### Trump's Geopolitical Strategy (or Lack Thereof)

**Two Interpretations Discussed:**

1. **The Narcissist View:** Trump is a "violent sociopath narcissist who will just do whatever it takes to grab the limelight" (Liebreich doesn't fully subscribe to this)

2. **The Strategic View:** There's a genuine **hemispheric strategy** outlined in the October national security document focusing on "our hemisphere" (the Americas), representing a shift from the first Trump administration's China-containment focus

**Worthington's Take:** Trump is "an amazing propagandist" with "an innate sense of how to pick a symbolic action that will completely dominate the world's attention." The Venezuela action diverted attention from domestic problems like the Epstein files.

**Concerning Implication:** If the US focuses on its hemisphere and wants the Ukraine situation to "go away," does this give China carte blanche to take Taiwan? As Worthington asks: "Does that mean China now has the carte blanche to clean up what it would consider its hemisphere?"

### The Fracturing Climate Consensus

**The Shift:**
- 2025 saw the US become openly hostile to climate multilateralism
- US "ripped up" the climate deal at the International Maritime Organization
- No formal US presence at COP30 in Brazil (first time ever)
- Trump administration attempting to block $25 billion in offshore wind

**Worthington's Observation:** There's "increasingly divergence between the politics of climate and then the reality on the ground." The **microeconomics of clean energy and geopolitics of security are ignoring the climate issue**.

**The COP Reality Check:**
Liebreich describes COPs as increasingly "theater of the absurd"—a "performative victim culture of 'Oh, I'm not going first, you should go first'" while real-world clean tech deals proceed regardless.

**Example:** The Tropical Forests Forever Fund announced at COP30 would theoretically generate $2 billion/year for rainforest protection. But the combined GDP of rainforest countries is **$6 trillion**. Liebreich: "I cannot get excited about a theoretical $2 billion per year for rainforest protection arrayed against the forces of economics."

### Europe's Energy Trilemma Awakening

**The Problem:**
Europe has been living in a "fantasy world" pretending there's no energy trilemma (affordable, clean, secure—pick two). The game was: focus on "clean" while making it affordable through **cheap Russian gas** (30-40% of European industrial energy).

**The Zeitenwende (Time Change):**
After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Europe lost its cheap gas but hasn't found a new model. It can do:
- Clean at very high cost
- Secure and clean at very high cost
- But not affordable, clean, AND secure

**Worthington's Nuclear Point:**
Nuclear remains Europe's single biggest source of clean terawatt-hours, yet countries continued phasing out "perfectly good assets" even during an energy crisis. "You've got an existing asset already amortized producing clean terawatt hours, and yet you decide that you can do without that because you're living in a fantasy land of cheap gas and renewables."

**The Hydrogen Mirage:**
Liebreich calls hydrogen "the refuge of fools, dreamers and scoundrels" because it allows politicians to avoid hard decisions by pushing solutions beyond election cycles.

**Shocking Example:** Germany built a 400km hydrogen pipeline (€100 million) that is now filled and compressed with hydrogen but has **nobody feeding in hydrogen and nobody buying hydrogen**. Yet the EU's latest energy package includes **€240 billion for hydrogen pipelines through 2040**.

**Green Shoots:**
Despite the malaise, Liebreich sees reasons for optimism:
- Better understanding that demonizing nuclear "is not a thing anymore" (Germany and Austria's anti-nuclear push failed)
- Europe has "extraordinary assets" including a synchronized grid from Latvia to Cardiff to Ukraine
- Strong companies: Schneider Electric, Wärtsilä, Siemens, Hitachi Europe (former ABB)
- If gas prices stay lower, Europe has time to "use the time wisely and pragmatically"

**Liebreich's verdict:** "I'm going long Europe."

### The Battery Revolution

**2025: The Year Batteries Arrived**

**Key Developments:**
- Grid-connected battery projects moved from hundreds of megawatt-hours to **tens of gigawatt-hours**
- Another **40% cost drop**
- New sodium-ion chemistries promise continued cost reductions
- Major projects announced in Saudi Arabia and globally

**The Pumped Hydro Comparison:**
Liebreich had an "eye-opener" moment challenging his long-held belief that nothing beats pumped hydro for storage costs:

- **Snowy 2.0** (Australia): ~$70/MWh when accounting for real costs (~$20B including grid connections, not the stated $12B)
- **Big battery systems in China:** $80-90/MWh
- **Outside China:** $110-130/MWh

Batteries are only **~50% more expensive** but can be placed **anywhere**—colocated with demand, strategically around grid bottlenecks, without massive grid investments.

**The Learning Curve Advantage:**
Unlike pumped hydro (civil engineering), batteries are **manufactured units with learning curves**. "We ain't done yet" on cost reductions.

**Liebreich's Conversion:** "I can now be that optimistic. It is truly truly transformational."

**Micro to Macro Scale:**
- **Macro:** Australia deploying massive batteries for grid security with high renewable penetration
- **Micro:** Worthington visited a tiny Fiji island running entirely on solar + BYD batteries (no diesel), including desalination—enabled by New Zealand engineering + Chinese batteries

**The Systemic Change:**
Worthington visited Project Bo in Sierra Leone, where a college trains ~20 solar engineers per class. "What are they going to do their whole career? They're going to install this stuff. There's a whole stream of installations that comes out of that college forever."

### The Electro-Tech Revolution

**The System View:**
Building on work by Kingsmill Bond (Ember) and others, the hosts emphasize electrification as a **complete system transformation**, not just individual technologies getting cheaper.

**Three Components:**
1. **New ways of producing electricity:** Wind, solar (nuclear)
2. **New ways of managing it:** Batteries, HVDC, grid investment
3. **New ways of using it:** Transport, heating, industrial heat

**The Leapfrog Opportunity:**
For countries like Pakistan, India, Egypt: "Business people, farmers, homeowners can just say, I can go soup to nuts, A to Z electrically and I'm not going to be dependent on either an unreliable grid or on something that drives up your imports and destroys your balance of payments."

**The Egypt Example:**
Liebreich's analysis: If Egypt has $1 million to spend on energy for a growing economy (4% annual growth, 50% per decade):
- Spending on **oil or gas:** Consumed immediately
- Spending on **wind, solar, or nuclear:** Build an asset that produces **10-15x more energy per dollar** over its lifetime

Wind in Egypt: **$30/MWh**

**Liebreich's Conclusion:** "If their question is should we buy oil and gas or these other things that produce 15 times more energy per dollar, then to me it's a no-brainer."

**The Headroom Argument:**
Worthington highlights a crucial slide from the Electro-Tech deck: Country-by-country analysis shows **massive headroom** for wind and solar penetration. Many countries with good installation capacity, adequate grids, and investment climate are "nowhere near the highest penetration possible."

**The Solar Singularity:**
Liebreich references his 2018 piece on the "solar singularity"—the idea that solar penetration limits are far higher than commonly assumed, especially when considering:
- Industrial applications
- **Cooling** (currently ~10% of global electricity, set to explode as more of the world gets access)
- AI data centers
- Electric transportation

**Chris Wright ("Chris Wrong"):**
The new US Energy Secretary claimed solar and batteries "will never get to 10% even in 50 years"—referring to primary energy (falling into the "Clacks and Alert fallacy"). The hosts note the irony that Wright invested in Ferro Energy (geothermal), Oklo (nuclear), and did part of his masters on solar, yet doesn't understand the battery revolution.

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Energy Investment Multiplier

**Framework:** When evaluating energy investments for growing economies, compare energy output per dollar spent:

- **Fossil fuels (oil/gas):** Consumed immediately, no lasting asset
- **Clean energy assets (wind/solar/nuclear):** Produce **10-15x more energy per dollar** over their lifetime

**Application:** For any country dependent on energy imports with a growing economy, building clean energy assets is economically superior to buying fossil fuels, independent of climate considerations.

### The Energy Trilemma

**Three Goals:**
1. **Affordable**
2. **Clean**
3. **Secure**

**Reality:** You can optimize for two, but achieving all three simultaneously is extremely difficult.

**Europe's Mistake:** Pretended there was no trilemma by focusing on "clean" while secretly relying on cheap Russian gas for "affordable," ignoring "secure."

### The Electrification Staircase

**New Framework** (developed with Adrian Hill, Silvia Madeddu, Tom Butler, Will Drake):

Unlike the "hydrogen ladder" (do/don't do), the **electrification staircase** adds a **time dimension**:
- Some applications electrify now (heat pumps in new homes, EVs)
- Some are harder and take longer (certain industrial processes)
- All will eventually electrify, but at different rates

**Purpose:** Help policymakers and investors understand the sequencing and prioritization of electrification efforts.

### The Leapfrog Concept

**Definition:** Developing countries can skip intermediate technological stages (like landlines to mobile phones) by adopting the latest technology directly.

**Energy Application:** Countries can skip building centralized fossil fuel infrastructure and go straight to distributed solar + batteries + electric transport.

**Why It Works Now:**
- Technology costs have fallen dramatically
- Systems are modular and scalable
- Reduces import dependence
- Provides energy security

### The Souffle Indicator

**Metaphor:** When a technology or policy "souffles," it deflates after being puffed up beyond its actual value or viability.

**Examples:**
- **Hydrogen:** The "hydrogen souffle has souffled pretty hard" as reality meets hype
- **AI data centers:** May be approaching "peak crazy" (though perhaps not until 2027-2028)
- **Stargate IPO:** Rick Perry's company went from $15B to half that value quickly

**Application:** Watch for technologies or investments that are more narrative than substance, especially when they serve to delay hard decisions.

## 5. Practical Implications

### For Investors

**Energy Storage:**
- **Large-scale battery projects** are now competitive with pumped hydro and have better growth prospects
- Look for opportunities in **sodium-ion batteries** as next-generation cost reduction
- Grid-connected batteries moving to **tens of gigawatt-hours** scale

**Geographic Focus:**
- **"Most of the world"** (India, Southeast Asia, Africa, Latin America—70% of global population) offers better growth prospects than mature markets
- These markets will prioritize **energy security and affordability over climate**, making economic clean energy solutions attractive
- Mexico's affordable EV initiative (Alinia, ~$4,500 vehicle) represents a massive market segment

**Avoid:**
- **Hydrogen infrastructure** in Europe (€240B planned spend likely to be stranded)
- **AI data center bubble** may peak 2027-2028
- **Pumped hydro** projects with long timelines and grid connection requirements

**Opportunities:**
- **European electro-tech companies** (Schneider, Siemens, Wärtsilä, Hitachi Europe) positioned for global grid buildout
- **EV supply chains** serving affordable segments in developing markets
- **Solar + battery** systems for distributed generation in emerging markets

### For Understanding Macro

**Oil Price Dynamics:**
- Current $60 oil reflects **structural demand weakness** from electrification, not just cyclical factors
- However, oil remains "only one big shock away" from $100-120
- **Fiscal breakevens** for oil producers (Saudi, Gulf states need $80-90) create instability if prices stay low
- Low oil prices **could accelerate transition** (people want off oil) or **crash economies** (stopping investment)

**Geopolitical Realignment:**
- Watch **China-India relationship** as potential counterweight to Western hemisphere focus
- **Energy security** increasingly driving policy, not climate
- Countries lacking domestic oil (China, India, most of Asia) have strong incentive to electrify rapidly

**The CBAM Effect:**
- Europe's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (started January 1, 2026) creates **real financial liabilities** for importers
- Regarded as "very hostile act" by manufacturing/exporting economies
- May accelerate **non-Western alliances** around trade

**Europe's Trajectory:**
- Near-term: continued malaise, high energy costs
- Medium-term: potential recovery if gas prices stay moderate and pragmatic policies emerge
- Key indicator: whether hydrogen spending actually materializes (€240B planned) or souffles

### For Policy and Business Strategy

**The Electrification Imperative:**
Liebreich: "Amongst policy makers, amongst investors, there is a new understanding that **all roads to the energy future go through electricity**."

**Actionable Priorities:**

1. **For Developing Countries:**
   - Prioritize building clean energy assets over buying fossil fuels (10-15x more energy per dollar)
   - Leapfrog to distributed solar + batteries rather than centralized fossil infrastructure
   - Invest in **training programs** for solar/battery engineers (Sierra Leone model)

2. **For Developed Countries:**
   - Stop retiring existing nuclear assets during energy transitions
   - Focus grid investment on **strategic bottlenecks** rather than blanket upgrades
   - Use AI to optimize **cooling systems** (major upcoming demand driver)

3. **For Energy Security:**
   - Recognize that **electro-tech systems lack buffers** (no coal piles, no gas line pack)
   - Invest in **hard security** for critical infrastructure (undersea cables, etc.)
   - Maintain **pragmatic gas capacity** for security even while building clean energy
   - Address **weather dependency** through storage, overcapacity, and interconnections

4. **For Transportation:**
   - **Truck electrification** is happening faster than expected (China: 10-20% of new trucks/buses)
   - Focus on **affordable EV segments** for mass market (not just premium vehicles)
   - Range anxiety diminishing: 420-mile range vehicles now available

5. **For Industrial Strategy:**
   - **Cooling** will become ~10%+ of electricity demand and growing rapidly
   - **AI data centers** create concentrated demand (gigawatts in small areas) requiring new approaches
   - **Industrial heat electrification** represents massive opportunity

### Specific Actionable Insights

**For European Businesses:**
- Don't assume hydrogen infrastructure will materialize—plan for electrification
- Leverage existing grid advantages for competitive positioning
- Consider nuclear life extensions rather than replacements

**For Emerging Market Governments:**
- Calculate the **energy multiplier** for any energy investment
- Prioritize energy independence through domestic clean energy over import dependence
- Create **training pipelines** for clean energy installation and maintenance

**For Energy Importers:**
- Every dollar spent on imported oil/gas is a dollar not building domestic energy assets
- **Balance of payments** argument for clean energy is stronger than climate argument
- Energy security and economic arguments align with clean energy deployment
