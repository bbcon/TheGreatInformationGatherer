# How the US Makes and Breaks Global Deals: Secretary John Kerry on Diplomacy, Iran, and Climate

## 1. Episode Overview

**Guest Background:** Secretary John Kerry served as US Senator from Massachusetts, 2004 Presidential candidate, Secretary of State under President Obama, and Special Presidential Envoy for Climate under President Biden. He is currently co-chair of Galvanize, a global climate investment initiative.

**Main Topics:** The conversation, recorded live during London Climate Action Week, covers Kerry's experiences negotiating the Iran nuclear deal (JCPOA), the Paris Climate Agreement, and the current state of US diplomatic credibility following the Trump administration's withdrawal from both agreements.

**Key Thesis:** Kerry argues that successful diplomacy requires **relationship-building, mutual respect, and verification over trust**—and that while Trump damaged US credibility, the clean energy transition has gained unstoppable market momentum that transcends political cycles.

## 2. Major Themes Discussed

### The Iran Nuclear Deal: A Masterclass in Diplomacy

**The Question:** How did Kerry negotiate the JCPOA, and what lessons does it offer for current Iran negotiations?

**Kerry's Perspective:** The negotiation took approximately **3 years total**—about a year of secret preliminary work followed by 20 months of formal negotiations after the initial Plan of Action. Kerry emphasized that Iranians are "among all the peoples I've dealt with in the world...the most prideful" with deep historical consciousness of Persian civilization.

**Key Mechanisms:**
- **Secret preliminary phase:** Kerry worked through Sultan Qaboos of Oman, who personally vouched for Obama and Kerry's seriousness to the Ayatollah
- **Relationship building:** Kerry developed personal rapport with Foreign Minister Javad Zarif and Sultan Qaboos (visiting him in Germany during cancer treatment)
- **Strategic focus:** Deliberately limited scope to nuclear issues only, avoiding missiles and regional proxies that would have made agreement impossible
- **Verification architecture:** Changed Reagan's "trust but verify" to **"don't trust and verify"**—implemented 130 foreign inspectors, daily monitoring, cradle-to-grave uranium tracking with cameras, and snap-back sanctions

**Results Achieved:**
- Iran destroyed 15,000-20,000 centrifuges voluntarily
- Cement poured into plutonium reactor at Arak
- Enriched material removed from country
- **Breakout time extended from 2 weeks to over a year**
- Israeli intelligence, along with China, Russia, France, Germany, and Britain all agreed the deal was working

**On Trump's Withdrawal:** Kerry stated bluntly that Trump "just lied about it" when calling it "the worst deal in history" and a "pathway to a nuclear weapon." The current negotiations may achieve something similar to the JCPOA but with **$300 billion in reconstruction aid** (versus the $1.7 billion in frozen Iranian assets returned under the original deal).

### The Paris Climate Agreement: Pre-Cooking Success

**The Framing:** How did Kerry avoid a repeat of the 2009 Copenhagen failure?

**Kerry's Strategy:** "I was determined that we were not going to go to Paris and have a failure. And the way to do that I thought was to **pre-cook with the Chinese** what we would do together."

**The Process:**
- Kerry went to China within weeks of becoming Secretary of State
- Spent a year (2014-2015) negotiating bilateral commitments with President Xi
- The simultaneous announcement in Beijing shocked the world and set the stage for Paris success
- Leveraged the principle of **"common but differentiated responsibilities"**—allowing each country to write its own plan

**The 3 AM Crisis:** Kerry described being woken at 3 AM during Paris negotiations when countries were "freaking out" over differentiated responsibilities. He intervened personally, arguing that the entire Paris structure was "a monument to common but differentiated" since no country could dictate another's commitments.

**Evolution Through COPs:**
- **Glasgow:** Got countries to increase commitments
- **Sharm El Sheikh:** Limited progress (Kerry contracted COVID)
- **Dubai (COP28):** Brought **51-52 oil and gas companies** to sign onto transitioning away from fossil fuels to achieve net zero by 2050

### US Credibility and the Trust Deficit

**The Challenge:** Host Michael Liebreich pressed Kerry on how allies should trust US commitments when administrations can reverse course so dramatically.

**Kerry's Response:** He modified his position from "trust but verify" to essentially arguing that **verification should replace trust entirely**: "It shouldn't be on trust. And you can't get by with that with most professional people."

**On Rebuilding Trust:**
- Kerry leveraged his personal relationships from chairing the Senate Foreign Relations Committee
- Emphasized he wasn't there to "apologize for the past, but to build the future"
- Predicted that trust would rebuild "extremely rapidly" under a new administration
- Acknowledged the fundamental problem: "How can you trust a point of view that's tweeted out at 3:00 in the morning and by 7:00 in the morning it's totally different?"

**The Deeper Issue:** Liebreich pushed back that the problem isn't just Trump—it's that any future administration could similarly abandon commitments. Kerry suggested future voters would "make a fundamental judgment about whether their candidate is crazy or not," but didn't fully address the structural credibility problem.

### The China Challenge: Petro-State vs. Electro-State

**Liebreich's Provocation:** The US is acting as a "petro state" pushing LNG while China dominates clean energy technologies, forcing allies into uncomfortable proximity with an authoritarian regime.

**Kerry's Framework:** "I am a huge believer in the capacity of good diplomacy to change dynamics as they appear. Because I've done it. I've seen it. I know what happens."

**Examples of Successful Engagement:**
- **Syria chemical weapons:** Lavrov called Kerry within an hour of an offhand comment; they negotiated removal of all chemical weapons in 3 days of Geneva talks. The OPCW won the Nobel Peace Prize for the operation.
- **Ross Sea protection:** Putin held up the world's largest marine protected area for 10 years; Kerry's conversations unlocked it
- **Ebola response:** Kerry secured Chinese equipment and support for West African response
- **Iran deal:** Russia (Lavrov) was in the room and helped close the agreement

**On China Specifically:** Kerry argued for "very serious negotiations" about cooperation rules, market access, and addressing dumping. He emphasized it's "absurd" to think containment will work and that serious dialogue on AI guardrails and other issues is essential.

### The Market-Driven Energy Transition

**Kerry's Core Narrative Shift:** "The days of worrying about the COP are over. We have transitioned to a whole new period."

**The Evidence:**

**The Reframing:** Kerry argued that ESG has been "weaponized" and climate "doomsday" narratives have been "discredited," but that **CEOs worldwide are transitioning regardless** based on market logic.

**The Only Question:** "Will this happen fast enough to avoid the worst consequences?"

## 3. Frameworks & Mental Models

### The "Don't Trust, Verify" Principle
Kerry inverted Reagan's "trust but verify" to **"don't trust and verify"** for the Iran negotiations. This framework emphasizes:
- Comprehensive verification mechanisms over good faith
- Multiple redundant monitoring systems
- Automatic consequences (snap-back sanctions)
- Application: In any high-stakes negotiation, especially with adversaries, build verification into the structure rather than relying on trust

### Relationship-Based Diplomacy
Kerry repeatedly emphasized that **"relationships are really critical to good diplomacy"**:
- You can't "blow in and blow out and not know people"
- Mutual respect is essential even with violent disagreement
- Personal connections unlock possibilities (Sultan Qaboos, Lavrov, President Xi)
- Application: Invest time in relationship-building before crises emerge; maintain channels even with adversaries

### Strategic Scope Limitation
In the Iran deal, Kerry deliberately **excluded missiles and regional proxies**:
- Including everything would have meant negotiating "forever"
- Nuclear breakout time was the unacceptable risk
- Better to solve one critical problem than fail trying to solve everything
- Application: In complex negotiations, identify the core risk and focus there, even if it means leaving other issues unresolved

### Pre-Cooking Success
The Paris approach of **bilateral pre-negotiation with China** before the multilateral forum:
- Prevents public failure
- Creates momentum and peer pressure
- Demonstrates feasibility to skeptics
- Application: In multilateral settings, secure key stakeholders privately before going public

## 4. Contrarian Takes

Kerry argued strongly against the conventional wisdom that some adversaries are beyond negotiation, citing his Vietnam War protest position: **"The duty of a president is to make sure you exhaust every possibility before you ask young people to go fight and die somewhere."** He explicitly criticized Trump's Iran bombing as failing this test.

### The COP Process Is No Longer Central
Kerry's claim that **"the days of worrying about the COP are over"** challenges the climate community's focus on UN negotiations. He argues the market has taken over as the primary driver, with COPs now secondary to corporate and investor decisions.

### China Engagement Is Essential, Not Optional
Against the Washington consensus on China containment, Kerry insisted: **"It is absurd to sit around and think you're going to play the politics of containment."** He argued for serious dialogue on AI, climate, and other global challenges despite authoritarian governance concerns.

### ESG Narrative Has Failed
Kerry acknowledged that ESG has been "weaponized" and that "doomsday prophets of climate crisis" have had their "narrative discredited." This represents a significant rhetorical retreat from climate advocacy's traditional framing, pivoting instead to market inevitability.

### Sultan Al Jaber Was the Right Choice for COP28
While activists criticized the appointment of an oil company CEO to lead climate talks, Kerry defended it: **"If you don't talk to other people and you don't bring them to the table, we're never going to get where we need to go."** The result—getting 51-52 oil and gas companies to commit to transitioning away from fossil fuels—validated the approach.
